Each of the ten playable races names its children differently. Below: the convention for each, then a table of given names and (where the race uses them) surnames or bynames. Roll a d20 for the given name and another for the second column, and combine them by the convention. None of the given names belong to anyone famous (a surname or byname may honor a hero, a clan, or a great House) — reuse and remix freely.

> **Bynames over surnames.** Across Skyrim, an *earned epithet* often does more work than a family name: Crag-Walker, Half-Hand, the Quiet, Two-Winters. Hang one on any NPC you want the table to remember.

### Nord

Nords name children by **omen and ceremony**, with a hard, plain, Atmoran-rooted sound. Most carry a **family name** or, more memorably, a compound **byname** describing a deed, a trait, or a clan. The great clans — Battle-Born, Gray-Mane, Shatter-Shield, Black-Briar, Snow-Shod, Stormcloak — are Skyrim's political families; save those names for their own people. The Skaal of Solstheim name their children the same way.

### Redguard

Redguard names are **unisex** and built from a given name plus an optional surname carrying a **derivation prefix**:

- **af-** — "related to" (a person)
- **al-** — "comes from" (a place)
- **at-** — "direct descendant of"
- **Ibn** — "son or descendant of"

So a warrior from the city of Sentinel might be *Tarek al-Sentinel*, and a sword-singer's descendant *Salima at-Frandar*. Many Redguards use the given name alone.

### Altmer (High Elf)

The High Elves of Summerset bear **long, melodic, heritage-laden names**. A single flowing given name is usual; the proud add a lineage — a family name, or simply the city of Summerset their line calls home. Procedural family names join a lofty prefix (*Ancar-, Caelin-, Cael-, Sil-, Aran-*) to a suffix (*-ion, -ius, -or, -dor, -wen, -dil, -ril, -aril, -mar, -mir*).

### Khajiit

The cat-folk of Elsweyr take **no family name**. A Khajiit name is fronted by an **honorific prefix** marking status or life-stage, and it changes as the bearer does:

- **dar-** — clever, nimble (a thief's prefix)
- **do-** — a warrior
- **dro-** — an elder, the respected old
- **ja-/ji-/j'** — a young adult
- **jo-** — a scholar or mage
- **la-** — a young woman
- **ma-/m'** — a child or apprentice
- **ra-** — a leader
- **ri-** — a chieftain, or one of great standing
- **s'** — an adult

Many also carry a colorful **byname**. Apostrophes mark the join: *Ri'zadha, Do'rasha, J'khari the Tale-Spinner.*
