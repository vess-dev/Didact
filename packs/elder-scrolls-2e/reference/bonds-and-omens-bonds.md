A Bond ties a character to something beyond themselves: another player character, an NPC, a faction, a place, or a mystery left unsolved. It might be a debt to the Companions, a soul gem holding someone the character loved, a sealed dispatch they cannot read, or a Daedric Prince's mark left in a dream.

A character resolves a Bond by uncovering the truth behind its mystery or rooting out its cause — freeing the trapped soul, delivering the dispatch, paying the debt. When they do, they should experience **Growth** of some kind (see the **Growth** chapter), and the Warden may, if they like, hang a new Bond on the now-empty hook. A character is never *required* to resolve a Bond; some are carried for a lifetime, and that is fine.

### Bonds Should...

- Give a character an item (often _petty_) *or* a single unique ability — never a pile of gear.
- Pull the character into the implied setting: the war, the Guild, the courts of the dead, the reach of the Thalmor.
- Flesh out a character's history *without* leaning on events already tied to a specific Background.
- Hand the character a quest, an opportunity, or a clear plan of action to pursue.

> **Worked example.** *You owe a blood-debt to the Companions of Jorrvaskr. Take a shield-brother's iron ring (petty); any Companion will know you by it, for good or ill.* This is a single petty item; it reaches straight into a named faction; it says nothing about the character's profession (any Background can carry it); and it hands the player an obvious thread to pull — find the Companions, settle the debt. That is the whole anatomy of a Bond in one line.
