These rules exist to make unusual PCs *distinct*, not to punish the player who picks one. A vampire, a werewolf, and an ordinary Nord should all have the same moment-to-moment agency at the table — they simply carry different edges and different costs. The Warden's job is to put each PC's edge **and** its cost into play roughly as often. A vampire who is never caught in daylight is just a strong mortal; a draugr who can never find a barrow to mend in is just a corpse. Use these as starting texture, and let the fiction turn the dial. If a rule here is making a player miserable without producing an interesting story, set it aside.

A few conventions used throughout:

- **Transformations** (Beast Form, the Vampire Lord) are limited to **once per day**, and each carries a cost — a Fatigue, a hunger, or both. While transformed you are **Transformed** (a status): you use that form's attacks and lose your gear and spellcasting. A form lasts one scene; its special powers work **once per transformation**.
- A transformed beast's natural weapons use the two-weapons rule — two limbs written as, e.g., `d6+d6` (roll both, keep the higher). A bite is a single die, used *instead of* the claws, not as well.
- "Mortal" below means the living races; it excludes vampires, the beast-blooded, Daedra, and the undead.
