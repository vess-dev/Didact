Brynjar, Dralsi, and Eirik have climbed down into the upper hall of **Korvundr's Rest**, an ancient Nord barrow said to hold the burial drum of a forgotten dragon-cult thane. Each carries a torch, but only **Brynjar** has struck his alight; underground there is no other light to be had. The Warden opens on the first chamber.

### The Antechamber

A long burial hall of fitted stone. The **floor** is strewn with grit and dust. A row of stone **burial alcoves** lines the left wall. A carved **word wall** dominates the far end, its runes glowing faintly. A barred **iron gate** blocks the passage east; an open archway leads down and west into the dark.

- **Floor**: Dry and undisturbed near the door, but scuffed in a track leading toward the alcoves — something has dragged itself across it more than once.
- **Burial alcoves**: Six niches, most holding desiccated, seated corpses wrapped in old mail. One alcove, third from the left, is empty, its dust recently broken.
- **Word wall**: A wall of ancient Nordic carving, one glyph lit with a low blue glow. It is set-dressing — a relic of the old tongue, humming faintly, but inert to anyone without the gift to read it. Studying it reveals nothing mechanical; it simply *is*, a marker of how old this place is.
- **Iron gate**: A heavy portcullis, lowered and locked. Beyond it, the Warden has noted, lies the burial chamber and a door sealed by a dragon-claw mechanism. Through the bars the party can make out a dais and a clawed, three-ring keyhole.

> **Warden:** "You come off the stair into a long stone hall. Brynjar's torch throws light maybe forty feet; past that it's just shapes. The far wall is carved over with old Nordic runes, one of them glowing a dull blue. Down the left side are six burial alcoves with seated corpses in rotted mail — five of them, anyway; the third is empty and the dust in front of it has been freshly broken. East, an iron portcullis is barred shut, and through it you can see a raised platform with some kind of clawed lock. An open archway leads down and west. What do you do?"

> **The players declare their intended actions for this cycle.**

> **Brynjar:** "I'll get a proper look at those alcoves — especially the empty one and that dragged track in the dust."
> **Dralsi:** "I want to study the gate and that clawed lock through the bars, see if I can work out how it opens."
> **Eirik:** "I'll examine the word wall. It's glowing; I want to know if it's dangerous."

The Warden resolves each in turn, narrating them as simultaneous.

> **Warden:** "Brynjar first. The dragged track runs from the empty alcove out toward the middle of the hall and back. The five filled niches each hold a wrapped corpse, sword across its lap, very still — but the empty one's wrappings are torn, not rotted, like something climbed out of it recently. What do you do?"

> **Brynjar:** "Right. I draw my axe, keep it ready, and call quietly to the others that one of these is missing and probably walking. I don't touch the bodies."

> **Warden:** "Wise. The seated corpses don't stir while you watch them. That's your action. Now — Dralsi."

> **Warden:** "Dralsi, the portcullis is solid iron, barred from this side oddly enough, but rusted into its housing. Through the bars you study the dais beyond: it's a stone pillar topped with three stacked rings, each carved with animal shapes, and a clawed slot above them. It's a dragon-claw lock — the kind that needs a specific carved claw turned in the right sequence to open the inner door. What do you do?"

> **Dralsi:** "Is the gate itself the lock, or is the claw mechanism only on the inner door past it?"

> **Warden:** "Only the inner door, past the gate. The portcullis is a separate problem — it's barred and rusted. You'd need to lift it or find a winch."

> **Dralsi:** "Then I look for the winch or a lever on this side, near the gate."

> **Warden:** "There's a rusted crank set into the right-hand wall by the gate. It'll take real effort, or oil and time. Noisy either way. That's your action — note it for later. Now let's see what Eirik turns up at the word wall."

> **Eirik:** "Remember, I just want to know if it'll hurt me. I'm not touching it."

> **Warden:** "You step close. The runes are old Nord, carved a finger deep, and one is lit that cold blue. It hums, low, in your chest. Nothing reaches for you; nothing changes. As far as you can tell it's simply ancient — a monument, not a machine. You don't have the learning to read it. Do you press on or move along?"

> **Eirik:** "I leave it. Anything that old makes me nervous. I'd rather help with that crank — could the two of us lift the gate faster?"

> **Warden:** "You could. But cranking a rusted iron portcullis in a tomb full of the seated dead is going to be loud, and loud has a cost down here. Together you'd raise it in one go instead of straining over it — but I'll be rolling on the events table the moment that gate shrieks up. Worth it?"

> **Dralsi:** "The thing that climbed out of that alcove already knows we're here, probably. Let's just open it. Together, fast."

> **Warden:** "All right. You two throw your weight on the crank. Let's see how the barrow answers..."

> **The Warden rolls on the Dungeon Events table: Encounter.**

> **Warden:** "The crank gives with a screech of rust and the portcullis grinds upward — and from the dark of the western archway, the missing alcove's tenant answers. A draugr lurches into the torchlight, dry skin stretched over old bone, a notched sword in its fist, and it lets out a rattling, wordless cry. The gate locks open with a clang. That's the cycle. Everyone, save DEX to act before it does — and Eirik, you've got a draugr coming up the west passage and a barred gate now standing open behind you. What do you all do?"

> **Eirik:** "Of course it's a draugr. I drop the crank and get my shield up."

> **Brynjar:** "I move to put myself between it and the others — axe ready."

> **Dralsi:** "I'll keep one eye on those other five alcoves. If one woke, the rest might. And I want that claw lock — but later. Survive first."

> **The draugr closes, and the delve continues into combat.**

Notice what the cycle did and did not cost. The party paid time and a single events roll for the privilege of opening the gate fast; the word wall, for all its menace, gave them nothing but atmosphere and the sense of the barrow's age; and the dragon-claw door beyond is still sealed, a promise they'll have to come back to once the dead are dealt with. None of it was hidden from them. Each cost was named before it was paid.
