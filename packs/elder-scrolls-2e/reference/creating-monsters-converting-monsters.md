There is no perfect way to convert creatures from other systems. These tips should help.

- **Hit Protection**: A good rule of thumb is +1 HP per **Hit Die**, on top of a minimum **3 HP** (an average person). Treat a Hit Die as roughly a d6 (mean 3.5). Always set a creature's **HP** *first*, then its **STR**.
- **Armor**: Watch for phrases like "as leather" or "as plate." Converting from B/X-style games (such as Old School Essentials), ignore THAC0 and use descending AC where you can (7 = Leather, 5 = Chainmail, 3 = Plate). If only ascending AC is given, read it as (12 = Leather, 14 = Chainmail, 16 = Plate). Whatever the source, Armor lands in the **1–3** range only.
- **Strength**: Look at Hit Dice and HP. If a creature is hard to kill but *not* because it is nimble or skilled, give it more **STR** (not more HP). Start at 10 and adjust.
- **Dexterity**: Start at **10**. Raise it for the quick, agile, or nimble-fingered (often noted as "speed"); lower it for the slow, bulky, or clumsy. See Saving Throws below.
- **Willpower**: **Morale** is a useful guide, typically 2–12. The greater a creature's personality, spirit, or presence, the higher its WIL. The table below converts Morale to WIL.

|         |   |    |    |    |
|---------|---|----|----|----|
| **ML**  | 4 | 8  | 10 | 12 |
| **WIL** | 6 | 12 | 15 | 18 |

### Saving Throws

- A source's *saving throws* tell you a lot about a creature — especially how well it shrugs off physical trauma, magic, or poison.
- Stat blocks often use shorthand like (E1) or (F1) — a Level 1 Elf or Fighter. These quickly hint at a creature's capabilities.
- Consider adding descriptive tags such as "immune to poison" or "hard to pin down" based on the saves. The table below shows which saves should bump which ability score.

| Death or Poison | STR |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| **Wands**                      | **DEX**            |
| **Paralysis or Petrification** | **WIL** or **STR** |
| **Breath Attacks**             | **DEX** or **STR** |
| **Spells, Rods or Staves**     | **WIL**            |

### Combat

- **Damage Die**: Usually copies over "as is" from most OSR systems, but check it against the weapons in the **Marketplace** first.
- **Multiple attacks**: Convert to _blast_ or to multiple dice (e.g. d6+d6).
- **Devastating attacks**: Make the damage _Enhanced_, add the _blast_ tag, or give the attack multiple dice.

### Abilities

- Sometimes the source's mechanics simply *do not translate*. That is fine. Focus on what is most interesting about the creature and write a "version" of its ability that fits this game.
- An ability can often become an attack with a damage die, letting Critical Damage reveal its true horror.
- If a source's attack asks the PCs to dodge or save against some ability, consider making it an out-of-combat "trap" they can trigger; otherwise have it succeed automatically. Combat is dangerous, and it falls to the Warden to telegraph that danger before blades are drawn.

### Magic

- Magical creatures can "know" any number of spells without carrying Spell Tomes. When you do this, make their corpses magical (and dangerous) — a slain conjurer's body still crackling with bound Daedra.
- Spells are tricky. You may hand a magic-user one or more Spell Tomes, but remember they might *drop* them when they fall.
- Consider letting some dropped Spell Tomes implode when their wielder dies — use the Die of Fate to decide which.

### Use the Fiction

The easiest way to convert a creature is to read its original stat block and any flavor text, write a few plain sentences about it, then turn those sentences into a Cairn-style stat block.

Take this creature, from an older bestiary:

#### Wer-fox

Can take the form of a fox, or of a seven-foot fox-headed woman.
_HD 5, Speed 120', Armor 14, Morale 11, Attack: +4 d8 (claw, bite, or choke)_
- Defense: Cannot be harmed by metal.
- Special: Shifts at will between a fox and a maiden with one fox leg hidden (same stats).

_**From this, I can see that she:**_
- Appears as a seven-foot woman with the head of a fox.
- Does not have especially high HP, and is quite fast.
- Has high Morale, and is plainly willful.
- Is agile and lithe.
- Attacks with teeth and claws, choking her prey when she can.
- Can become a fox at will.
- Is immune to metal weapons.

_**What does that tell us?**_
- She is fast and likely fight-savvy. Starting at **3 HP** and adding 1 per Hit Die gives **8 HP**.
- Foxes have no protective hide, and she is otherwise human below the neck. **No Armor.**
- She is decently strong, a touch above a human. **12 STR.**
- I picture her running prey down across the snowfields north of Dawnstar. She is fast. **14 DEX.**
- Her Morale is high and foxes are cunning. **15 WIL.**
- For attacks I would use claws (d6+d6) and bite (d8).

_**That leaves us with:**_
8 HP, 12 STR, 14 DEX, 15 WIL, claws (d6+d6), bite (d8)

_**Now her abilities** — just read back the plain description we wrote:_
- Appears as a seven-foot woman with the head of a fox.
- Shifts into a fox at will.
- Is immune to metal weapons (cold iron and steel pass through her).
- Chokes her victims into unconsciousness.

_**The final result:**_

#### Wer-fox

8 HP, 12 STR, 14 DEX, 15 WIL, claws (d6+d6), bite (d8)

- Takes the form of a fox, or of a woman with one fox leg hidden.
- Immune to attacks from metal weapons — only spell, tooth, or wood will bite her.
- **Critical Damage**: The victim is choked unconscious.
