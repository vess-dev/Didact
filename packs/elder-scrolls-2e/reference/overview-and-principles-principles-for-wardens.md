### Information

- Describe the world plainly and generously as the characters move through it.
- Players never need a roll to learn what is plainly around them.
- Answer their questions helpfully and straight.
- Be honest, keep your descriptions consistent, and always remind them they may keep asking.

### Difficulty

- Default to the logic of the situation, not to numbers and mechanics.
- If a thing the players want is sincerely impossible, no roll will grant it.
- When what a player describes makes sense and uses the situation well, let it happen.
- Saves answer a great many uncertain moments, and are often all a risky action needs.

### Preparation

- The world is a living, shifting, unpredictable place. It improvises and turns sharply.
- Use your tables and generators to spin up *situations*, not scripted stories or plots.
- The folk of Skyrim remember what the party says and does, and how they leave their mark.
- No one wants to die — not the bandit chief, not the conjurer. Give each their own self-interest and will to live.

### Narrative Focus

- What matters is the story that emerges at the table, not arithmetic or a character's abilities. Offer weapon trainers, College tutors, and personal quests so characters can improve and specialize.
- Watch what the players want and need, then set believable chances for it in their way.
- A dagger at the throat kills all the same, no matter how fine the Daedric plate or how long the training.

### Danger

- The world holds genuine peril of pain and death for the characters.
- Telegraph real danger when it looms — the deadlier the threat, the plainer the warning.
- Set traps in plain sight and let the players take their time working out an answer.
- Give them room to solve problems and to reach into the world.

### Treasure

- A treasure belongs to the place it was drawn from; it carries a story. A jeweled Akaviri blade pried from a barrow lord's grip means more than a sack of loose coin.
- Treasure is richly valuable, almost always _bulky_, and rarely good for much beyond its worth and the prestige it brings.
- Relics are not treasure — though they are useful and full of wonder.
- Dangle treasure as a lure toward strange places guarded by fearsome foes.

### Choice

- When a scene drifts, set a firm choice before the players to force the matter.
- Use a plain "so — A, or B?" when their intentions are murky.
- Work it out together in this back-and-forth to keep the game in motion.
- See to it that the characters' deeds leave their mark on Skyrim.

### Die of Fate

- Now and then you will want a touch of chance — the weather, a passing encounter, whether a Dwemer cog still sits in some trader's chest.
- In such moments, roll 1d6. A 4 or more generally favors the players.
- A 3 or less tends to mean ill luck for the party or those they hold dear.
