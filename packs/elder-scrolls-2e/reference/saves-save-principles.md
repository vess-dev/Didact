- When uncertain, ask whether the party has the time, tools, skill, or upper hand to beat the problem outright. If the answer is "no," a save is usually called for. If the answer is "I'm not sure," reach for the **Die of Fate**.
- It is fine to say "no" — but always hand the party another door to try. If they're blocked by something outside their control, give them an alternative.
- A death should never blindside a player. If a choice could get someone killed, make sure they know the stakes before they commit.
- Keep meaningful options on the table. The more real choices a scene offers, the more interesting it becomes.
- A save's result needn't be flat pass-or-fail. Weigh the whole picture — the planning, effort, resources, and skill poured in — and shade the outcome accordingly.
- Be open with your players. Not every secret of the world, but everything their characters could plausibly know.
- Skip the dice when you can. If a PC can solve a problem with fiction alone, _do not call for a roll_.
- Fold several rolls into one. A single throw raises the tension and keeps the players' choices front and center.
