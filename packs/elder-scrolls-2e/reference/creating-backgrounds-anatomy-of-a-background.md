### Theme

Start by jotting down the common adjectives that describe your new Background. If you are designing a profession, ask what would lead a person into that life — what drives someone to take the Stormcloak oath, or to spend their days alone in a frozen mine. If you are writing something stranger (a Briarheart, say, or a Dremora bound to Mundus), think about how that strangeness sets the character apart from others — or draws certain folk closer. As you write the gear and tables, keep one eye on Skyrim's implied setting, your inspirations, and the tropes the archetype carries, and let them steer the results.

### Starting Gear

Characters here are defined largely by their inventory, so starting gear is the first place to paint a Background's canvas. Each Background gets:

- **Septims, food, and a light source.** Make even these specific: 3d6 Septims (the Cairn standard), Rations measured in uses, and a thematic light — a Nord's pitch-pine torch, a College apprentice's candlelit lantern, a hooded thief's shuttered lamp.
- **Up to four _unique_ items** that reinforce the theme. At least one must be a weapon of some kind, using the ordinary weapon dice from the Marketplace. Armor, if any, should come to 1 or 2 in total.
- **Seven slots, total, at most.** Starting items should occupy no more than seven inventory slots, so a character is not overburdened from the first step out the door. (Recall: _petty_ items take no slots, _bulky_ items take two, and a purse under 100 Septims is _petty_.)

> **Currency and balance.** All prices and purses are in **Septims** (the same numbers as Cairn gold). Keep gear within Cairn limits: weapons d4–d12, Armor never above 3. A finer material — glass over steel, ebony over iron — is *flavor and value*, not a bigger number. Only a **masterwork** piece (one already tempered, as from the Skyforge or an Orc stronghold's forge) steps up a die or gains +1 Armor, and it can never be tempered again.

### Tables

The tables are where a character's particular story comes together, and where the diversity lives that keeps two characters of one Background from being twins. Each Background carries a **defining question** followed by **six rows** of answers.

- **The first table** asks about the character's *distant or recent past* — the experiences that brought them to where they now stand. Its results should offer a unique item, a unique ability, or a combination of the two. This is the origin twist: the dragon's scale found in the ashes, the oath sworn and never broken, the brand of a Morrowind slaver.
- **The second table** asks who the character is *today*. Its results are usually a specific item (often with a name of its own) or a skill that broadly defines them — the named axe, the trained falcon, the trick with locks no one taught them.

Write all six rows of each table to be distinct and interesting in their own right. A Background whose every row is "a different sword" wastes the form; vary item, ability, relationship, and complication across the rows.

### Power Budget

Backgrounds in Cairn are flavorful first and mechanical second. Keep every ability to one of these sizes, and write frequencies with exactly these words:

- **Always on, but narrative** — a sense, a piece of knowledge, a contact, a standing, a debt. *"You can always tell a forged coin." "Any Khajiit caravan will shelter you."* Use freely.
- **Always on, but narrow** — *"you need not save to X"*, where X is a small, specific thing (climbing rigging, crossing thin ice, calming a horse); one element's attacks _Impaired_; unarmed d6; +1 maximum HP.
- **Once per day** — the default for anything that bends a roll: re-roll a failed save of a narrow kind, make one attack _Enhanced_, channel one Spell Tome without Fatigue, a small magic, a 1d4–1d6 heal. **Once per combat** is for narrow fighting tricks only (one weapon, one circumstance).
- **Strong** — re-rolling *any* save, more than one free casting a day, an extra action, a summoned ally: once per day *and* with a price (a Fatigue, a debt, a Daedric string), or as an item with 1–3 charges and a **Recharge**.
- **Once per session** is only for asking the Warden a question or calling in a favor; **once per campaign** for a single great boon.
- **Never:** numbers added to saves, always-on combat bonuses ("your attacks are _Enhanced_ whenever…"), Armor above 3, damage above d12, or a Spell Tome above Adept. A caster Background gives **one** Spell Tome; Scrolls and charged items can give a second taste of magic.

> **A worked sketch — the Skaal Hunter.**
> *Theme:* weathered, reverent, self-reliant; a hunter of the All-Maker's people on snowbound Solstheim, far from home and watching the Stones fall silent. Adjectives: hardy, quiet, devout.
> *Starting gear:* 3d6 Septims; Rations (3 uses); a horker-fat lamp and its oil; a hunting bow (d6, _bulky_); a bone hatchet (d6); a Skaal coat of fur and hide (1 Armor, counts as warm clothing). Two weapons, one coat, and the staples: seven slots, the limit.
> *First table — "What did the All-Maker grant you?"* The rows mix a narrative gift (the shaman read your birth at the Wind Stone and named you a tracker), a narrow always-on knack (on snow, ice, or shore, the Supply action never comes back empty), a companion (an ice wolf that chose you), a piece of lore (how the Skaal work Stalhrim), a consumable (frost-fletched arrows, 3 uses), and a hook with an edge (you woke sleepwalking at the All-Maker's stone and broke free when the others did not).
> *Second table — "What does the village know you for?"* Each row is a named item or a trained knack: a Stalhrim-tipped spear, a bear's-tooth token blessed at the Beast Stone (once per day, a hunting cry that sends a foe fleeing), boots that cross thin ice, the shaman's ward against the Adversary.
>
> See the finished version in Skaal Hunter.

Build outward from the theme, keep the gear inside the slot and Septim limits, and make every row earn its place.
