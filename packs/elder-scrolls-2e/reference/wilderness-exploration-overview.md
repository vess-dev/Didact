Travel in The Elder Scrolls 2e is deliberately broad-strokes. You do not track every mile of tundra or count footsteps up a pass. Instead the journey is divided into watches, and each watch the party chooses one meaningful action, the dice decide whether they stray off course, and you roll once on the events table to see what the road throws at them. The dull stretches blur past; the interesting moments — a frozen ford, a circling sabre cat, a guttering torch in a blizzard — are the ones that get play.

The aim is to keep travel a real part of the adventure without drowning it in bookkeeping. A crossing of the Pale in winter should *feel* like a crossing of the Pale in winter: cold, slow, and never quite safe. The watch cycle and the difficulty tables exist to deliver that feeling cheaply, and the party's agency lives in the choices they make each watch — push on or make camp, take the road or cut across the ice, hunt or hurry.
