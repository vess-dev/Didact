A mount is bought from the Marketplace and adds **carry slots** to its rider, just like a horse, mule, cart, or wagon there. A loaded mount lets the party bring more out of a barrow than they could ever shoulder themselves. Slots, speed quirks, and Septim prices live in the **Marketplace** under *Transport*; this chapter covers what a mount does on the road and in a fight.

### Negating Travel Penalties

Wilderness travel is measured in **watches**, with penalties piled on by **path**, **terrain**, and **weather** (see *Procedures*). A mount is one of the surest ways to wear those penalties down.

- A party that rides — every member mounted — may **ignore one watch of penalty** drawn from **Path** or **Terrain** on a journey (the riders' choice which). The journey can never drop below the base watch cost for the distance — a horse erases hardship, not the miles themselves.
- A mount bred or born to a particular country eases that ground further: a **Skyrim horse** treats snow, ice, and mountain (**Perilous**) terrain as one step easier, on top of the above.
- **Weather** penalties and effects still bite. A blizzard chills horse and rider alike; the cold, the lightning, and the drowning rules apply to a mounted party exactly as to one on foot.
- A **pack mule** or a hitched **cart/wagon** is *slow* and grants no travel relief — it is pure cargo. Hitching a cart to a riding horse cancels that horse's penalty-easing while it pulls.

### Feed, Camp, and Stabling

- On the road a mount eats like a person: when the party takes the **Make Camp** action, **each mount consumes a Ration** (or a use of *Animal Feed*) along with the riders. A mount left unfed becomes Deprived and will, in time, sicken or bolt.
- In a settlement, a mount is left at a stable. **Stabling and feed cost 5 Septims per night** (see the Marketplace's *Upkeep & Recovery*). A stabled mount is safe, fed, and waiting when you return.

### Leaving a Mount Behind

A horse cannot follow into a Nordic crypt, a Dwemer stairwell, or a Falmer warren. When the party dives underground or into close country, the mount is left at the threshold.

- A mount left **at a stable or with a trusted keeper** is simply there later.
- A mount left **tied in the wild** is at the mercy of the fiction. When the party returns, the Warden may roll the **Die of Fate**: on a 3 or under it has wandered, been stolen, been eaten by a sabre cat, or run off from a dragon's shadow. A loyal or exceptional steed (below) is far more forgiving.
- A mount's carry slots — and whatever was stowed in them — stay with the mount. Take what you need before you walk away from it.
