Companions are not bought. A PC earns one by:

- **Becoming Thane.** Do a Hold a great service and win its Jarl's favor, and you are named Thane — and granted a **housecarl**, sworn to guard you and your hall. A housecarl starts at **Loyalty 4** (the oath is already given) and will not leave without your word.
- **Hiring on, then proving worth.** A sellsword taken on for coin (see below) becomes a true companion only once the party has bled beside them and earned it — at which point Loyalty begins to matter.
- **Standing with a guild.** Earn an ally's trust within the Companions, the College, the Thieves Guild, or the like, and they may ride out with you. (A guild tie sets the **Faction Reputation** for recruiting them; see the Warden's Guide.)
- **Saving a life that can't be repaid**, fulfilling a years-old request, offering something concrete they want — a cure, a home, a name cleared — or finding them at a crossroads and making a promise the Warden believes you'll keep.
- **Taming or raising one.** A beast won over (see below), or a servant conjured or called from the grave.

### Hirelings-for-hire (mercenaries)

Sellswords wait in the inns and mead halls of every Hold — Whiterun's Bannered Mare, Riften's Bee and Barb, the warrens of Markarth. A mercenary is **hired** by the day, at Marketplace rates, and is a **hireling** until earned: paid, no Loyalty, takes Morale saves. Fight long enough beside one, treat them right, and the Warden may rule they have become a companion — give them a **special trick** and start their Loyalty at **3**.

### Conjured & raised servants

A Conjuration spell or a necromancer's rite can put an ally at your side, but the dead and the summoned are **not** companions — they have no Loyalty and take no Command action:

- A **conjured Daedra or bound weapon** (from a Spell Tome) lasts only the scene, then unravels back to Oblivion. Use the spell's own short stat line — a summoned servant is a lesser thing than the same creature met in the wild (see the Spell Tomes chapter); it obeys its summoner without question while it lasts.
- A **raised corpse** (a reanimated draugr, skeleton, or fresh dead) serves until it is destroyed or the magic lapses, then falls. It mindlessly follows simple orders and rots; it cannot be healed by Restoration meant for the living. The Vigil of Stendarr and most decent folk react one step worse to anyone who walks with the risen.
