At a **forge** (with its smelter and tanning rack), a PC creates or improves weapons and armor. Smithing is the trade of ingots and hides: ore is smelted to ingots, skins are cured to leather, and from these a smith hammers out a blade or a cuirass — or reworks one already made into something keener.

### Forging gear

Making a piece of gear from raw stock is one **Downtime Action** at a forge, plus the appropriate ingots or leather (bought, mined, or looted) and a few Septims for fuel and the use of the fire. A freshly forged weapon or suit of armor enters play at its ordinary statline for its type (see the Marketplace): an iron sword is a d8, a steel mail cuirass 2 Armor, a suit of plate 3 Armor. The **material tier** sets the fiction — its weight, beauty, dread, and worth — and which material you need to temper it.

### Tempering (improving gear)

**Tempering** is the heart of smithing in play. One **Downtime Action** at a forge, plus a unit of the right **material** for the piece, **tempers** it — once, for good:

- A **weapon** steps its damage die up one rung: d6 → d8, d8 → d10, or d10 → **d12** (the cap).
- A suit of **body armor** gains **+1 Armor**: light armor becomes 2 Armor, mail becomes 3. Plate is already at the cap, and a shield or helmet never gives more than +1.

A piece can be tempered **only once**; after that, more work at the forge only deepens its fiction (its edge, its polish, its resale). Tempering needs material **of the piece's own make or finer**; you cannot true a Daedric blade with a bar of iron. A unit of that material — an ingot, a hide, a bar of stock — costs about what the piece itself would in the Marketplace, multiplied for the finer makes as below. **Masterwork** gear — from the Skyforge, an Orc stronghold's forge, or a smith of Eorlund Gray-Mane's skill — comes already tempered: it has used its one step.

A rushed or improvised job — working without a proper forge, hurrying between watches, attempting a tier beyond your skill — calls for a **STR save**. On a failure the material is wasted (and the Warden may rule the piece nicked or dulled until reworked); the item itself survives, untempered.

### Material tiers

The smith's materials, weakest to strongest. Every tier obeys the same caps — Armor **≤ 3**, damage **≤ d12** — so the difference between iron and Daedric is told in the fiction: lighter, finer, costlier, more dreadful, and (for the best of them) more readily enchanted. The right place on this ladder is mostly a question of what you can find and afford. As a rule of thumb, Dwarven, Elven, and Orcish work is worth about five times the Marketplace price; glass, ebony, Nordic, and Stalhrim about twenty times; dragonbone and Daedric fifty times or more — when they can be bought at all.

| Tier | What it means in the fiction |
| --- | --- |
| **Iron** | The common stock of every village forge. Heavy, plain, serviceable. Iron ingots are cheap and everywhere; this is where a smith learns the hammer. |
| **Steel** | Iron folded with corundum — tougher, holds an edge, the soldier's standard across the Holds. Lighter and finer than raw iron for the same protection. |
| **Dwarven** | Forged from salvaged Dwemer metal (it cannot be mined — only smelted from ruin-salvage). Golden, ancient, and far sturdier than steel; a mark of the delver who looted a Dwemer hall. |
| **Elven** | Pale moonstone work, quicksilver-bright. Light and elegant — the Altmer aesthetic — favoring the swift fighter over the bulwark. |
| **Orcish** | Dark, brutal stronghold-craft, heavier and grimmer than elven. What the Orsimer beat out at their clan forges; ugly, dependable, and feared. |
| **Glass** | Malachite, not true glass: a translucent green volcanic stone, prized and rare. Astonishingly light for its strength — beautiful, expensive, and a clear sign of wealth. |
| **Ebony** | Hardened volcanic "stone-metal" from the ash lands, black and faintly glassy. One of the finest mundane materials; weapons of it are deadly, armor of it is dreaded, and it takes an enchantment readily. |
| **Nordic / Stalhrim** | Two ancient northern makes. **Nordic** is the strong-but-light steel of old Solstheim. **Stalhrim** is "enchanted ice" mined only on Solstheim with an ancient Nordic pick — frost-cold to the touch and rivaling the very best; gear of it carries cold enchantments uncommonly well. |
| **Dragonbone** | Smith-work from the bones and scales of slain dragons. Wickedly sharp, frighteningly tough, and unmistakable; only a Dragonborn's hunts (or a very lucky scavenger) supply the stock. |
| **Daedric** | The pinnacle. Ebony quenched with a beating **Daedra Heart** in an ill-omened rite — black shot through with red, radiating a low menace. The dreadful equal of dragonbone; armor of it terrifies, and it drinks enchantment more eagerly than any other make. Still 3 Armor; still d12. Its superiority is the fear it sows, the price it commands, and the magic it will hold. |

> **The cap is the cap.** "Daedric plate" is still 3 Armor and a Daedric greatsword is still d12. Spend the steps where you like — but a finer material buys you a lighter, lovelier, more enchantable piece and a tale to tell, not a number the rules forbid.
