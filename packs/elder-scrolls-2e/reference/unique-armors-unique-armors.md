Named, one-of-a-kind armors, shields, masks, and wearables of Tamriel — the Daedric artifacts handed out by the Princes, the masks of the Dragon Priests, and the heirloom panoply of dead heroes and guild masters. Unlike ordinary marketplace armor, each has a name, a history, and a reason its last wearer is remembered. There should be only one of each in your campaign — save the issued garb of an order (the Nightingale and Shrouded armors), which the campaign frames hand to each who takes the oath.

Place them deliberately: on the altar of a Daedric shrine at the end of a terrible bargain, behind the puzzle-locked door of a Nordic barrow, sealed in a Dwemer vault, or worn by the legend the party came to kill. When someone walks out of a tomb wearing a Dragon Priest's mask, the whole Hold hears of it within a season.

**On armor "sets" and the cap.** Skyrim's armor comes in grades and matched sets; The Elder Scrolls 2e does not. Armor's value comes from its **type**, never its make, and **Armor is capped at 3**. A unique suit of **light** armor is already as fine as light armor gets: **2 Armor**, not _bulky_. A unique **heavy** suit is plate: **3 Armor**, _bulky_. What the famous name (or the artifact's enchantment) buys is *fictional* — it is lighter, dreadful to behold, valuable, unbreakable — plus, in these entries, **one special property**. A matched "set" grants only its body piece's Armor and its property, never a stacking bonus; pair a body armor with a helmet or shield (+1 Armor each, while worn) up to the cap of 3. Rings, circlets, amulets, and robes grant **no Armor** — they are worn for what they do, not what they stop. Where a piece carries a charged power, it lists its charges and a **Recharge:**; everything else is always at work. *As ever, sometimes the flavor is the mechanic.*
