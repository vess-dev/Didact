Tamriel sorts its spells into five schools. In this game the schools are **flavor labels only**: they tell you what a spell *feels* like and who tends to teach it, and they grant no extra rules, no specialization, no separate skill. A Destruction tome and a Restoration tome are cast exactly the same way. At a glance, so a Warden can stock a study, a barrow, or a College shelf to taste:

- **Destruction** — raw harm: streams, bolts, runes, walls, and cloaks of fire, frost, and lightning. The loudest, most feared art.
- **Restoration** — mending and warding: healing the living, raising wards against spells, and turning, repelling, or burning the undead.
- **Alteration** — bending the physical: stone-hard flesh, conjured light, detection, telekinesis, paralysis, breathing water, transmuting ore.
- **Illusion** — bending the mind: calm, fear, and frenzy (singly or over a crowd), invisibility, silence, and second sight.
- **Conjuration** — calling and binding: atronachs, familiars, and Dremora summoned from Oblivion; the dead raised; weapons of bound light; soul-trapping and banishment.
- **Mysticism** — the old sixth school of souls, absorption, reflection, and dispelling, which the Psijics call the Old Ways. The College of Winterhold let it lapse in the Fourth Era and parceled its arts out among the others, but its tomes still surface in old libraries and older tombs, beside other arts of the Third Era that Skyrim's mages no longer teach.
