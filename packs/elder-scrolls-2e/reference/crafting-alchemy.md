At an **alchemy lab** (a mortar, alembic, and retort — never on the move) a PC brews **potions** and **poisons** by combining **ingredients** that share an effect. A potion is the gentler kind; a poison is its cruel twin, smeared on a blade or slipped into a cup.

### How it works

Every ingredient — a flower, a fang, a fungus, a scrap of monster — carries hidden properties. Combine **two or three** ingredients that share a property and brew them as one **Downtime Action** at a lab, and you draw that property out as a usable dose. A PC learns what an ingredient does by **eating** a pinch of it (revealing one property), by reading a recipe, or by experimenting at the lab. Knowing your ingredients is the whole art; the Warden tells you what a given pinch teaches.

Each brew is one **Downtime Action** and yields **1d4** potions or poisons of the recipe (roll once). A finished potion or poison is _petty_, **single-use**, and causes no Fatigue — it behaves exactly like a Cairn consumable. Drinking a potion is an action; coating a blade with a poison is an action, and the poison works on the **next hit** that deals damage after Armor.

This is the **Herbalism** Training example (see **Procedures → Downtime**) writ large: given the right ingredients and a lab, the work simply succeeds. A rushed or improvised batch (no proper lab, brewing under threat) calls for a **WIL save**; on a failure the Warden picks an unhappy result — the ingredients are wasted, or the dose turns to a weak **poison** instead.

> **Foraging.** Ingredients are bought from apothecaries, looted, or gathered in the wilds. A PC taking the **Supply** action by a meadow, marsh, or barrow may collect a handful of alchemical ingredients alongside (or instead of) rations, at the Warden's call.

### Sample potions & poisons

Representative recipes, each brewed from ingredients sharing the named effect. Treat the list as a menu, not a limit; the Warden may add others in the same register. All are _petty_, single-use, no Fatigue.

| Brew | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Potion of Healing** | Restores **1d6 STR**. A master's **Potion of Greater Healing** restores all lost STR. Either can stabilize a PC suffering Critical Damage. A healing draught is magical healing (see *Healing has limits*, below). |
| **Potion of Cure Disease** | Cures all mundane disease (Rockjoint, Ataxia, and the like). It does **not** cure Vampirism or Lycanthropy. |
| **Potion of Resist Fire** | For the rest of the **scene**, fire attacks against you are _Impaired_. |
| **Potion of Resist Frost** | For the rest of the **scene**, frost and cold attacks against you are _Impaired_. (A Stalhrim-flavored brew is especially prized in the north.) |
| **Potion of Resist Shock** | For the rest of the **scene**, lightning/shock attacks against you are _Impaired_. |
| **Draught of Fortitude** | Re-roll one failed **STR** (or DEX, or WIL — the brew is keyed to one) save, **once**, before the next Downtime. |
| **Potion of Invisibility** | You cannot be seen until you attack, cast, or the scene ends. Attacks you make from it are _Enhanced_; saves to move unseen are **not required** while it lasts. |
| **Potion of Waterbreathing** | You breathe water freely for a **scene** — no drowning saves while submerged. |
| **Damage Health (poison)** | Smeared on a blade or arrow as an action. The **next hit** with that weapon is _Enhanced_. |
| **Paralytic (poison)** | The **next hit** with the coated weapon forces a **STR save**; on a failure the target loses its next turn as its limbs lock. |
| **Lingering Poison** | The **next hit** with the coated weapon forces a **STR save**; on a failure the target takes **1d6 STR** and the venom *lingers*: at the start of each of its next three turns it saves again, losing 1 more STR on each failure, until a save succeeds (see **Hazards → Poison & Venom**). |

> **Healing has limits.** A healing potion is magic in a bottle. A creature's STR can be mended by magic — spell, potion, Relic, or blessing — only **once per day** (see the Spell Tomes chapter); a second draught that day steadies the nerves and restores HP, but no more STR. Bandages, a surgeon's kit, and a herbalist's poultice are craft, not magic, and are not counted.

### Skooma & Moon Sugar

**Moon Sugar** is a sweet, crystalline narcotic; refined into **Skooma**, it is a potent and illegal drug smuggled out of Elsweyr. It is not an alchemical aid but a vice. A dose grants a brief, reckless rush — then the crash: anyone who takes Skooma makes a **STR save** or is **Deprived** until they next rest safely. (There is no addiction track; the danger is the crash, the crime, and the company it keeps.)
