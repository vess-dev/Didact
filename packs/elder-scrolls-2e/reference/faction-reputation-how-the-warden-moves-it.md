- **Move one step at a time, on a witnessed or reported action.** A faction reacts to what its people saw or were told — not to the party's private intentions. Doing their dirty work, returning a stolen relic, sparing a captured member, or carrying word for them is a step up. Robbing them, killing a member, siding openly with a rival, or exposing a secret is a step down.
- **One step per beat, not one per kill.** A whole night's work for the Thieves Guild moves their standing once, not once per crate. Reputation tracks the relationship, not the receipts.
- **The deed has to land.** If no one survives to tell the tale and no evidence remains, nothing moves. Witnesses, rumor, survivors, and the faction's own informants are how word travels. A clever party can earn a faction's gratitude with one hand and rob it blind with the other, so long as the two never meet on the books.
- **Sworn buys a favor.** Once per session, a +2 faction will grant one meaningful favor — a safe house thrown open, a debt forgiven, a Justiciar patrol "delayed," a hard door unlocked, a hireling lent for free. It is a real favor with real weight; spend it like one.
- **Reputation is local to the faction, not the law.** Earning the Thieves Guild's trust does not square things with Riften's guards — that is **Bounty** (see Renown & Infamy). A faction can love you while the Hold wants you hanged.
