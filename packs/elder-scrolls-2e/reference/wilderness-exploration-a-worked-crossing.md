The party has agreed to guide a nervous Imperial courier from a tundra farmstead outside Whiterun to a Hold garrison set at the foot of a mountain valley. On the map it is a single day's walk: the whole route runs over *Easy* tundra along a clear *Trail*, so by the tables it should take one day and no more. Skyrim, of course, has opinions.

> At the top of each day the Warden rolls for the season's weather and judges how it bears on the day's travel. The roll comes up **Fair** — overcast, a steady cold wind, no penalty. After every Wilderness Action the party rolls 1d6 to see whether they get lost, and the Warden rolls once on the Wilderness Events table.

### Day One, Morning Watch

- The party takes the **Travel** action, following the trail north across open tundra. They roll for getting lost on the *Trail* — and stay on course.
- **Events:** **Sign**. Cresting a low rise, one of them looks back south and catches a smear of black smoke far off against the gray sky, there and then gone behind the hills. A signal fire? A burning farm? Something is happening on the road behind them.

### Day One, Afternoon Watch

- The party takes the **Travel** action again, holding to the same trail across the same tundra. They roll once more for getting lost — and again hold their line.
- **Events:** **Environment**. The Fair wind sharpens into the afternoon and a thin snow begins to fall, fattening as the light fails. The trail blurs under white and the footing turns soft and treacherous. The Warden adds **one watch** to the journey: the party can only slog to the edge of the foothills by nightfall, well short of the garrison in the valley beyond. They will have to shelter in the open snow rather than behind the garrison's walls.

### Day One, Night Watch

- With the garrison out of reach and snow falling, the party takes the **Make Camp** action. They pitch a hasty lean-against-the-rocks shelter, and each member (and the courier) eats a Ration. A lookout rotation is set, splitting the night between them — no small thing this close to the mountains.
- **Events:** **Encounter**. The snow thins near midnight, and the second watcher hears it before seeing it: a heavy, wet snuffling and the crunch of something large breaking the crust uphill. A **frost troll** has caught the scent of the camp and is lumbering down out of the rocks toward the firelight. The watcher must wake the others fast — and the party now faces a choice between a fight in the dark and abandoning the fire to slip away into the storm.

> The party could have driven on through the night instead of making camp — the garrison's walls were tempting. But night travel is the dangerous choice: the Warden rolls *twice* on the events table after dark, the odds of losing the trail in the snow climb, and a party that skips the night's sleep wakes Deprived and finds even Easy tundra turned a step harder. A frost troll at the camp is bad. A frost troll on the open trail, exhausted, in the dark, would be worse.
