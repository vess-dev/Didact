An Omen is the seed of an adventure not yet sprung — a premonition of disaster, or the hidden cause of trouble the characters already face. Where a Bond is personal, an Omen belongs to the world. Dragons in the skies. An aurora burning the wrong color over Winterhold. Caravans vanishing on the road to Markarth, leaving only briar-carved hearts behind.

An Omen is resolved when the characters drag its origin into the light or put down the danger it warns of. Resolving an Omen should yield **Growth** — for the party as a whole, or for whichever character it has entangled. And if an Omen is ignored, it must *come to pass*: the dead climb out of their barrows, the frost does not lift, the Black Door opens. An Omen left alone is a promise the Warden should keep.

### Omens Should...

- Never hinge on one specific player character for relevance — an Omen must matter no matter who is at the table.
- Always tell the characters something about the setting they did not already know.
- Reveal a pattern, or some other plain sign, that something terrible is underway.

> **Worked example.** *Whole caravans vanish on the road to Markarth. The only thing left behind: heart-shaped stones carved from briar.* No single character is required for this to bite — it threatens any party that travels the Reach. It teaches the table something new (briarheart rites, and the Forsworn behind them). And the repeated, escalating disappearances are a pattern impossible to ignore. Follow it to its source and the party finds a Forsworn redoubt; ignore it and the raids creep closer to the road they must take.
