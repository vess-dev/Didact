A good first session hits these beats, roughly in order. Let the players choose how long each takes.

1. **Arrival.** Drop the party into Skyrim with a clear, immediate goal tied to their shared reason for being together. A wagon cresting a snowy pass. A barrow door at the end of a climb. A cart of prisoners rolling into a doomed town. Make the cold, the danger, and the stakes obvious in the first few sentences.
2. **First save.** Call a save in the first ten minutes — a slick ledge (DEX), a frozen lock, a guard to talk past (WIL). Teach the system by using it. Make the failure interesting, not lethal.
3. **First choice that costs.** Before the first real fight, give the party a fork where both paths have a price — help the wounded straggler or stay on schedule; pay the toll or risk the road. Who these characters are will surface here.
4. **First combat.** A short fight, two or three rounds, against something the table understands — bandits, a pack of wolves, a draugr or two woken in the dark. Make sure every PC gets to act. Call **Scars** when a hit takes a PC to exactly 0 HP.
5. **First Scar.** The first time a hit takes a PC to exactly 0 HP, pause and read the Scar entry aloud together. That is the moment the game teaches itself — the world is dangerous, but a blow that would kill in another game leaves a mark and a story here.
6. **First hook for next time.** End the session with a *specific* NPC from a *specific* place asking the party to do a *specific* thing. The reason to gather again lives in that sentence. (The Adventure Hooks chapter will roll you one in thirty seconds.)
