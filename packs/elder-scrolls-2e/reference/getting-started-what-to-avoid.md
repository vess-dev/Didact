A short list of habits that kill Elder Scrolls 2e games faster than any dragon:

- **Railroading.** If the players walk away from the hook, let them. Pick it up later or drop it. Skyrim has more hooks than the Warden can prep, and the Adventure Hooks chapter makes more on demand.
- **Resurrecting the boss.** If a player talks the bandit chief down, the chief stays down. Don't undo a clever or merciful resolution because you wanted the fight.
- **Gotcha traps.** Every lethal danger should be telegraphed in the fiction first. A frost trap glitters; a pressure plate sits oddly; thin ice creaks. No PC should die to something the players were never shown.
- **Rules-lawyering.** This is a fiction-first game. When a rule and the fiction disagree, the fiction usually wins. Cite a rule as support, not as a trump card.
- **The clean ending.** The Civil War has no side that is simply right; most Daedric bargains cost more than they pay; a guild's rise leaves bodies. Let endings be as ambiguous as the source. A party that walks out without the prize can still have done something that mattered.
- **Punishing failure with boredom.** A failed save should make the scene *more* interesting, not stall it. Fail-forward is always on the table.
