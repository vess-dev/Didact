In Skyrim a sword is only as good as the smith who folded it, a poultice only as kind as the hand that picked its flowers, and a soul only as useful as the gem that caught it. The three crafting trades — **Smithing**, **Alchemy**, and **Enchanting** — let a PC turn ordinary inventory into finer gear, useful consumables, and charged Relics.

Each trade is worked as a **Downtime Action** (one per PC at a time, between sessions, in safe conditions) at the right **station**: a forge, an alchemy lab, an arcane enchanter. There is no new resource to track. You spend the things you already carry — ordinary items, **ingredients**, **soul gems**, and **Septims** — plus the time. If you have the station, the materials, and the Downtime Action, the work succeeds; the Warden calls for a save only when you rush, improvise, or attempt something beyond your skill.

What crafting **cannot** do is break the Cairn ceiling. Tempering improves a piece **once** — a weapon's die one step (never past **d12**), a suit of armor +1 Armor (never past the cap of **3**). Potions behave like any Cairn consumable — _petty_, single-use, no Fatigue. Enchanted gear becomes a **Relic** with charges and a Recharge condition. Better materials make gear lighter, finer, and more dreadful in the fiction — they never push the numbers higher than the rules already allow.

> **Stations away from home.** A homestead with the right wing (see the Warden's Guide) gives you a forge, lab, or enchanter at will. Otherwise you borrow a town's: the Skyforge in Whiterun, the Arcane Enchanter in a College alcove, the alchemist's bench in any apothecary — usually for a few Septims or a favor.
