**Paths** are the connectors of the map, from well-trod roads and trails to rough wilderness routes through the Wilds. A good path offers a real choice between POIs, weighing safety, speed, and difficulty against one another.

### Path Types

- **Standard** paths are obvious, well-trod routes between two POIs. _Drawn as a thick line._
- **Hidden** paths appear on no known map. Finding one always costs something. _Drawn as a dashed line._
- **Conditional** paths may be blocked or need special gear (a boat, climbing rope, a bridge) to cross — and some cannot be navigated at all without a map or a guide. _Drawn as a crossed line._

### Draw Paths

- Starting with the Heart, create 1–4 paths between each location.
  - Make loops, branches, dead-ends, and shortcuts between POIs.
  - Some paths run in chains tied to a single terrain (a mountain pass, a river course).
  - Some paths are Conditional, drawn as a crossed line.
  - Some paths are Hidden, drawn as a dashed line.
- Name each path by the numbers of the POIs it joins, e.g. 1 <-> 3. Note this in your notebook under the first POI or in a separate list.
- Decide whether a path is a road, a trail, or a wilderness route, and write the result down.
- Roll on the Path Features table for each path, noting the results.
- Work out the distances between POIs, and note the travel time on the map:
  - A Watch is 8 hours long. Mark days between POIs wherever you can (usually two Watches and one rest per day of travel).
  - A short distance takes one Watch, a medium distance two Watches, and a long distance three Watches.
  - For Trails, add 1 more Watch; for Wilderness routes, add 2.

### Put It All Together

Ask yourself the following:

- What draws people to this region? What keeps them here?
- How do the folk here regard outsiders? How do outsiders regard them?
- What hold do the Factions have over the people who live here?
- Who or what threatens their agendas?
- How would a faction deal with newcomers — say, an adventuring party?
- How do the region's terrain and weather shape the people who live there?

You should now have a firm idea of the people and terrain of this region, and your first region is complete. To grow the Realm, repeat the process as often as you like.

For example, if my results gave me a people who were _religious_ and sought _conversion_, I might take them for proud Talos-worshippers, prone to zeal and preaching even with the Thalmor watching. And if their land was _abundant_ in ore yet _scarce_ in food, I would guess they were a hard mining folk who bought their bread from the valley below, and went hungry whenever the road was snowed shut.

Suppose a faction came up as a band of _Nomads_ whose chief advantage was _conviction_. I might decide they were a Khajiit caravan barred from the city gates, kept moving by suspicion and law alike. If their agenda was to _cultivate a rare resource_ but they were _hindered by cultural taboos_, I might rule that they had hired the party to do the gathering in their place — perhaps frostbite-spider venom, or a rare mountain flower found only on a high, haunted ridge — and that the work must be kept quiet.

Finally, the party would meet one of the faction's agents: a spy posing as a peddler. Only she knows where the flower grows and how to take it safely. I keep in mind that she should turn up somewhere important — likely the region's **Heart**.
