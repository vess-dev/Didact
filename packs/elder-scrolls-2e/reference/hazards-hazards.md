Skyrim kills in small ways first. A long drop, a frozen lake, a guttering torch in a draugr's tomb, a winter night with no walls. This chapter is the short list. Every rule follows Cairn's pattern: a clear cost, a save when the outcome is genuinely uncertain, and damage to an **Attribute** (usually STR or DEX) rather than HP when the harm is environmental. **Armor** applies only when it would realistically help, and never exceeds the cap of 3.

**When a hazard and the fiction disagree, trust the fiction.** These are starting points, not tables to be consulted mid-scene. Telegraph every hazard before it matters — a player who never saw the trip-wire or the thin ice was not given a fair chance to avoid it. That is the contract.
