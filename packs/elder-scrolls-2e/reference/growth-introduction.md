The Elder Scrolls 2e keeps the Cairn approach to advancement: there are no experience points, no levels, and no reward for tallying slain draugr, emptied coffers, or barrows explored. A Warden who has played other games will feel the absence — and should resist filling it. What changes a character is not a bookkeeping milestone but a moment in the fiction that *mattered*: a Daedric heart consumed, a Spell Tome mastered with a sword at your throat, an oath sworn beneath the rafters of Jorrvaskr.

The world of Skyrim keeps moving — the war grinds on, the dragons return, the Holds change hands — and the player characters move with it. The events of a session can mark a character deeply, sometimes in ways they would never have chosen. With a few notable exceptions (such as **Scars**), growth always stems from a character's experience in the world. It can also come out of **Downtime**: weeks spent training under a Companion, researching a curse, or strengthening ties with a faction.
