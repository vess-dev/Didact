### Weather

Each day, the Warden rolls on the weather table for the appropriate season. If the "**Extreme**" weather result is rolled twice in a row, the weather turns to "**Catastrophic**" — a snow squall becomes a full blizzard, a thaw floods the valley, a slope lets go in an avalanche.

#### Weather Difficulty

| Weather | Effect | Examples |
| :--------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
|     **Nice**     | Favorable conditions for travel.                                                     | _Clear skies, sun on the snow_                               |
|     **Fair**     | Favorable conditions for travel.                                                     | _Overcast, a steady breeze_                                  |
|  **Unpleasant**  | Add a **Fatigue** _or_ add one **watch** to the journey.                             | _Gusting winds, cold rain, light snowfall, biting chill_     |
|  **Inclement**   | Add a **Fatigue** _or_ add **+1 watch**. Increase terrain **Difficulty** by a step. | _Sleet and thunder, freezing rain, deep slush underfoot_     |
|   **Extreme**    | Add a **Fatigue** _and_ add **+1 watch**. Increase terrain **Difficulty** by a step.| _Blizzards, howling icy winds, whiteout snow, river floods_  |
| **Catastrophic** | Most parties cannot travel under these conditions.                                   | _Full blizzard, avalanche, Solstheim ashstorm, tidal surge_  |

Skyrim's cold adds its own bite: a PC without warm clothing, shelter, or fire is **Deprived** after a watch in the snow, and **Extreme** weather leaves anyone without shelter Deprived at once. See **Hazards → Frost & Cold**.

### Wilderness Exploration Cycle

1. The **Warden** describes the current **point** or **region** and how the path, weather, terrain, or party status might affect **travel speed**. The party plots or adjusts a course toward their destination.
2. Each party member chooses a single **Wilderness Action**. The **Warden** narrates the results and then rolls on the **Wilderness Events** table. The party responds.
3. The **players** and the **Warden** record any loss of resources and new conditions (torch use, _deprivation_, etc.), and the cycle repeats.

### Wilderness Events

|       |                 |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1** | **Encounter**   | Roll on an encounter table for that terrain type or location. Don't forget to roll for NPC reactions if applicable.                                                |
| **2** | **Sign**        | The party discovers a clue, spoor, or hint of a nearby encounter, locality, hidden feature, or some fact about an area ahead.                                                                                                  |
| **3** | **Environment** | A shift in weather or terrain.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **4** | **Loss**        | The party faces a choice that costs them a resource (rations, tools, a lamed pack-mule), time, or effort.                                                                                                                      |
| **5** | **Exhaustion**  | The party meets a barrier (a snowed-over pass, a flooded ford, a rockslide), forcing effort, care, or delay. This might mean spending extra time (and an additional **Wilderness Action**) or adding **Fatigue** to inventory. |
| **6** | **Discovery**   | The party finds food, treasure, or other useful resources. The **Warden** may instead choose to reveal the primary feature of the area.                                                                                       |

### Wilderness Elements

#### Night

- The party can choose to travel by night and rest by day, but night travel is far slower and more treacherous.
- Traveling at night is always more dangerous. The **Warden** rolls _twice_ on the **Wilderness Events** table.
- Some terrain and weather may be easier to cross after dark (the Solstheim ash, for one). The **Warden** should weigh these against the added risks.

#### Sleep

- The last **watch** of the day is typically reserved for the **Make Camp** action.
- Characters need to sleep each day. Anything beyond a minor interruption can negate or cancel the benefits of sleep.
- If the party skips the **Make Camp** action, each member adds a **Fatigue** to inventory and is Deprived. Traveling sleep-deprived also raises the terrain **Difficulty** by a step (i.e. _Easy_ becomes _Tough_).

#### Light

- Torches and other radial light sources illuminate about 40ft ahead of the party, and beyond that only a dim outline of shapes.
- Characters without a light source may suffer _panic_ until their situation is remedied.
- Environmental conditions (a sudden mountain gust, blowing snow, spray off a river) can easily snuff a torch.

##### Light Sources

- A torch can be lit 3 times before degrading.
- A lantern can be relit indefinitely but requires a separate oil flask (6 uses).

### Wilderness Actions

#### Travel

- Travel begins. Obvious locations, features, and terrain of nearby areas are revealed according to their distance. This action is typically taken by the whole party as one.
- The party rolls 1d6 to see if they get lost along the way. This risk can rise or fall depending on path **Difficulty**, maps, party skills, and guides.
- If lost, the party may need to spend a **Wilderness Action** to find their way again. Otherwise, the party reaches the next **point** along their route.

> Remember to compare the result of getting lost to the relevant path **Difficulty**.

#### Explore

- One or more party members search a large area — looking for hidden features, scouting ahead, or treading carefully.
- A Location (a shelter, a village, a cave, an abandoned mine) or a Feature (a hot spring, an ice-locked river, a beached longship, a standing stone) is discovered.
- The **Travel** action is still required to _leave_ the current area, even once it has been fully explored.

#### Supply

- One or more party members may hunt, fish, or forage for food, collecting 1d4 **Rations** (3 uses each). The chance of a greater bounty rises with each additional participant (1d4 becomes 1d6, up to a maximum of 1d12).
- Relevant experience or equipment may also increase the bounty collected.
- The party may come upon farmsteads and small villages, spending Septims and a full **watch** to resupply.

#### Make Camp

- The party stops to set up camp in the Wilds. Each party member (and their mounts) consumes a **Ration**.
- A **lookout rotation** is set so the party can sleep unmolested — no small thing when a frost troll might prowl the pass. A smaller party may have to risk sleeping unguarded or switch off over several nights.
- Party members who were able to rest remove all **Fatigue** from their inventory.
