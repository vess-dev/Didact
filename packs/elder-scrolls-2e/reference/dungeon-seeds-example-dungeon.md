For this example, let's assume we are looking at a POI generated using the methods described in the Setting Seeds chapter. Our relevant results were:

- **Terrain**: _Tundra Plains_
- **Type**: _Nordic Barrow_
- **Feature**: _Isolated_

### What kind of dungeon is this, and what makes it unusual?

_A remote Nordic barrow on the high tundra, its iron door half-buried under a cairn of frost-rimed stones, cut off from the nearest steading by a long day's walk through the snow._

We then roll on the **Purpose**, **Construction**, and **Ruination** tables and get the following results:

- **Purpose**: A _burial barrow_, built by a _dragon cult_.
- **Construction**: The entrance is _a cairn of stones_, and its halls are cut from _hewn stone_.
- **Ruination**: _Divided_ by _competing rituals_.

### Why was this dungeon built and what caused its downfall?

_It was raised in the Merethic Era by a dragon cult to bind its honored dead — and to guard a Spell Tome of dread power against the day their dragon lords would return. Its dead were laid in alcoves and sworn to keep the Tome forever. For an age they slept. Then a Reach-born necromancer named **Velsa Greywether** found the barrow, broke its first seals, and began a long ritual to bend the draugr to her own service. Her ritual collided with the old binding-rites still humming in the stone, and the barrow split against itself: the draugr who heeded the old cult-priest's call on one side, the corpses she has already turned on the other. The Tome lies behind the cult's last seal, and both sides want it._

Continuing on to the current inhabitants of our dungeon, we get the following results (rolling for _two_ factions):

- **Denizens**: The creatures living here are _resilient_, yet _obstinate_.
- **Stand-out NPC** (Velsa): _suave_, yet _selfish_.
- **Faction A**: Goal: _Preservation_. Obstacle: _Opposition_.
- **Faction B**: Goal: _Dominion_. Obstacle: _Tradition_.

### What do we know about the creatures and factions that occupy the dungeon?

_The barrow's dead are hard to put down and refuse to abandon their charge. The old cult's draugr endure out of duty; the necromancer's risen serve out of compulsion. Neither side will yield._

### What is each faction trying to achieve, and what stands in their way?

**The Sworn Dead** — the draugr still answering the cult-priest **Hevnaak's** call — wish only to preserve the seal and the Tome behind it, as they have for more than four thousand years. They are opposed at every turn by the intruder and her puppets, who now hold the barrow's upper halls. **Velsa's Risen** — the necromancer and the draugr she has already bent — seek dominion over the whole barrow and the power sleeping at its heart. What stands in her way is the binding-rite itself: the old cult's tradition wove the final seal so that no one who has _raised_ the barrow's dead may pass it. Every corpse she turns to her cause is one more reason the seal will not open for her.

### Dungeon Theme

With some idea of the history, purpose, and denizens of the dungeon, we finally have our theme. Let's put it all together:

_Beneath a cairn on the lonely tundra lies a Nordic barrow built by a dragon cult to guard its dead and a single, dreadful Spell Tome. For more than four thousand years the draugr kept their watch. Then a necromancer broke in from above and began turning the dead to her own ends — and the barrow split in two. The old cult-priest's Sworn Dead hold the deep halls and the sealed reliquary; the necromancer's Risen hold the entrance halls above. Between them lies a flooded gallery neither side will cross._

_The Sworn Dead want only to keep the seal shut, as they always have. Hevnaak, the dragon-cult priest, sleeps behind the last seal and stirs whenever the binding-rite is threatened._

_Velsa Greywether wants the Tome and the barrow both. She cannot break the final seal herself — the old rite bars anyone who has raised the dead — and every corpse she turns deepens the bar. She has begun to suspect she will need living hands to open it for her, and she is not above bargaining._

_At the heart of the barrow, behind the cult's last seal (between **8** and **9**), lies the **Tome of the Hollow Choir** — a Spell Tome that calls the dead from their rest. In the wrong hands it could wake every barrow in the Hold._

#### Hooks

- A frightened farmer from the nearest steading offers 300 Septims apiece to "clear the dead thing's den" before it empties his fields of livestock and laborers. He does not know what truly waits below.
- The party is approached in secret by an agent of the **Vigil of Stendarr**. The Vigil has marked the barrow as a site of necromancy. The party is to descend, find and destroy the **Tome of the Hollow Choir**, and put down the witch who sought it. They are offered 1,000 Septims (total) and the Vigil's gratitude. Members of either faction below will stand against them.

### Map

_The barrow is drawn as nine keyed rooms below an entrance. The entrance sits at the top of the map near the page border; the flooded gallery (**7**) lies across the middle, dividing the upper rooms (**1–6**, held by the Risen) from the deep rooms (**8–9**, held by the Sworn Dead). A hidden tunnel links **6** to **8** beneath the water._

### Rimewatch Barrow

#### Features Throughout

- **Light**: Iron braziers and wall-sconces stand in most chambers. In the upper halls (**1–6**) a few burn with pale green necromantic flame; in the deep halls (**8–9**) none are lit.
- **Smell**: Cold stone, old dust, and — nearer the water — rot and stagnant melt.
- **Noise**: A slow drip of meltwater, the occasional grind of a stone slab, and faint chanting from somewhere below.
- **Build**: The walls and floor are hewn stone, fitted without mortar, carved with serpentine dragon-cult motifs and the curling script of the old tongue.

### Entrance: The Snow-Cairn

A low **iron door** is set into a hillside beneath a heaped **cairn** of frost-rimed stones. A weathered **stone arch** frames it, carved with a coiled dragon.

- **Iron Door**: Unbarred, slightly ajar. Cold air and the smell of rot leak out.
- **Cairn**: The stones have been recently disturbed — someone cleared the door not long ago. Boot prints in the snow lead in, not out.
- **Stone Arch**: An expert can read the worn carving: a warning that "the choir sleeps; let it not be woken." Steps lead down to **1**.

### 1 - Hall of Stories

**Lore**: _Hall of Stories_. _Writing._

A long, descending gallery. The walls are carved floor-to-ceiling with row upon row of pictographic **friezes**. A barred **gate** of iron blocks the far end. A **lever** sits in a niche.

- **Friezes**: They tell, in the old tongue and in pictures, the founding of the barrow and the binding of its dead. A reader who studies them learns that the final seal cannot be opened by any hand that has raised the dead (a clue to **8**). They also show the order in which the gallery's standing stones were raised (the key to **7**).
- **Gate**: The classic Nordic puzzle-gate. Three rotating **stone rings** above the lever must be turned to match three animal totems carved on a nearby **dragon claw** keystone (set in the wall, _bulky_, worth 250 Septims).
- **Lever**: Pulling it with the rings wrongly set drops a portcullis behind the party for a round and rings a stone gong (a _loud_ alarm — the Risen in **2** come to investigate).

### 2 - Barracks of the Risen

**Monster**: _Undead_. _Patrolling._

Stone sleeping-alcoves line both walls. Three **draugr** in ill-fitting iron stand watch, their eyes lit with the same green fire as the **brazier** in the center of the room. A scorched **circle** is chalked on the floor around it.

- **Draugr**: These are Velsa's Risen — newly turned, clumsy, and bound to her will. They patrol the upper halls and challenge intruders, croaking half-remembered words of the old tongue.
  - They will not cross into the flooded gallery (**7**); their mistress has forbidden it.
- **Brazier**: Burns with cold green necromantic flame. If extinguished (slow, awkward work: it must be smothered), the Risen in this room collapse, inert, for one scene.
- **Circle**: The chalk binding that ties these particular draugr to Velsa. Scuffing it breaks her hold — they become Sworn Dead and turn on her Risen instead.

### 3 - Embalming Chamber

**Lore**: _Infirmary_. _Smell._

Stone slabs hold the half-prepared **dead**. Clay **canopic jars**, bundles of dried herbs, and an alchemist's **kit** are laid out on a side table. The reek of natron and rot is strong.

- **Dead**: Two bodies, partly wrapped, not yet risen. One still wears a silver amulet of Arkay (worth 120 Septims) — proof a recent traveler died here, not an ancient Nord.
- **Canopic Jars**: Three are sealed; one holds a _petty_ vial of embalming oil that, drunk, cures one disease (1 use). The others hold only ash.
- **Kit**: A working alchemist's kit, and Velsa's ritual notes. The notes confirm she cannot pass the final seal and is "seeking living hands, willing or not" (a clue to her bargain).

### 4 - Collapsed Gallery

**Trap**: _Crushes_. _Crossing._

A wide chamber whose ceiling has partly fallen. A narrow path threads between heaps of **rubble** beneath a sagging **slab**. Loose **stones** litter the floor.

- **Rubble**: Passable, but the footing is treacherous. A bypass to the East is open but tight.
- **Slab**: Cracked and barely held. Crossing the center of the room (or any loud disturbance, like combat) brings it down: DEX save or take 1d6 STR damage and be pinned (a STR save to crawl free).
- **Stones**: Among them, a careful searcher finds a **steel war axe** (d8) dropped by some earlier looter, and 2d20 Septims in a rotted purse.

### 5 - The Watcher's Den

**Monster**: _Humanoid_. _Scheming._

A vaulted chamber Velsa has made her own. **Velsa Greywether** stands over a stone table strewn with **scrolls**, flanked by two of her strongest **draugr thralls**. A caged **death hound** paces in the corner.

- **Velsa**: A Reach-born necromancer in road-worn robes. She would rather talk than fight a well-armed party. She knows she needs living hands to open the seal in **8** and will _offer_ to let the party take "any gold or trinket" if they will open it for her — meaning to betray them the moment the Tome is in reach.
- **Scrolls**: Among them, a usable **Scroll of Ice Storm** (_petty_, 1 use) and a hand-drawn map marking the hidden tunnel from **6** to **8**.
- **Death Hound**: Frost-touched and starving. If freed (or if Velsa is threatened), it attacks; its bite chills.

#### Velsa Greywether

6 HP, 11 STR, 12 DEX, 14 WIL, bound dagger (d6), frost spell (d8, _blast_)

- A gaunt, sharp-eyed necromancer of the Reach, robed in gray and bone-fetishes. Calm, persuasive, and utterly without scruple.
- Carries the **Tome of the Hollow Choir's** lesser cousin — a **Spell Tome: Reanimate Corpse** — at her belt; she must free both hands to read it. Cut her down and it can be taken.
- **Critical Damage**: Her death-grip on the Risen fails. Every draugr she raised (in **2** and **5**) collapses inert for the rest of the scene.

#### Draugr Thrall

6 HP, 1 Armor, 12 STR, 10 DEX, 8 WIL, ancient war axe (d8)

- An ancient Nord corpse, leather-dry and ember-eyed, in rusted iron and fur. Slow to wake, relentless once roused.
- Immune to poison and disease; frost damage against it is _Impaired_.
- Cannot attack in the round it climbs from an alcove or sarcophagus.

#### Death Hound

6 HP, 12 STR, 13 DEX, 5 WIL, freezing bite (d8)

- A frost-rimed undead hound with pale breath, bred by vampires as a guard-beast. Velsa bought this one, and has not fed it.
- Immune to poison; frost damage against it is _Impaired_. Whatever it bites is **slowed**.
- **Critical Damage**: the cold sinks deep; the target loses 1d4 more STR.

### 6 - Cistern Cells

**Lore**: _Cistern_. _Moisture._

A dripping vaulted room, knee-deep in black **meltwater**. Rusted iron **cages** hang from the ceiling on chains. A low **arch** on the South wall is choked with rubble.

- **Meltwater**: Knee-deep and bitterly cold. Wading it for long without warming up afterward leaves a PC Deprived (the cold).
- **Cages**: Three. One holds the slumped, frozen body of a Vigilant of Stendarr — and his pack, with a **lantern**, Rations (2 uses), and a journal naming Velsa and warning that the barrow's true danger lies "behind the last door, not before it."
- **Arch**: The choked South arch hides the entrance to the **hidden tunnel** to **8** (a Hidden path). Clearing it is the work of a few minutes — and it bypasses the flooded gallery entirely.

### 7 - The Drowned Gallery

**Special**: _Pool_. _Reveals._

A long, flooded hall, the dividing line of the barrow. Dark **water** stands waist-deep across the whole floor. A row of submerged **standing stones** marches down the center. At the far end, an iron **gate** leads down to the deep halls.

- **Water**: Cold and still. Neither faction will cross it: the Risen are forbidden, and the Sworn Dead guard the gate beyond. Something pale shows through the water: the bones of those who touched the stones in the wrong order.
- **Standing Stones**: When all are touched in the order shown by the friezes in **1**, the water drains through hidden sluices, revealing the gallery floor — and a sunken **chest** (300 Septims, a **Spell Tome: Healing**). Touched in the wrong order, the sluices reverse and the room floods to the ceiling (drowning rules; a 3-round grace, then a STR save each round).
- **Gate**: Barred from the far side by the Sworn Dead. Beyond lie **8** and **9**.

### 8 - The Sworn Watch

**Monster**: _Undead_. _Protecting._

A round, pillared chamber lit by no flame. Six stone **sarcophagi** ring the walls; their lids are intact. A sealed **door** of black stone stands at the North, carved with a dragon swallowing the sun. A faint, cold **chant** fills the air.

- **Sarcophagi**: Each holds a **draugr** of the Sworn Dead. They wake only if the sealed door is touched, the chant is interrupted, or one of their number is destroyed — then all six rise over two rounds.
  - These draugr will _not_ pursue beyond this chamber. They exist only to keep the seal.
  - If the party arrives having freed or destroyed Velsa's Risen, the Sworn Dead may be reasoned with (a WIL save): they care nothing for gold, only the seal.
- **Sealed Door**: The cult's final seal. It opens only for a hand that has _never_ raised the dead — meaning Velsa can never pass it, but the PCs likely can (Warden's discretion). Pressing a living, unstained palm to the dragon's eye grinds the door open to **9**.
- **Chant**: The binding-rite, whispered by the bound choir beyond the door. While it continues, the sarcophagi cannot be forced.

#### Draugr (Sworn Dead)

6 HP, 1 Armor, 12 STR, 10 DEX, 8 WIL, ancient sword (d8)

- Ember-eyed Nord dead in old iron and fur, far older and surer than the Risen above. They croak the words of the old tongue as they fight.
- Immune to poison and disease; frost damage against them is _Impaired_.
- The eldest among them can loose a short **frost shout** once per combat — a cone of killing cold (d6, _blast_).
- Cannot attack in the round they rise from a sarcophagus.

### 9 - The Hollow Choir

**Special**: _Voices_. _Silences._

A high, narrow reliquary behind the seal. A robed **figure** kneels before a stone **lectern**, unmoving. The walls are honeycombed with empty burial **niches**, and a faint **choir** of whispering voices fills the room.

- **Figure**: **Hevnaak**, the dragon-cult priest, more than four thousand years dead, kneeling in eternal vigil. He turns to ash if destroyed, leaving his mask and gear lootable. He wakes only if the Tome is taken from the lectern — or if the party makes plain they mean to destroy it, in which case he may _help_ them, his duty discharged at last.
- **Lectern**: Holds the **Tome of the Hollow Choir** (see below). Lifting it silences the whispering choir — and, if Hevnaak yet kneels, wakes him.
- **Niches**: Empty. They are meant to be filled — the Tome could fill them all, from any barrow in the Hold.
- **Choir**: The whispering of the bound dead. It guides nothing and harms nothing. It is the Tome's own voice: lift the Tome and the room falls silent, and the whispering goes with the book.

#### Hevnaak, Dragon-Cult Priest

10 HP, 2 Armor, 10 STR, 11 DEX, 16 WIL, withering touch (d8), frost shout (d8, _blast_)

- A withered Nord priest in a graven mask and rotted ceremonial robes, kneeling in the dark since the Merethic Era. He speaks the old tongue and, sometimes, the common one.
- Immune to poison and disease; frost damage against him is _Impaired_. His mask is a lesser dragon-priest mask: a helmet (+1 Armor) that the Warden may give one small power in the style of the masks in **Unique Armors**.
- He does not wish to fight a party that means to end the threat he has guarded against. If they mean to _take_ the Tome, he resists to the last.
- **Critical Damage**: A target struck down by his touch ages a decade in an instant — STR loss from his touch returns only with a season's rest, not a week's.

#### Tome of the Hollow Choir

- A heavy black Spell Tome bound in graven bone, cold to the touch. Read aloud in both hands, it calls the unburied and the barrow-dead within earshot to rise and serve the reader for one scene (each 3 HP, 1 Armor, its old weapon d6, as **Reanimate Corpse**); then add a Fatigue (a WIL save if Deprived or in danger, per the rules of magic). _The whispering of the bound choir leaks faintly from its pages whenever it is opened._
- It is no mere Relic but a true Spell Tome of dread power. In the wrong hands it could wake every barrow in the Hold. The Vigil of Stendarr wants it destroyed; a necromancer would kill for it.
- Destroying it (the Vigil's price) requires burning it in a consecrated fire, or casting it into the deep — the Sea of Ghosts, or the Deep beneath a Dwemer ruin, where no hand will find it again.
