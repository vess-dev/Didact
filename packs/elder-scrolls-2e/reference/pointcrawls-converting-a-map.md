This leans directly on the wilderness travel procedure — the same path, terrain, and weather difficulties you'd use for any journey.

- **Mark the places worth reaching.** Number every point of interest on the map: Hold capitals, garrisons and forts, wayshrines, mines, barrows, standing stones, the inn at a crossroads.
- **Trace the routes between them.** Draw in the obvious connectors — maintained roads, mountain trails, river crossings, coastal tracks. Note any point that has no good route to the others; reaching it will mean cutting cross-country through the open Wilds.
- **Time each leg.** For every pair of joined points, work out the rough travel time in watches (or days), totalling the penalties from the path, the terrain, and the weather, and folding in the party's pace, their mounts, and anything else in play that might speed or slow them.

### A Worked Conversion

Suppose you are mapping a corner of the North: a road climbing from a river-vale up through a high mountain pass, with a branch road bending west into the marshes of Hjaalmarch. You've numbered seven points and traced the routes. Walking the legs one at a time, on foot, the travel times come out like this.

- **Point 1 to Point 2 — six watches.** This leg runs over a *medium* distance, by *trail* through mountains and pine forest.
  - Add two watches for the *medium* path distance.
  - Add one watch for the *trail*.
  - Add two watches for the *perilous* mountain terrain, plus one more for the stretch of forest.

- **Point 2 to Point 3 — four watches.** A short hop deeper into the high country.
  - Add one watch for the *short* path distance.
  - Add one watch for the *trail* through the mountains.
  - Add two watches for the *perilous* mountain terrain.

- **Point 2 to Point 4 — three watches.** The kind country: the maintained road down into the forested vale.
  - Add two watches for the *medium* path distance.
  - Add nothing for travelling a well-kept *road*.
  - Add one watch for the *tough* forest terrain.

- **Point 4 to Point 5 — four watches.** The branch west, into the bogs of Hjaalmarch.
  - Add one watch for the *short* path distance.
  - Add one watch for the *trail* through the marsh.
  - Add two watches for the swamp, which the Warden rates *Perilous* — sucking mud, hidden sinks, foul water, and worse things wading in it.

- **Point 4 to Point 6 — three watches.** Out across open ground.
  - Add two watches for the *medium* path distance.
  - Add one watch for the *trail* across the plains.
  - Add nothing for the *easy* plains terrain.

- **Point 7 — at least five watches from anywhere.** This is the unjoined point: a barrow off in the trackless Wilds, with no road or trail to it.
  - Add two watches for the *medium* path distance.
  - Add two watches for cutting through the open *Wilds*.
  - Add one watch for the *tough* forest terrain along the way.

With every leg timed, the map is now a pointcrawl: the party can look at the web of routes, weigh the long safe road against the short perilous trail, and you can total their chosen path into days at a glance. Re-time a leg on the fly whenever the weather turns or a bridge washes out — the arithmetic is the same, and a flooded ford or a snowed-over pass is just another penalty added to the count.
