### Meddling With Something (or Someone) They Do Not Understand

> Brynja and her shield-siblings were sent to find a horker-hunter and his daughter, lost somewhere in the haunted pines below Mount Anthor. On the way, Brynja found a cairn split open by frost-heave, and inside it a black, fist-sized stone that pulsed with a cold light — a **black soul gem**, fully charged, humming with a trapped human soul. She knew it was valuable (but not what it truly was) and pocketed it, ignoring her companions' muttering. Over the next days she began to see frostbite spiders where there had been none: in the rafters of inns, along the road, at the edge of every camp. One night she dreamed of a robed figure with a face of bone and antlers, reaching into her chest. She woke afraid, and told no one. The dreams sharpened, and the spiders crept closer, until they skittered openly across tavern floors. Resolute, she carried the gem to a wayshrine of Arkay and shattered it on the altar, freeing the soul within. Only then did the dreams stop and the spiders vanish. But the next morning her breath fogged the air no matter the warmth, and the cold no longer touched her at all. A Wise Woman of the nearby village told her that by carrying a stolen soul so long she had drawn the eye of a creature of the cold deeps, and that she now belonged half to it. She would feel no cold and need no fire — but on the night of a black moon, she must answer when it called, or be drawn down into the Deep to answer in person.

Brynja made a chain of choices that spiralled out of her control: taking the gem despite warnings, ignoring the spiders, dismissing the dreams begging her to give it up, and at last destroying it. Each step deepened the Trigger; the change that followed was permanent, double-edged, and entirely earned.

### Long-Term Mastery of a Spell Tome or Relic

> All through her travels, Sigrlaug leaned on a **Frostbite** Spell Tome she had "borrowed" from the College of Winterhold. Time and again she tried to push it past its limits — reaching for more targets, more cold — and made a WIL save each time. Not every attempt succeeded, and now and then she paid dearly: a destroyed page, a sudden Fatigue, frost-scorched hands. Then, deep in a Falmer warren beneath the Reach, the party met a chaurus the size of a cart. Seeing a chance to turn the cave's own chill against it, her companions ringed her while she read aloud from the Tome under the thing's charge. The Warden called for a WIL save, since she was casting fast and under duress — a success! The Warden ruled that from then on, Sigrlaug could channel that Spell Tome under duress or time pressure **without** first making a WIL save.

Sigrlaug had bent that Tome to her will many times, with mixed results. She took a real risk casting it quickly in a desperate moment, and because a Spell Tome is a unique object of great power, her success despite that risk made the growth land all the harder.

### Forging a Bond With a Being of Great Power

> Eyvar and his companions found a forgotten shrine in a sea-cave beneath the Pale, its idol cracked and weeping brine. After much study and trial (most of it Eyvar's), they cleansed and woke the half-mad spirit bound within — a drowned thing older than the Nords, neither Aedra nor Daedra. In gratitude it offered its unknowable essence to anyone willing to take it. Eyvar stepped forward and was bound to it, leaving his old prayers behind for a new and stranger faith. Days later, gills opened along his neck after he was dragged under by a riptide off the coast — and he rose unharmed, breathing the sea.

Through his patient effort to understand the idol and learn its purpose, Eyvar showed a clear pattern of behavior. The outcome was deeply uncertain, yet he took a grave risk by giving himself over to an entity he did not know or understand. Two Triggers, met — and the change came.

### Overcoming a Long-Standing Woe, Misdeed, or Foe

> At last Old Thorgrim ran down Hrokkar the Cruel, the vampire who had butchered his village a generation before. But instead of taking the creature's head, Thorgrim used a charged **Soul Trap** Relic to wrench the vampire's essence from its body as it lay broken — and then, to the table's horror, drew that essence into himself. The Warden called for a WIL save to master so vile a spirit: a success! The Warden asked the player to re-roll Thorgrim's WIL, telling him he could keep the result if it was higher. It was. But the Warden also ruled that should Thorgrim ever again hold a beaten enemy at his mercy, he must make a WIL save — or the cruelty of the thing he had swallowed would surface and act through his hands.

In his relentless pursuit of a powerful foe, Thorgrim showed a distinct pattern of behavior. Drawing in the vampire's essence was a clear risk, and although the Soul Trap Relic was familiar to him, using it to move a soul between bodies was plainly meddling with a thing not fully understood. Growth came with both a gift and a leash.

### Injury or Contamination

> After days of searching, Galdrek the Alchemist found the root of the wasting sickness gripping a poor Hold village: a nest of **Namira's rot** fungus and rotting chaurus eggs choking the bottom of the town well. It took hours of grim work to clear the network, but he left with a full purse and an easy conscience. He then set out on a ten-day crossing of the volcanic tundra of Eastmarch toward a Dwemer ruin he'd heard whispers of. A week on, he noticed a strange film clouding his waterskin; cutting it open, he found he had been drinking from the tainted well all along. Oddly, none of the usual sickness had touched him. The Warden explained this was likely because he had been counteracting it with his habitual sips of **frost mirriam**, brewed for cold resistance against the highland nights. He spent a few hours making a fresh skin and pressed on. Two days later, pale growths rose along his forearms, and a familiar gray film spread over his skin — and his appetite for Rations vanished entirely. The Warden ruled that from then on Galdrek no longer needed ordinary food, subsisting instead on rot, spores, and mushroom; but should he ever be soaked through, he must take a Fatigue and shed a cloud of spores the following day.

This involved only a small risk, but the interaction between two unique items — the tainted fungus and the frost mirriam — was potent enough to justify a kind of *passive* growth. Even so, it was Galdrek's deliberate (if ultimately needless) habit of dosing himself with frost mirriam that served as the true Trigger.

### Learning Through Trial and Error

> Falgrim was bookish, anxious, and neither strong nor quick. Over months on the road he had built a way to stay out of straight fights: hurling a weighted net over an enemy, then darting in while the target floundered, prone and defenseless. Without his allies guarding him, the move left Falgrim badly exposed, especially against several foes at once. Each time he tried it, his player had to spell out exactly how Falgrim closed on his mark, and if the obvious dangers went unaddressed, the Warden called for a DEX save. After one especially reckless attempt failed, the whole table — Warden included — was so taken with the care and cunning behind it that the Warden ruled that from then on, Falgrim would no longer save to throw the net in combat. It would simply succeed, because Falgrim had clearly mastered the trick of taking foes off their feet.

Though Falgrim had failed more than once with his net, the thought and preparation behind each attempt, and his stubborn drive to "get it right," were enough to earn a new ability. Remember that in a normal fight the PCs have already made their first-round DEX saves, so further DEX saves are only called for when a character courts *additional* risk — a bold maneuver, a flight from battle, and the like. Nor must the target of an action always save: only the character *most at risk* rolls.

### Triumph (or Failure) Against the Grain of a Natural Talent

> Rolfgar and his companions were hunting a band of poachers led by a notorious werewolf. They tracked the outlaws to a hide-out among the Falkreath pines and crept up at dawn, hoping the pack would still be sleeping off the night's kill. Rolfgar led the party along the camp's edge, meaning to slip in unseen. It went harder than hoped: the poachers had posted sentries at four points. The Warden ruled that since Rolfgar was leading *and* had the worst natural aptitude for it (his player had rolled a dismal 5 DEX), he would make the save the whole party depended on to enter undetected. He passed — and the Warden declared that from then on, Rolfgar would no longer need to save DEX in such situations, his character having plainly found a knack for moving unseen.

This is a clear case of a character working *against* type. Despite Rolfgar's low DEX, his choice to lead the party and his ultimate success were Trigger enough for growth. Sometimes the most memorable advancement comes from the character least suited to it.

### Faction Advancement

> Three weeks ago the **Companions** of Jorrvaskr asked Aela's recruit, Ormvald, to clear a giant's camp menacing a farm outside Whiterun. He returned early, the work done well. The shield-siblings were impressed enough to formally invite him to take the first oath and join as a **Whelp**. The Warden explained that the rank carried no strict duties but paid a share of any contract and came with the welcome of any Companion hall, asking only that he take on occasional work in the order's name. To rise higher, he would one day have to prove himself in the Circle. Ormvald agreed to join as a Whelp for now, and the Warden laid out the Downtime **Milestones** tracking his standing with the Companions:

- [ ] Complete one job on behalf of the Companions.
- [ ] Complete two jobs on behalf of the Companions.
- [ ] Complete three jobs on behalf of the Companions.
- [ ] Train in the yard with a member of the Circle.
- [ ] Be inducted into the Circle.

> The Warden told Ormvald he would receive a set of the order's gear — a steel sword (d8) and a shield (+1 Armor) from Eorlund's racks — the run of Jorrvaskr, and a cut of each contract while on the order's business. He was also expected to report to the Harbinger or a Circle member when he could. A few sessions later the Companions asked his help guarding a caravan through the bandit-thick passes of the Reach. The whole party rode along, and despite a near-fatal ambush, the job was done. Ormvald's player marked off the second Milestone, having already cleared the first before joining.

> Months pass. Ormvald completes two more contracts and trains in the yard with Farkas of the Circle, learning endurance and the two-handed forms — raising his maximum HP, and earning a Skyforge greatsword of his own. He also learns the Circle's secret: that its inner members carry the blood of the beast. Offered the gift, he kneels, drinks, and is inducted. The Warden explains the new shape of his life: as one of the beast-blooded, Ormvald is immune to mundane disease and can take Beast Form once per day — but silver bites deep, in beast form or out, and the Hunt must be fed. (See **Playing Unusual PCs** for the werewolf in full.)

This example draws on activity both in play and between sessions, using two Downtime procedures (*Strengthening Ties* and *Training*). Farkas served as the **Master** for the training, and although it isn't shown here, that strand of Ormvald's journey likely carried a second tier of Milestones. Faction advancement is rarely free: the gift of the blood was also a chain.

### Resolution of a Bond or Omen

> Cursed by a Forsworn hagraven after he burned one of their briar-shrines, Cobjorn carried a **Stone Heart** (1 Armor) lodged in his chest. With each passing month the stone grew heavier by one slot, and so long as the debt stood, he could not truly die. Desperate, his player asked the Warden whether Cobjorn might spend a Downtime Action researching a cure. The Warden offered these **Milestones**:

- [ ] Find a Master — a hagraven, a Wise Woman, a priest of Arkay, or a powerful spirit of the wood.
- [ ] Seek the Master out and learn their price.
- [ ] Meet that price to lift the Stone Heart.

> Cobjorn spent days between adventures questioning healers and priests across the Hold, to no avail. At last a tracker the party had hired before let slip that she had once seen a **spriggan matron** deep in a grove beneath Eastmarch's hot springs. In her tale she had followed a wounded elk to a steaming pool; the beast plunged in and vanished, and moments later rose again healed and unafraid, while the grove's wooden guardian watched from the treeline. The tracker had laid down her weapons and pack, and the spriggan let her pass; the next day an old wound of hers had closed on its own. For a price, she offered to guide Cobjorn's band to the grove. He agreed, and they set out the next morning (and his player marked off a Milestone). Over the following weeks they found the spriggan matron and won her favor by tending the grove and driving off the loggers who had poisoned its roots. In the end she offered each of them a Boon — and Cobjorn was at last freed of the Stone Heart.

Cobjorn's player had rolled a Bond at character creation tying him to a curse of the wild places (the kind of hook the **Bonds** table in the Player's Guide is built to provide). To lift it he had to research the location of a Master, then strike a bargain. Luckily he had a standing tie to a hireling familiar with the grove and its denizens, which gave the party their thread to pull. Resolving a Bond or Omen should always yield growth of some kind — for the entangled character, or for the whole party.

> See **Bonds and Omens** for designing the hooks themselves, and the Player's Guide **Character Creation** chapter for the Bonds and Omens tables.
