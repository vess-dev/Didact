The Player's Guide gives travelers the lay of the land. This is the deeper map — the Warden's gazetteer of the nine Holds and the island of Solstheim, with the things a guidebook leaves out: where the dead lie thickest, which Jarl is about to fall, and what trouble is ripe for a party to walk into.

Each Hold below gives its **capital**, its **Jarl and where they lean in the Civil War** (with the rival who replaces them if the Hold changes hands), a short reading of the **land**, a clutch of **key locations** — always a Nordic barrow, a Dwemer ruin where one exists, and a notable site or two — a few **troubles** ripe for adventure, and one or two **named NPCs** to put a face on the place. It is meant to be usable at the table, not exhaustive; grab a Hold, a trouble, and a face, and you have an evening.

A note on the war: the Holds lean, but they are not fixed. Should the Stormcloaks or the Legion win a Hold, the Jarl named here may be deposed and the rival installed. Run the map as it stands at the start of 4E 201, and let the war move the pieces as the party's deeds — and the campaign — dictate.
