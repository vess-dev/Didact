The Player's Guide tables are a starting set, not the limit. When you write Bonds and Omens for your own game, draw on the specific texture of Skyrim:

- **Anchor to real things.** A named Hold, faction, Daedric Prince, ruin, or NPC gives a Bond or Omen weight. "A curse" is thin; "a Forsworn hagraven cracked every mirror you pass" is a hook.
- **Bonds are personal; Omens are atmospheric.** If a thread only matters to one character, it is a Bond. If it would trouble *any* party passing through, it is an Omen.
- **Leave room to interpret.** The best entries pose a question the table answers in play. Who marked you in that dream? What is owed for the knucklebones in your pack? Where is the dead Jarl's hidden heir?
- **Tie resolution to Growth.** Decide, even loosely, what changes when the thread is pulled to its end — a cured affliction, a faction's favor, a re-rolled attribute, a new ability gained at a cost.
- **Make ignoring an Omen cost something.** An Omen the players walk past should ripen into the next session's crisis.

> For what "Growth of some kind" can look like, and for the Downtime **Milestones** that often carry a Bond or Omen to its resolution, see the **Growth** chapter.
