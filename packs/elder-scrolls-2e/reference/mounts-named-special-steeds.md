A handful of mounts are legends in their own right. These are **not** a new subsystem — each is written as a **Relic** or a **companion**, balanced to its kind. They start where their owners do (the Dark Brotherhood, the Soul Cairn, a stable in Whiterun) and are found, earned, or bonded in play, never bought off a counter.

### Shadowmere

The Dark Brotherhood's black mare, with eyes like coals — a horse the Brotherhood swears cannot truly die.

- A **companion mount**: +4 slots; a fearless warhorse that **never makes the startled-mount save** and lets her rider charge as a daring maneuver (_Enhanced_).
- **6 HP, 1 Armor**, hooves **d8+d8** when she fights of her own accord.
- *Near-undying:* should Shadowmere be slain, she does not stay dead. Leave her be; by the next sunrise she rises from the nearest dark water, whole, and finds her way back to her rider.

### Arvak

*(Relic — a charged summons, not a living mount.)*

A skeletal steed bound to the **Soul Cairn**, whose name is spoken to call him across the veil.

- **1 charge.** Speak his name to summon Arvak — a tireless undead horse, +4 slots, who **never startles** and treats **Perilous** terrain as one step easier (he simply walks where living horses balk). He serves until you dismiss him or the day turns.
- Being a thing of bone and soul, Arvak needs no feed and cannot be stolen or eaten when left behind — when unsummoned, he is simply gone.
- **Recharge:** dawn. Arvak returns to the Soul Cairn each day and can be called again the next.

### Frost

A magnificent white warhorse of fine breeding, swift and bold — the prize of a Whiterun stable, taken by theft or by the favor of its owner.

- A **companion mount**: +4 slots; **8 HP**, hooves **d10+d10**. As a trained warhorse he **never makes the startled-mount save**, and a rider may charge from his back as a daring maneuver (_Enhanced_).
- Spirited and proud: Frost will not suffer a cruel hand, and a rider who mistreats him may find the bond — and the steadiness — gone.

### A Giant-Tamed Mammoth *(flavor aside)*

The giants of Skyrim herd **mammoths** the way a farmer herds cattle, and a rare few wanderers have struck the same bargain. A tamed mammoth is no riding horse — it is a walking baggage train (treat as a wagon's worth of slots, *slow*) and a wall of tusk and bone in a fight (a *detachment*-grade beast in the Bestiary). The Warden should treat a mammoth as a story and a logistics problem, not a purchase: gentle one and you have moved more loot than a caravan, but you have also told every giant in the Hold exactly where their herd went.
