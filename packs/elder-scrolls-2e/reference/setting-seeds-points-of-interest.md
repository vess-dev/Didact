Points of Interest on a map (POIs) fall into the following kinds:

- **Heart**: The chief settlement of a region. It need not be the biggest or the most important place, but it should always matter to the region's history and folk — a Hold capital, a thriving trade town, a fortified longhouse.
- **Settlements**: Permanent, largely self-sufficient population centers, usually ruled by a Jarl, a steward, or a faction.
- **Waypoints**: A safe place to refill supplies, get a good night's rest under a roof, or ask the road ahead — an inn, a wayshrine, a hunter's lodge.
- **Curiosities**: A wilderness locale of note: somewhere strange or remarkable. It may be dangerous, but only if the party meddles with it.
- **Lairs**: A bandit camp, a spider nest, a den of beasts, or some other source of heightened monster activity.
- **Dungeons**: An explicitly dangerous site: a Nordic barrow, a buried Dwemer ruin, a Falmer-infested cave.

### Draw Points of Interest

- Draw the letter **H** near a water source on the map.
- Roll 3–8 d6 onto the map, setting aside any dice that roll off the page.
- Note the face-up result of each die, then use the POI Die Drop Table to mark each POI. A die showing a 4, for instance, marks a Lair, written as the letter "L" on the page.
- Roll any dice that fell off the page, repeating the last step until none remain.
- Starting with the Heart, number each POI on the map.
- Roll on the relevant tables for each POI — including the Heart (usually a Waypoint or Settlement). Note the results in your notebook.

> Some POIs may let the party see more of the world than what shows on their map — a high tower, the Throat of the World, an eagle's view from a peak.
