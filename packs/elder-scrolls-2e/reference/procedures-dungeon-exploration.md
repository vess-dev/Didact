> Although the word "dungeon" is used here, it means any dangerous, enclosed locale: a draugr-haunted Nordic barrow, a buried Dwemer ruin, a Falmer warren, a frost-rimed cave, a bandit fort, a Forsworn redoubt, a sunken shipwreck.

### The Basics

- Dungeon exploration runs as a cycle (see below), divided into **Turns**, **Actions**, and their consequences.
- On their **turn**, a character can move a distance equal to their light's perimeter (about 40ft) and take one **action**. A character may instead use their action to move up to three times that distance — but moving fast raises the chance of a roll on the **Dungeon Events** table.
- The **Warden** presents obvious information about an area and its dangers freely, at no cost. Moving quickly or carelessly may rouse a wandering draugr, spring a trap, or trigger a roll on the **Dungeon Events** table.

### Dungeon Exploration Cycle

1. The **Warden** describes the party's surroundings and any immediate dangers (combat, traps, surprises, etc.). The players then declare their characters' intended movements and **actions**.
2. The Warden resolves the **actions** of each character simultaneously, along with any **actions** already in progress. The Die of Fate is a useful tool whenever the Warden is in doubt.
3. The players record any loss of resources and any new conditions (item use, _deprivation_, etc.). The cycle then begins again. If appropriate, the **Warden** rolls on the **Dungeon Events** table. Keep common sense in mind when interpreting the results.

### Dungeon Events

Exploring a barrow or ruin is always dangerous, and time must always be weighed against the risk of waking the dead, the Dwemer's old machines, or worse.

Roll on the table below when the party:

- Spends more than one dungeon cycle in a single room or location,
- Moves quickly or haphazardly through a room,
- Moves into a new area, level, or zone,
- Creates a loud disturbance (a sprung gate, a shattered urn, a spell going off).

|       |                 |                                                                                                                                       |
| :---: | :-------------: | :-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: |
| **1** |  **Encounter**  | Roll on an encounter table. Possibly _hostile_. (See Reactions.)         |
| **2** |    **Sign**     |                          A clue, spoor, track, drained corpse, scratched ward, or scent is discovered.                               |
| **3** | **Environment** | The surroundings shift or escalate. Water rises through a flooded crypt, a ceiling sloughs ice, a Dwarven automaton clanks awake, a draugr ritual nears its close. |
| **4** |    **Loss**     |           A torch gutters out, an ongoing spell fizzles, a soul gem cracks. The party must resolve the effect before moving on.       |
| **5** | **Exhaustion**  |                The party must rest (triggering another roll on this table), add a **Fatigue**, or consume a ration.                  |
| **6** |    **Quiet**    |                                       The party is left alone (and safe) for the time being.                                          |

### Actions

- **Actions** are any non-passive activities: _searching for traps_, _forcing open a barrow door_, _listening for danger_, _disarming a trap_, _fighting draugr_, _channeling a Spell Tome_, _dodging a swinging blade_, _fleeing_, _resting_, and so on.
- Some **actions** have special rules (see below); others may take multiple **turns** to complete.
- Loud or noticeable **actions** may trigger an **encounter** with whatever sleeps in the dark.

#### Searching

- A character can spend a **turn** making an _exhaustive_ search of **one** object or location in an area, revealing any relevant hidden treasure, traps, secret doors, hollow walls, and the like.
- Larger chambers and complex ruins (a Dwemer hall, a sprawling barrow) may take a few **turns** to search properly.
- Searching a room _first_ is a safer way to explore, but it has a steep cost: time.

#### Resting

- A character can spend a turn **resting** to restore all **HP**.
- A light source and a _safe location_ are required to **rest**. Present or oncoming danger makes **rest** impossible.
- **Resting** does not clear **Fatigue**, as it is impossible to safely **Make Camp** inside a barrow or ruin.

### Panic

- A character surrounded by enemies, swallowed by darkness, or facing their deepest fears may experience _panic_. A **WIL** **save** is typically required to avoid losing control and becoming _panicked_.
- A _panicked_ character must make a **WIL** **save** to overcome the condition as an **action** on their **turn**.
- A _panicked_ character has 0 **HP**, does not act in the first round of combat, and all of their attacks are _impaired_.

### Dungeon Elements

#### Light

- Torches and other radial light sources illuminate about 40ft of barrow or ruin, and beyond that only a dim outline of shapes. Torches burn until put out by a character or their surroundings.
- A torch can be lit 3 times before it degrades for good. A lantern can be relit 6 times per oil flask, but takes up more inventory slots.
- Characters without a light source may suffer _panic_ until their situation is remedied.

#### Doors

- Barrow gates, sealed Dwemer doors, and rubble-blocked passages may be locked, stuck, or wholly blocked. Characters can try to force a door open (or wedge one shut) using available resources (a pry bar, iron spikes) or through raw ability.
- The party's marching order determines who is most exposed to whatever lies beyond a door.
- A character can detect, through careful observation (listening, smelling, peering through a crack), signs of life and other hazards beyond nearby doors and walls.

#### Traps

- A cautious character should be given any and all information that would let them _avoid_ springing a trap — the worn flagstone, the taut wire, the rune carved into the lintel. An unwitting character triggers a trap according to the fiction, or otherwise has a 2-in-6 chance.
- **Traps** can usually be detected by carefully **searching** a room.
- Damage from traps is taken from Attributes (usually **STR** or **DEX**), _not_ from **HP**. Armor can reduce the damage, but only if it would apply (a shield does no good against a poison-dart gas trap or a frost-rune blast).
