A pointcrawl is a way of laying out the wilds as a handful of named places and the routes that join them. Rather than the open, go-anywhere grid of a hexcrawl, a pointcrawl offers a fixed set of paths — this trail over that pass, this road along that river — and quietly sets aside the empty country in between. It keeps the parts of a journey worth playing and discards the parts that are not, which suits the watch-based travel of The Elder Scrolls 2e perfectly.

The setting tools elsewhere in this book assume a pointcrawl by default. Plenty of older maps don't: some are hex grids, some just print distances in miles or league-marks between towns. That's no obstacle. Almost any map of a stretch of Skyrim can be turned into a pointcrawl with a little work.
