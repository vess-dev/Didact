A venom — a frostbite spider's bite, a poisoned blade, a doctored cup — calls for a **STR save**; on a failure it deals **1d6 STR** (a creature's entry may say otherwise). A fang or a coated blade delivers its venom only when the attack deals damage after Armor; a blow the armor turns carries nothing. And a creature's own venom takes hold once: unless its entry says otherwise, a victim saves against it only the first time that creature hits it in a fight. (A fresh dose smeared on a blade is a fresh venom.)

A **lingering** venom keeps working after that first loss: at the start of each of the victim's next three turns it saves again, losing **1 more STR** on each failure. A successful save ends the venom early, and so does an antitoxin, a Cure Poison draught, or the right Restoration.

Inhaled toxins — a gas-trap's vapor, swamp damp, the spores of certain fungal caverns, alchemical smoke — deal **1 WIL** per round of exposure, and a **WIL save** is required to avoid hallucination while breathing them.

> Deep in a webbed barrow a **frostbite spider** drops from the ceiling and sinks its fangs in. The Dawnguard hunter fails his STR save and loses **1d4 STR** — and the spider's venom lingers, so he saves again at the start of each of his next three turns, another point of STR going cold with every failure, until he shakes it off, the venom runs its course, or someone forces an antitoxin down his throat.
