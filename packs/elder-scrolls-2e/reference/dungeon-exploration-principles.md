- **Name the danger before it bites.** Tell the party whether the cracked ice overhead looks settled or about to drop, whether the oil slick underfoot is fresh, whether the lever is rusted or oiled. Players make good decisions only when they can see the trade-offs you see.
- **Re-anchor them often.** State where each character is standing, what their senses are picking up, and what stands between them and the exits. People forget the map; reminding them is your job, not a penalty.
- **Lead with what matters.** Describe the obvious threats and any NPCs first, then the one or two memorable objects that define the room. Save the wall textures for when someone searches.
- **Report, don't direct.** Tell them what is happening in front of them right now. Don't narrate their fear, their awe, or their conclusions — let them arrive at those.
- **Seed what's ahead.** Let cold drafts, dripping water, distant chanting, the reek of carrion, or a downward slope hint at the next stretch before they reach it.
- **Keep the cycle legible.** Make sure everyone knows whose action is being resolved, who has already gone, and that all actions in a cycle land at once. Treat it like combat's cousin.
- **Charge them in time.** Lingering in one room, rushing through carelessly, making noise, or crossing into a new area all risk a roll on the Dungeon Events table. Say so out loud before they commit, so the cost is a choice and not an ambush.
