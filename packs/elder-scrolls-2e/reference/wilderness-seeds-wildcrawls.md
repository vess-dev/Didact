A wildcrawl is a light method for exploring a broad stretch of Skyrim's wilds — the open tundra, a pine wood, the crags of the Reach, an ice field, or the ash of Solstheim — laid out as branching trails strung between scattered points of interest. It shares much with a tomb or ruin delve, but trades stone walls and low ceilings for the sprawling, non-linear shape of open country. In place of rooms, a point of interest is a clearing, a stand of barren pines, a frozen tarn, a steam vent, or any sharp change in the land. Unlike a barrow, though, the Wilds let a party cut corners: leave the trail, ford the river, or climb a ridge for a wider view — each at a price.

### Exploring the Wilds

- Run the **Dungeon Exploration** procedures from the Player's Guide, but in place of the **Dungeon Events** table, roll on the **Wilderness Events** table.
- When the party leaves the trail to push through open country, the Warden should name the price up front: extra watches, the toil involved, any gear it will take (climbing irons, a boat, snowshoes), and any plain dangers in view. Striking off-trail also forces an immediate roll on the **Wilderness Events** table.
- Points in a wildcrawl sit close together. Following clear, well-trodden trails, a small wild (10–15 POIs) can be crossed in a single **watch**.
- Lean on the fiction and good sense to judge how much daylight remains, whether a safe place to rest is at hand, and what the snow, dark, and cold are hiding when the sun goes down.
