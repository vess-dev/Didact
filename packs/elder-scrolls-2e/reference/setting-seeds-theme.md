### Factions

A region is usually held in the grip of one or more factions, each with its own **Advantages**, **Agenda**, and **Obstacles**. Every faction should have one or more representatives — its **Agents** — charged with seeing the agenda through. A small region with few POIs wants only 1–2 factions; a sprawling one might field as many as 5–6.

Agents are often each responsible for a single goal in the faction's agenda. A large faction may field several agents, one per goal. An agent may carry private motives that cut against the faction's aim — a seam a clever party can pry open for its own profit.

#### Advantages

Factions hold advantages that help them pursue their agenda: their influence, materials, wealth, and other singular assets. Factions lean on these advantages as hard as they can, and work without rest to gain _more_.

#### Agendas

Factions pursue their agendas on their own, drawing the party in only when the party's help would carry the agenda forward. An agenda is a chain of 3–5 goals building toward one clear objective. The goals are _progressive_, each resting on the success (or failure) of the one before.

Each goal should aim at securing a distinct advantage that opens the way to the next. At least one goal must confront the faction's chief obstacle — perhaps the first, perhaps the last. Every agenda has at least one obstacle barring its completion. Further obstacles can rise through faction Actions (below) or through the turns of the fiction.

Completing a faction's agenda should be a real event — one that may reshape the political or natural face of the region.

> Let the region map keep changing to mark the work of the factions as they advance their agendas and gather more advantages.

#### Example Faction

##### Type

- **Cultists**: _The Ashen Circle_. A coven of Reachmen who keep to the high crags, feared by every village below.

##### Advantages

- **Magic**: A _hagraven matriarch_ holds the coven's rites. None below the crags will face her willingly.
- **Conviction**: The Circle believe the Reach was stolen from them, and that the spirits owe them its return.

##### Agents

- Módr the Briarheart, a warrior with a heart of bound brambles in his chest. WIL 15.

##### Agenda

- Hire (or coerce) outsiders to recover an old totem of the Old Gods from a flooded barrow.
- Use the totem to bind the spirits of the Reach to the coven's will.
- Drive every Nord settler from the valley and crown the matriarch over a free Reach.

##### Obstacle

The hagraven has been warned that a Forsworn rival means to take her place at the totem's unveiling.

#### Faction Rules

- By default, factions act apart from what the party does. If the PCs do nothing, the faction still moves to reach its aims.
- The **Warden** leans on the fiction to judge whether a faction is poised to advance its agenda.
- Some goals are time-sensitive or hinge on explicit conditions. At other times it is fitter to add a new goal after a major event, an alliance, or fresh friction between factions.
- Whenever a faction is poised to advance a goal, roll a **d6** on the Faction Actions table.
- If two factions are _opposed_, the faction _most at risk_ makes a WIL save using its highest-ranking agent's score. On a fail, it does not roll on the Faction Actions table this time.

#### Faction Actions

| d6 | Consequence | Impact |
| ------ | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1**  | **Failure**       | A new **Obstacle** is introduced, and an **Advantage** is lost. |
| **2**  | **Setback**       | An **Advantage** is lost.                                       |
| **3**  | **Status Quo**    | Nothing is gained, but nothing is lost.                         |
| **4**  | **Mixed Success** | A **goal** is achieved, but an **Advantage** is lost.           |
| **5**  | **Success**       | A **goal** is achieved, and no **Advantages** are lost.         |
| **6**  | **Major Success** | A **goal** is achieved, and a new **Advantage** is found.       |

> The party's deeds can always override the need for a **faction** action, or shift the roll's outcome one way or the other.

### Topography

A region's terrain and natural features are an essential part of any map, shaping its history, resources, settlements, culture, and more.

#### Landmarks

**Landmarks** are unique, visible POIs that double as bearings: a word wall on a windswept ridge, a leaning Dwemer tower, a standing stone, a dragon's burial mound. They help travelers fix their place, and stand out _especially_ from high ground or a clear viewpoint. Mark a landmark _on the map with a triangle_.

#### Draw Terrain

1. Roll **1d6**, then roll that many dice onto the map. Set aside any dice that roll off the page.
2. Note the face-up result of each die, then use the Terrain Die Drop Table to mark the terrain type on the map. A die showing a 4, for instance, marks Tough terrain.
3. Draw long, winding lines across the page, carving the map up around each die.
4. Roll on the appropriate table for each result, writing the terrain and landmark on the map.
   - Replace the die with a triangle, writing the landmark beside it.
   - Roll any dice that fell off the page, repeating the last step until none remain.
   - If a stretch of map still wants terrain, roll a die anywhere you like and read the face-up result as before.

#### Rivers, Seas, and Lakes

Every region needs a water source — where else would the locals drink, or hear word from the rest of the Realm?

- To make a waterway, draw a wandering line from the highest ground (a mountain, a glacier) to the lowest (a river valley, the coast).
- Where the river crosses higher ground, it may pool into a **lake**. If your map has a sea (Skyrim's whole north coast opens onto the Sea of Ghosts), the river may end there. Otherwise it runs off the edge of the map.

### Weather

Weather presses hard on the people, culture, and terrain of a region. It dictates what the party must wear, what tools they must pack, and how brutal the journey will be — and in Skyrim it can kill the unready outright. To set a region's typical weather, roll on the Seasonal Weather table once for each season. You may also roll on the Unusual Weather table around a notable landmark or terrain.
