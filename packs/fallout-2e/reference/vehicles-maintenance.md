Vehicles do **not** heal by themselves. A damaged vehicle is a broken thing full of bent metal and leaking fluid, and a mechanic has to put it back together. Frame HP does not restore with rest; only with a workshop, scrap, and time.

- **Field patch** (no station). A PC or mechanic in the field can spend **one full Wilderness Action**, **1 Scrap**, and **25 caps** worth of junk to restore **1 Frame HP**. Maximum of 1 field patch per watch, and field patches cannot exceed half the vehicle's max Frame HP — the frame stays kinked until a real workshop gets at it.
- **Workshop repair**. At a settlement or outpost with a **Workshop / Scrap Bench**, a mechanic spends a **Downtime Action**, **2 Scrap**, and **1d6 × 20 caps** per point of Frame HP restored. A PC who isn't a Tinker, and has no hired mechanic, may still do the work, but the caps cost doubles.
- **Frame rebuild**. A *broken* vehicle (0 Frame HP over several hits — see **Crashes**) needs a full rebuild: **two Downtime Actions**, **10 Scrap**, and **1d6 × 100 caps**, plus a WIL save from the mechanic — on a failure, the rebuild works but the vehicle's max Frame HP is permanently reduced by 1 until a proper pre-war parts cache is found.
- **Rad storm exposure**. A vehicle left in the open through a rad storm takes **1 Frame HP** damage automatically and may need a Die of Fate roll to start.
- **Settlement garage**. A vehicle garaged in a settlement with a **Power Generator** and a **Workshop** restores **1 Frame HP per week** at no scrap cost, while the party is away. This does not stack with active repair — the garage is maintenance, not a full rebuild.

A fully functional vehicle with one scratch on the hood is still a fully functional vehicle. A vehicle at half Frame HP or less is visibly rough — the engine stutters, a wheel wobbles, something inside smells burned. The Overseer should describe it.
