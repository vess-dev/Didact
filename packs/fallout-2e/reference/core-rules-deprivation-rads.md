- A character missing a basic need (food, clean water, sleep) is **Deprived**. While Deprived, they cannot recover HP or Attributes, or clear Rads or Fatigue. A character Deprived for more than a day takes **1 Fatigue** for each day it lasts.
- **Rads** fill inventory slots. Each Rad is one slot, lasting until cleared by RadAway, a medical clinic, a settlement's Industrial Water Purifier, or a specific chem, perk, relic, or background ability. **Sleep and ordinary rest do not clear Rads.**
- Characters gain Rads from: exposure to hot zones, drinking dirty water, handling hot relics or raw fusion cells, long marches through irradiated ruins, rad storms and rad-rain, or Overseer fiat from the fiction. Ordinary hardship — cold, heat, hunger, a forced march, a night without sleep — is Fatigue or Deprivation, never Rads.
- A character forced to add a Rad who has no free slots must drop an item to make room.
- Ten slots full = 0 HP. A maxed-out pack breaks you.
- **NPCs and creatures do not track Rads.** When an effect would give a Rad to a rad-susceptible NPC (a gamma gun, a radium rifle, a glowing one's touch), it loses **1 STR** instead; that loss does not by itself force a Critical Damage save. Ghouls, robots, super mutants, and other rad-immune things ignore it.

### Rad Scars & Ghoulification

Rads clear. Rad *Scars* don't.

- Any time a PC takes a Rad, **save against a Rad Scar**: roll 1d20. The target is **20 minus the PC's current Rad count** (including the one just taken). When several Rads arrive at once, add them all, then roll one save per Rad against the new total. Roll equal to or under to resist. Roll higher and the PC gains a **Rad Scar** — a permanent physical change from prolonged exposure. The more Rads you carry, the harder the save: at 1 Rad you resist on a 1–19, at 3 Rads on a 1–17, at 5 Rads on a 1–15, at 10 Rads on a 1–10. Clear Rads fast, or pay.
- When you gain a Rad Scar, roll 1d20 on the Rad Scars table (see the **Rad Scars** chapter): 1–10 is a **Minor** Rad Scar, 11–20 is a **Major** Rad Scar. Roll once and keep it.
- A PC who accumulates their **third Rad Scar** is no longer fully human. At the next long rest, they roll 1d6:
  - **1–3**: They *ghoulify*. Their background converts to **Ghoul** (keep attributes, HP, perks, gear). Their face and skin change. They stop rolling for Rad Scars, though Rads still fill their slots (see **Playing Unusual PCs → Ghoul PCs**). They may re-roll Bonds if they wish.
  - **4–5**: They *resist*. The transformation stalls. One of their Rad Scars may be re-rolled at the Overseer's option, but a new one will come. Flag this PC — the next one sticks.
  - **6**: They *break bad*. The PC becomes a **feral ghoul** under the Overseer's control. The player makes a new character. The old one may be fought later.
- A PC who *wants* to become a ghoul may voluntarily skip the 1d6 roll and choose result 1 instead, once they have at least two Rad Scars.

A ghoul PC is already ghoulified. They do not accumulate Rad Scars, but a ghoul who takes too many Rads can still **go feral** — if a ghoul PC spends a full day at six or more Rads without clearing any, roll 1d20 at the next watch. On a 20, the player loses the character to ferality.
