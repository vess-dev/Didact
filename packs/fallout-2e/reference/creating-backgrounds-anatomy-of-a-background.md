### The Pitch

Open every Background with a short second-person **blockquote** — a 2–5 sentence italic "you…" voice that drops the player straight into the role. It is the first thing they read, and it should set the tone before a single die is rolled. Speak to the character directly: what have they seen, what do they carry, what do they believe? A Wasteland Medic's pitch tells her which wound kills in an hour and which in a week; a Raider's admits he hasn't told the party everything. Make it vivid and specific, not a résumé.

### Theme

Write a list of adjectives that describe the role. If it's a profession, ask why a person chooses that life out here. If it's something stranger (Ghoul, Synth Runaway, Deathclaw), ask how that strangeness shapes daily life and other people's reactions. Keep inspirational touchstones in mind: films, novels, old modules, the stuff you love about the genre. Let them in.

### Names

Follow the pitch with a **`## Names`** list — about ten sample names that fit the Background's culture and era. A player who doesn't want to invent one can grab a name and start playing in seconds, and the list quietly reinforces the setting: a Vault Dweller's names read differently from a Ghoul's or a Raider's. Mix given names, surnames, and nicknames as the role suggests.

### Starting Gear

Characters are defined by what they carry. Starting gear is the foundation of the Background.

- 3d6 caps, Rations, and a light source. Keep these specific: a chem cook might start with a lantern instead of a torch, a vault dweller with a flashlight.
- Up to four *unique* items that lean into the theme (_petty_ keepsakes don't count against the four). At least one must be a weapon.
- Starting armor is at most 1 Armor. Exceptions are documented in the background itself: robot chassis and deathclaw hide (see **Playing Unusual PCs**), and the rare background built around a signature suit, like the Brotherhood Outcast's power armor.
- Total starting gear should occupy no more than seven slots.

### Tables

Each Background has two **Roll 1d6 tables** of six entries each.

- **Table 1** asks about the past: what happened to you? what forged you? Each result gives a unique item, an ability, or a hook sharp enough to hang a session on — ideally more than one of the three — and hints at what that character has lived through.
- **Table 2** asks about the present: what do you carry now? what do you do? Each result gives a signature item or a usable trick that defines the character in play.

Both tables together should make a single character feel specific. A player rolling Raider should get a result so concrete that five minutes later they already know how their character talks.
