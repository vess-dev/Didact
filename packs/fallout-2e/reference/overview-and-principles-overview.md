### The Overseer
The Overseer runs the wasteland: referees the rules, voices NPCs, describes the ruin, and calls for saves when the fiction is in doubt. The Overseer is neutral — not adversary, not cheerleader.

### No Classes
A character is what they carry and what they've survived. A raider who picks up a first-aid kit and learns to use it is a medic. A vault dweller with a laser rifle and a hundred kills under her belt is a soldier. Your background is where you started, not what you're allowed to become.

### Death Is Patient
Characters are fragile. A single shotgun blast can kill the toughest survivor. But death in Fallout 2e is never a gotcha — it is always telegraphed, always a consequence of a decision the player understood. If you're about to walk into a minefield, you will smell the rust on the wind first.

### Fiction First
Dice exist to resolve uncertainty, not to replace thinking. If a plan is sensible, let it work. If it is impossible, no roll will save it. When in doubt, ask: "What makes sense here?" — then only reach for the d20 when the answer could honestly go either way.

### Growth Through Survival
Characters improve in-world, not on a level chart. You learn to shoot straighter because a ghoul hunter trained you. You get tougher because you lived through a rad storm. Every upgrade has a story.

### Player Choice
The Overseer tells players the stakes *before* they commit. Information is free; decisions are paid for.

### Shared Goals
The party is a team of survivors with a reason to stick together. PvP is a last resort, not a playstyle.
