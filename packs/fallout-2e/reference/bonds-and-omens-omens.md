An Omen is a larger, shared hook that shapes the whole campaign. The youngest character at the table rolls it, and the table hears it aloud. Unlike a Bond, an Omen is not personal — it is environmental.

An Omen is a *setting move*. It promises something is happening in the world beneath the ordinary business of survival. It is the Overseer's excuse to drop weird details into unrelated scenes:
- A radio in a settlement plays the same song twice.
- A child in a farming town speaks a name nobody in that town has ever used.
- Birds fall dead on the road between two points of interest.

An Omen does not have to be resolved in one session, or ten. It is the slow pulse of the campaign. Its only requirement is that it must be *consistently felt* — every session, the players should see one small piece of evidence that the Omen is still unfolding.
