- A magazine takes **one inventory slot** while carried.
- Reading a magazine takes **one turn** in a safe, quiet space (a few minutes of concentrated reading). It cannot be done in combat or while Deprived.
- A magazine is **consumed when read**, and **only one character can ever benefit from a given physical copy.** Whoever reads it first takes the perk — the magazine ends up torn, dog-eared, annotated to death, or memorized and used as tinder. If a second character wants the same perk, they must find a different copy of the same issue.
- Perks gained from magazines do not require a Pip-Boy. They become part of the reader's trained instincts.

### Perk Slots & the Swap Rule

A character's mind and body can only hold so many trained instincts at once. Each PC has **five Perk Slots**, flat.

- When a PC reads a magazine and all five slots are full, they **must swap**: choose one existing perk to forget, and replace it with the new one. The magazine is still consumed. The old perk is gone until the PC finds and reads a fresh copy of the magazine that grants it.
- A PC may *refuse* to read a magazine. If they do, the magazine is not consumed — they may give it to another PC, carry it, or sell it. A magazine is only consumed when actually read.
- Swapping is a conscious act at the moment of reading; a PC cannot come back later and rearrange their slots at will.

The swap rule means old magazines still have value even after a character is "full" — they can always upgrade their loadout — but forces genuine choice. A character's perk loadout becomes a slow-evolving portrait of the wasteland they have walked through.

### Rarity

Some issues are marked _rare_ — expect to see fewer of them in a campaign. Overseers should place them deliberately, usually as rewards for significant risk or as the treasure of a specific NPC.
