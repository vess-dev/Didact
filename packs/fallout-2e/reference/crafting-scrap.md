**Scrap** is the abstracted currency of pre-war parts — circuit boards, copper wire, aluminum cans, screws, springs, gun steel, duct tape, ceramic plate. It is what you actually *build* with. Caps buy food and labor; Scrap builds a suppressor.

- Scrap is tracked as a number on the character sheet or party sheet. Each **1 Scrap** represents a handful of usable parts.
- Scrap is **bulky in quantity**. Every **5 Scrap** takes one inventory slot, rounded up. A settlement **Scrap Heap** (see Overseer's Guide → Settlements) stores scrap freely.
- Scrap is gained by:
  - **Scavenging** a ruin, wreck, or robot corpse — typically **1d6 Scrap** per location, Overseer's call.
  - **Breaking down** a weapon or piece of armor at a Workshop (1 Downtime Action): recover **half** the item's Scrap cost, rounded down (bought gear with no Scrap cost yields 1 Scrap, or 2 if _bulky_).
  - **Settlements** with a Scrap Heap: the party draws **1d6 Scrap** each time they visit, on top of caps.
  - **Wrecked vehicles**: 2d6 Scrap from the frame, plus whatever fuel and parts survive.
- Scrap cannot simply be bought. A trader will sometimes **buy** scrap at roughly **10 caps per unit**, or **sell** at **25 caps per unit** when they have it — which is rarely.

Scrap exists because caps alone shouldn't turn into a rifle. The wasteland has caps. The wasteland does not have screws.
