| Difficulty | Terrain | Penalty | Factors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Easy** | Settled plains, valleys, pre-war farmland | none | safe rest, travelers, good visibility |
| **Tough** | Ruined suburbs, broken highways, badlands | +1 watch | feral hounds, scorched ground, collapsed pavement, raider traps |
| **Perilous** | Glowing craters, irradiated swamp, deep ruins, Deathclaw range | +2 watches | hot zones, sucking mud, sudden storms, hostile fauna, no landmarks |

### Weather

Each day roll on the season table. Two **Extreme** results in a row → **Catastrophic**: a squall becomes a hurricane, a sandstorm becomes a firestorm, a rad storm scours the whole region.

| d6 | Spring | Summer | Fall | Winter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear | Hot | Fair | Fair |
| 2 | Fair | Hot | Fair | Cold |
| 3 | Fair | Fair | Cold | Harsh |
| 4 | Gusty | Dusty | Harsh | Harsh |
| 5 | Stormy | Stormy | Stormy | Extreme |
| 6 | Extreme | Extreme | Extreme | Extreme |

### Weather Effects

| Weather | Effect | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear/Fair** | Good travel. | Sunny, overcast, mild |
| **Cold/Hot/Gusty** | No effect if sheltered or dressed for it; prolonged exposure without shelter is a hazard — see Hazards. | Breezy, warm, chilly |
| **Dusty/Harsh** | Add a Fatigue *or* add +1 watch. | Dust storm, thunder, driving rain |
| **Stormy** | Add a Fatigue *or* +1 watch. Terrain difficulty up one step. | Lightning, flooding, rad-rain |
| **Extreme** | Add a Fatigue *and* +1 watch. Terrain difficulty up one step. | Blizzard, firestorm, rad storm |
| **Catastrophic** | Travel impossible for most parties. | Full rad storm, atomic squall, tidal surge |

**Rad weather.** Ordinary weather wears a traveler down; it does not irradiate them. But a storm that blows in across a hot zone carries the fallout with it. When **Stormy** or worse weather hits a region that holds a hot zone — Overseer's call; most Perilous terrain qualifies — it is rad-rain or a rad storm, and the Fatigue it would inflict is a **Rad** instead. The Overseer says so before the party chooses between the strain and the extra watch.

### Wasteland Exploration Cycle

1. Overseer describes the current point, weather, terrain, and party condition. Party sets a course.
2. Each PC picks one Wilderness Action. Overseer narrates results and rolls on the Wasteland Events table. Party reacts.
3. Resource use and conditions are tracked. Repeat.

### Wasteland Events

| d6 | | |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | **Encounter** | Roll on the terrain encounter table. Use Reactions. |
| 2 | **Sign** | A clue: spent casings, a cold campfire, a distant plume of smoke, an old Pip-Boy recording. |
| 3 | **Environment** | Weather or terrain shifts. |
| 4 | **Loss** | A resource cost: a wheel breaks, a water flask cracks, a horse throws a shoe, an ambush forces a detour. |
| 5 | **Exhaustion** | A barrier: rockfall, deep mud, a collapsed bridge. Spend another Wilderness Action, add a Fatigue, or both. |
| 6 | **Discovery** | Food, caps, fuel, a secret. The Overseer may reveal the primary feature of the next point instead. |

### Wasteland Elements

#### Night
- Traveling at night is slower and more dangerous. Overseer rolls events **twice**.
- Some terrain is easier by night (deserts, open salts). Overseer balances.

#### Sleep
- The last watch is usually **Make Camp**.
- Anything worse than a minor interruption ruins sleep.
- Skip sleep? Each PC adds a Fatigue and becomes Deprived. Terrain difficulty goes up one step.

#### Light
- Flashlights, torches, lanterns light 40 ft. Beyond that — shapes.
- No light = panic risk.
- Weather can douse flame easily.

### Wilderness Actions

#### Travel
- Travel begins. Obvious nearby locations and terrain are revealed. Usually taken by the whole party.
- Roll 1d6 to check for getting lost (compared to path difficulty).
- If lost, you may need to spend another action to find your way. Otherwise you reach the next point.

#### Explore
- Party members search a wide area — scouting, investigating a ruin from outside, picking a path.
- A Location (shelter, village, lab, cave) or Feature (dry well, crashed Vertibird, old checkpoint) is revealed.
- You still need **Travel** to leave.

#### Supply
- Hunt, forage, scavenge for food: 1d4 Rations (3 uses each). More bodies on the action → bigger die (1d6, 1d8, up to 1d12).
- A known settlement on the route can be resupplied at for caps and a full watch.

#### Make Camp
- Party stops. Each PC (and their mounts) consumes a Ration.
- A watch rotation is set. Small parties may need to sleep unguarded or split shifts across nights.
- A full night's safe sleep clears all Fatigue. It does **not** clear Rads — those require RadAway or other specialized treatment.
