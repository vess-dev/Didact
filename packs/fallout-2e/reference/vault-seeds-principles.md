---
- **Purpose**. What was the place built for, and what's it being used for *now*? Show the history through details. A vault built as an agricultural experiment will still have dead greenhouses; a pre-war bank will still have the faded name on the door.
- **Levels**. Deeper means more dangerous. Each level should force the party to weigh risk against reward.
- **Layout**. Multiple entrances and exits, multiple routes, choke points, hiding places. Give rooms memorable architecture.
- **Verticality**. Use collapsed floors, vent shafts, maintenance ladders, flooded sublevels.
- **Secrets**. At least one hidden area that most parties will miss. Don't hide load-bearing information there.
- **Variety**. Vary room shape, size, function. Architecture should tell the story.
- **Treasure**. Caps are boring alone. Mix in knowledge, allies, trade goods, and Relics. Great loot is specific to the place.
- **Challenges**. Let players solve problems. Make dangers obvious. Avoid unwinnable scenarios. Monsters and NPCs are part of the story, not obstacles.
- **NPCs**. Give them wants and agendas. Let them *live* in the place. Let them negotiate, even in combat.
- **Factions**. More than one group should want something here, and they should not agree.
- **Puzzles**. Clear goals, visible challenge, hidden solution, multiple paths. Built-in clues.
- **Maps**. Put the boring layout stuff on the map, not in prose. Number rooms.
- **Room Keys**. Short, specific, no feelings. Name every room.
- **Encounters**. Wandering events keep the ruin alive.
