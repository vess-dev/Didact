Wasteland travel in Fallout 2e is abstract. The minutiae of every mile are glossed away, but the system still lets players make meaningful choices about route, pace, rest, and resources.

### Travel Example

The party is escorting a wounded settler from the farm-town of **Dim Hollow** down to the clinic in **Five-Corners**. The old highway is faster to follow, but raiders hold the Overpass, so they take the drovers' trail a local showed them (see the map in **Pointcrawls**): a Short distance (+1 watch) along a beaten trail (+1 watch) across Easy terrain (no penalty). In fair weather that is two watches of travel — out after breakfast, at the clinic by dusk.

> At dawn, the Overseer rolls for weather. It's **Harsh** — a driving rain. The party must each add a Fatigue or add an extra watch to the journey. There is no hot zone upwind, so it is only rain, not rad-rain. They choose the extra watch; better to walk slow than arrive spent. The trip is now three watches. Each time the party Travels, they roll 1d6 for lost (a trail is 2-in-6), and after every watch the Overseer rolls on the Wasteland Events table.

#### Day One / Morning

- The party takes the **Travel** action. They roll 1d6 — a 5, not lost.
- **Events**: **Sign**. A scouts' cairn beside the trail, fresh. Three small stones stacked in a specific pattern — the caravaneer in the party recognizes it as Caravaneer code for *dogs ahead*. The party slows and loosens weapons.

#### Day One / Afternoon

- **Travel** again. 1d6 — a 2: they lose the trail in the rain. The watch is spent and they are no closer; they must spend another Wilderness Action finding their way before they can go on.
- **Events**: **Environment**. The rain hardens. Visibility drops to thirty feet.

#### Day One / Night

- The party could spend the night watch casting about for the trail, but skipping sleep means a Fatigue each, Deprivation, and Tough terrain in the morning. They **Make Camp** instead. Each PC consumes a Ration. The Ranger takes first watch. The settler is wrapped in every blanket they have.
- **Events**: **Discovery**. The rain slackens around midnight, and the Ranger on watch picks out Five-Corners' lanterns to the south. The Overseer rules that with a bearing like that the party is no longer lost — no extra action needed at first light. They sleep, and wake with no Fatigue to show for the day.

#### Day Two / Morning

- The Overseer rolls fresh weather: **Fair**. **Travel** — 1d6, a 6, not lost. Two of the three watches are behind them.
- **Events**: **Exhaustion**. A collapsed footbridge they didn't know about. They can spend another Wilderness Action going around, or add a Fatigue each and ford the creek with the settler on their shoulders. They take the Fatigue.

#### Day Two / Afternoon

- **Travel**. 1d6 — a 4, not lost. The third watch of travel brings them into Five-Corners at dusk, a full day later than planned.
- **Events**: **Sign**. Fresh brahmin tracks, all heading the same way they are: a caravan beat them in, and the clinic will be busy.

> The party could have pushed through the first night, but with a wounded passenger and the visibility as bad as it was, the risk of another event roll — and the Fatigue of a sleepless march — was not worth the saved time. A two-watch trip took five watches, one of them asleep. That is ordinary, and it is why parties carry spare Rations.
