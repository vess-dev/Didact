A built or city home with room can take in children — **up to two** (the Warden may raise this). Marriage is not required.

- **Eligibility.** The home needs a place for a child: **a bed and a container (chest) per child**. In Settlements terms, that means at least one **Cottages (beds)** slot built (or spare beds in the main hall) — a child sleeps in the beds that slot provides, and without a bed a child will not stay, just like any settler.
- **Where the children come from.** Once the party has done a kindness for orphans — most cleanly, after the Dark Brotherhood opener *The Black Sacrament*, where they may avenge the orphans of **Honorhall Orphanage** in Riften — a courier brings word that they may adopt. The matron **Constance Michel** at **Honorhall** places children with good homes; homeless orphans also turn up begging in the streets of the cities (Whiterun, Windhelm, Dawnstar, a farm outside Solitude). A child may be invited home with a kind word and a roof to offer.
- **Family life.** Adopted children call the party Mama or Papa. The household may play games with them, give gifts and a small allowance, and let a child keep a pet. **Sleeping under the same roof as your child grants a parent's blessing** (this frame's one addition to Settlements): once per day, a PC who spent the previous night at home with family present may re-roll a failed WIL save against fear or despair — a steader rests easier with kin near.
- **On the page.** A child needs a bed, so adoption presumes a built **Cottages (beds)** slot; that slot, not the child, raises **Population**. The children give the steading a heart worth defending. They are not stat-blocked combatants; in a raid, they are what the party is *protecting* — see the troubles below.

> **Adoption is a reward, not a chore.** Keep it light and warm. The mechanical weight is a bed in a Cottages slot and a once-per-day re-roll; the real payoff is fiction — a home with someone in it.
