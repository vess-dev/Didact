The legends say a **Dragonborn** — *Dovahkiin* — will rise when the World-Eater returns: a mortal born with the soul of a dragon, who devours the souls of the slain dovah and speaks their language as a weapon, the **Thu'um**.

This game has **no Shout mechanic for player characters.** The Voice is a force of the world, not a spell on a character sheet — it belongs to the dragons, to **Alduin**, to **Paarthurnax** and the **Greybeards** atop High Hrothgar, and to the ancient Tongues of legend. When a quest turns on a Shout (the *Dragonrend* that cast Alduin down; the *Bend Will* that frees Solstheim's stones), treat it as a **story device**: a vision read from an Elder Scroll, a power loaned for one terrible moment by a Daedric Prince or a Black Book, a ritual the party *enables* but does not wield.

Two clean ways to handle the prophecy at your table:

- **The party are mortal heroes, and the Last Dragonborn is an NPC** — an ally they fight beside, protect, or are sent to find. The world still turns; the heroes still matter.
- **A player character is touched by Akatosh.** If you want a PC to *be* the Dragonborn, make it a matter of **fiction and Growth, not rules**: standing in a slain dragon's dissolving soul-fire is a Growth trigger like any other. The Warden might grant a single narrow, permanent boon — re-roll one Attribute and keep the higher result, a save that becomes automatic in one narrow situation (a dragon's roar never sends you fleeing), or a mark the world can feel (the barrow-dead know you for what you are) — earned and specific, never a bonus to saves and never a Thu'um subsystem. The character is legend-touched; they do not get a list of Shouts.

Either way, dragons still breathe fire and frost (see the Bestiary), the Greybeards still summon, and Alduin is still the end of the world. Only the players' toolkit stays pure Cairn.
