The chapters that follow are **frames, not railroads** — toolkits for the great stories of Skyrim in the year 4E 201, written so a Warden can drop a party into any of them and run an evening, or weave several into a years-long sandbox. Most frames run in the same order: the **Pitch** and the **Stakes**; the **Tone & Touchstones**; the party's **Arrival**; the **Plot Beats** with their branch points; **The Place**; the **Cast** (with stat blocks where it comes to blows); the **Clocks** and **Threats**; the **Rewards** worth chasing; and the **Climax**. A frame skips what it doesn't need and adds a section of its own where the story turns on something special. None of it is fixed: cut a beat, swap an NPC, let the dice and the players carry it somewhere you didn't plan. The point is to have enough to improvise from, not a script to follow.

Everything here is set in **Skyrim and its add-ons** — the base province and the *Dawnguard*, *Dragonborn*, and *Hearthfire* tales. The wider continent, and the long shadow of the Oblivion Crisis two centuries past, live at the edges — a Thalmor dispatch, a Mythic Dawn relic in a necromancer's hoard, a Redguard sellsword, an East Empire ship — but the playable ground is Skyrim.
