Two reckonings: a question of conscience before the end, and the last battle itself.

### The Paarthurnax Question (after Beat 8, before Skuldafn)

The Blades will demand the death of **Paarthurnax** for crimes committed at the dawn of history. The party chooses:

- **Refuse.** Keep faith with the ancient dragon. The Blades withdraw their support and will not lift a hand for the rest of the arc — but **Paarthurnax stays their ally**, and the table keeps its mentor. Most parties refuse; let the cost (no Blades) and the reward (the old dragon's counsel, now and after) both be real.
- **Obey.** Climb to the summit and kill Paarthurnax (use his stat block above). The Blades resume their support — but give **no reward**, and the party has lost its mentor for good. A grim, lawful choice; do not punish it with Warden disapproval, but let the empty summit speak.

### Dragonslayer — the last battle in Sovngarde

Alduin waits in the misted Shadowed Vale of Sovngarde, feeding on the souls he has snared. The party has crossed the bridge, won the Hall of Valor, and gathered their allies. Resolutions turn on *who stands with them*:

- **With the heroes.** The three ancient heroes' Voices tear away Alduin's mist and pin the World-Eater to the ground (Dragonrend, supplied by allies — never by a PC). While they Shout he is no longer beyond reach: he cannot fly, and he can be hurt. The party closes — **Dragonbane** biting deep, the heroes' blades beside their own — and lands the killing blow. Alduin is destroyed; his soul cannot be absorbed (he is no ordinary dragon). The world is saved; the mist clears; the snared souls go free. **Tsun** returns the party to the Throat of the World as legends, **Hero of Skyrim** (+3 Renown). This is the intended ending.
- **Without the heroes.** If the party somehow reaches Alduin without recruiting Gormlaith, Hakon, and Felldir (they refused Tsun, fled the Hall, or skipped the recruitment), Alduin keeps his mist and his flight — and simply will not stay down. The party cannot win the fight as written; they are driven back to the bridge to regroup and *go recruit the heroes*. There is no path to victory that the party walks alone — the whole arc is about gathering allies, and the finale enforces it.
- **The cost of the climb.** Sovngarde is the land of the dead, and the party fights at the edge of it. A PC who falls here (0 STR) may, at the Warden's discretion and the table's taste, *remain* in the Hall of Valor among the honored dead — a heroic death that still counts toward the World-Eater's defeat, the survivor(s) carrying the tale home.

When Alduin falls, the Dragon Crisis ends. The party walks back into a Skyrim still at war (or freshly at peace, if they settled it) — scarred, renowned, and free to take up the **Civil War**, the **Companions**, the **College**, the **Thieves Guild**, the **Dark Brotherhood**, a **Daedric** bargain, or the troubles of **Dawnguard** and far **Solstheim**. The dragons that remain are leaderless now; some, like Odahviing, may even be reasoned with. The World-Eater is gone from the world — for this age.
