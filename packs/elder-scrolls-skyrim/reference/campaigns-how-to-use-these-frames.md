- **Pick one, or run many.** Skyrim is a sandbox. A party can chase the Main Quest while moonlighting for the Thieves Guild, building a homestead, and ducking the Civil War — the frames are designed to interleave, and a single character can belong to several factions at once (mind the opposed pairs in Faction Reputation).
- **The Holds are your map.** Use the Warden's Guide gazetteer, The Holds, for travel between frames, and the Setting, Dungeon, and Wilderness Seeds to fill the spaces in between with your own barrows, ruins, and troubles.
- **Scale to the party.** These are Cairn adventures: lethal, fiction-first, and unbalanced on purpose. Telegraph the deadly things (a dragon, a Dwarven Centurion, a master vampire) clearly, and let the players choose their fights. The DLC frames (*Dawnguard*, *Dragonborn*) assume a seasoned, well-equipped band.
- **Rewards are treasure, not a paycheck.** The great prizes — Wuuthrad, the Nightingale gear, Auriel's Bow, a Daedric Artifact — are quest's-end rewards, drawn from the Unique Weapons, Unique Armors, and Relics catalogs; the frames name them and leave their rules to the catalogs. Renown, a Hold's gratitude, a faction rank, and a home of one's own matter just as much.
