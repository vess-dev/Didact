> *Skyrim belongs to the Nords — but a corner of it can belong to you. A stretch of cleared ground, a raised frame, a hearth lit against the dark, a roof over heads you have chosen to call kin.*

Most of these frames send the party charging at a dragon, a guild master, or a god. This one sends them home. **Hearthfire** is the *between-adventures* campaign — the quiet wrapper around the settlement subsystem in the **Warden's Guide → Settlements**. A party that has earned a little standing buys a stretch of Hold land, raises a manor on it plank by plank across their Downtime, hires the folk to keep it, adopts children to fill it, and then discovers — as every Nord steader does — that owning a home in Skyrim means having something worth taking.

This is a lighter, shorter frame than the guild campaigns. There is no central quest and no villain to topple, only a place to make and a handful of troubles that *come with the keys*. Run it slow, between bigger arcs: a session here raising a wing, a session there driving off a giant, a half-session adopting an orphan from Riften. Drop the troubles in when the table wants a fight close to home, or string two or three together if the party wants a homesteading run of their own.

> **This frame does not invent a building economy.** Everything below points back at **Settlements** in the Warden's Guide — the **Population / Defense / Prosperity** dials (each 1–5), the **eight building slots**, the raid rules, the Jarl-and-Civil-War overlay. Building a wing is *filling a building slot*: a **Downtime Action**, a sum in **Septims**, and gathered materials. There is no "Scrap"-style track here and no parallel ledger; if a rule about building or raiding is needed, it lives in Settlements, and this frame only adds the fiction, the plots, the hirelings, the children, and the troubles.
