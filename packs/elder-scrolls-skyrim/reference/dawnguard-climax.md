The prophecy can be made or unmade, and Harkon must die either way. The Bow, the three Scrolls, and a Daughter of Coldharbour are all in play; Harkon shields himself at his blood-altar in the Cathedral of Castle Volkihar, in **Vampire Lord** form, and his ward blunts every mundane blow (attacks against him are _Impaired_) until it is broken (by **Auriel's Bow**, or by Isran's holy light). Three ways it can land:

- **The Dawnguard ending — deny the sun.** Isran's order storms the castle; the party breaks the altar-ward, slays Harkon in his winged form, and the Tyranny of the Sun dies with him. Serana, freed of her father, may renounce her curse (sought through Falion in Morthal) or keep it — her choice, and the campaign's quiet last beat. The Dawnguard hold Castle Volkihar as a sun-lit garrison.
- **The Volkihar ending — the crown was never yours.** The party gathered everything to enact the prophecy, only to find Harkon meant the eternal night for himself alone. They turn on him at his own altar. With his death the prophecy is theirs to enact — or to abandon. A party that *does* dim the sun rules a Skyrim of the undying, hunted forever by what's left of the Dawnguard and every Vigilant alive; a party that walks away keeps the power and spares the world. Garan Marethi (or Serana) holds the castle for the clan.
- **Serana's ending — neither parent.** If Serana's Trust reached 5, give the table a third door: with the Bow in hand and both her parents' schemes in ruins, **Serana** herself ends it — refusing to be the key to anyone's prophecy, destroying the altar, and walking out into the snow beside the party as her own person at last. Harkon dies; the Tyranny of the Sun is unmade not by hunters or monsters but by the daughter both sides tried to use. This is the ending to aim a long campaign toward.

Whatever the resolution, the sun comes up over the Sea of Ghosts — or it doesn't — and a four-thousand-year-old quarrel is finally, bloodily, over.
