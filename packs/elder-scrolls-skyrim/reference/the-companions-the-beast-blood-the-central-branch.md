Beat 3 forces the choice, and the campaign's ending turns on it. Run it straight, with full weight — this is a *story* transformation, the **Werewolf** of Playing Unusual PCs, never a Shout.

- **Drink (embrace the wolf).** A PC who drinks at the Underforge becomes **beast-blooded** per the Werewolf rules: Beast Blood (immune to mundane disease), the once-per-day **Change** into **Beast Form** (claws d6+d6, bite d8, +1 Armor from hide, the Howl of Terror), hunter's senses — paid for with **silver weapons _Enhanced_ against you**, no gear or speech while Transformed, the fresh kill after the hunt or be Deprived, and Reactions a step worse where the curse is known (but **Companions +1**). The party gains a fierce edge and a fierce cost.
- **Refuse (or hold back).** A PC may decline the cup. They stay a mortal Companion, fully kin to the Circle, simply unblooded — Aela thinks less of their nerve, but the bond holds. The party need not all choose the same way.
- **Seek the cure later.** From beat 5 on, a beast-blooded PC may want *out*. The cure is the same rite that frees Kodlak — a **Glenmoril witch's head** cast into the **Flame of the Harbinger** at Ysgramor's Tomb. This is why a wise party takes more than one head at the Coven (Beat 5): **each head can cure one beast-blooded person**, Kodlak's ghost included. At Beat 8 (or any later return to the Tomb with a head and the reforged axe), a PC may burn a head to **lose Lycanthropy** — shedding the Edges and the Costs alike, mortal once more. **Aela will refuse the cure** (she loves the wolf); **Farkas and Vilkas** can go either way at the table.

> A werewolf, an unblooded Companion, and a now-cured PC should all have the same agency at the table — different edges, different costs, same say in the story. Put the wolf's gift *and* its price into play about equally; don't let the curse become only a punishment or only a power.
