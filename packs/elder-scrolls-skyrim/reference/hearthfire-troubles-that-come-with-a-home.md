A homestead on the edge of the wild *will* draw trouble — this is the **raid** rule of Settlements (each season, roll a d6; if it beats the steading's Defense, a raid comes; a loss costs **Population −1 and a building destroyed**). The hooks below are the *kinds* of trouble that find a Skyrim steader, each a short scene the Warden drops in. Every attacker uses a stat block from the Bestiary where one exists. Resolve a raid as Settlements directs: party wins → no loss (a hard-won victory may raise Defense by one); party loses or is absent → Population −1 and a building down.

Pick three to five over the life of the steading; do not run them all at once.

### 1 — The build site is raided (bandits, or a giant)

The frame is up, the materials are stacked, and something wants what the party has gathered — or simply objects to a new house on its range. Run as a Settlements **raid** against the half-built steading (low Defense, so likely; spot it early only with a Watchtower or Watch-beacon).

**Bandits** (a band that has been watching the road; a _detachment_ of four or more) may come for it. Or, for a Heljarchen or Lakeview site near the foothills, a lone **Giant** wanders down and takes exception to the noise. A giant is a _detachment_ and a deadly one — better lured off, fenced out (a palisade *helps*), or paid in mammoth cheese than fought head-on.

> **Stakes:** if the party loses or is away, the raid costs **Population −1 and a building** (Settlements) — here, half-finished work torn down or a hired hand killed. The cellar's worth of stacked logs and stone is the obvious target.

#### Bandit (from the Bestiary)

4 HP, 1 Armor, 12 STR, 12 DEX, 9 WIL, iron sword or war axe (d8), or hunting bow (d6)

- The most common cutthroat in Skyrim, lairing in caves, forts, and ruined towers. Four or more fight as a _detachment_.
- A detachment travels with a leader in heavier mail (2 Armor) wielding a greatsword (d10) or a crossbow (d8).
- **Loyal**: test Morale against the leader's WIL (13). If the leader falls, the rest flee.

#### Giant (from the Bestiary)

18 HP, 1 Armor, 18 STR, 12 DEX, 8 WIL, club (d12), _detachment_

- A towering, primitive herdsman of the mammoths, daubed in war-paint, peaceful until disturbed. Then deadly.
- It will not wade into deep water, and it warns trespassers first — a stamp, a bellow, a raised club.
- **Critical Damage**: the club launches the target into the sky. They do not survive the landing.

### 2 — A skooma den festers nearby

A ruined shack, an old mine drift, or a cave within a watch of the steading has been taken over by **skooma** dealers — Khajiit smugglers or down-and-out men cooking the moon-sugar drug. They bring foot-traffic, theft, and trouble to the road, and the local Jarl will reward the family that cleans it out (good for **standing**). The den's toughs are bandits (above); the danger is the drug itself.

- **Skooma (the hazard):** a dose forces a **STR save or be Deprived** until a full night's rest; there is no addiction track (per the rules — skooma is a one-line danger, not a subsystem). A hired hand or, worse, an older adopted child tempted by the den is a quiet, character-driven hook.
- *Resolution:* clear the den (a short delve), turn the dealers over or run them off, and report to the Steward at the capital. A cleared den shifts the Hold's court one step better, and the Jarl may forgive the party's **Bounty** in that Hold, if they carry one.

### 3 — A hired bard, or a "long-lost relative," turns up

Not every trouble is a fight. Two social hooks for a quiet session:

- **The bard.** A travelling skald asks to take a hearth at the manor — flattering, harmless, and useful: a resident or visiting bard is the Settlements **Bard's Hearth** (_petty_ building), under which the steading's defending detachment **automatically passes its first Morale save per raid**. The catch is the Warden's to invent: the bard owes coin to the wrong people, carries a song someone wants silenced, or is sweet on a member of the household.
- **The long-lost relative.** A stranger arrives claiming kinship — a cousin, an uncle, a half-sibling come to share the family roof. They may be genuine (a warm subplot, and a free hand for the steading) or a **confidence trickster** casing the house to rob it, or worse, a Brotherhood scout (see below). Let the party investigate; reward suspicion *and* trust, depending on the truth the Warden chose.

#### Confidence Trickster ("the relative")

4 HP, 11 STR, 13 DEX, 12 WIL, concealed dagger (d6)

- A smooth-talking grifter playing on a family's good nature; all charm at the table and light fingers after dark.
- Avoids a straight fight; flees or pleads if cornered. May have an accomplice or two (use the Bandit block) waiting to clean the house out once the family sleeps.
- Caught in the act, the trickster drops everything and bolts; the first attack made to stop them is _Enhanced_.

### 4 — The Dark Brotherhood, or a Hold dispute, follows you home

The shadow side of having a fixed address: enemies now know where to find the party.

- **The Brotherhood follows you home.** If the party has crossed the **Dark Brotherhood** (or anyone has paid for the Black Sacrament against them), the assassins do not ambush on the road — they come to the *house*, by night, while the family sleeps. One killer slips the palisade; a watchtower or a wakeful housecarl is the difference between an alarm and a corpse. This is a tense, low-numbers raid: not a war-band, but a single deadly intruder among the people the party loves.
- **A Hold dispute follows you home.** The **Civil War** does not respect property lines (Settlements → *The Jarl and the Civil War*). A steading that flew one banner finds the *other* Jarl cold when the Hold changes hands: billeted soldiers, a punishing tax audit, a levy demand, or a war-party astride a strategic road that decides the manor is a forward post worth burning. Run it as a confrontation (talk the soldiers down, pay the levy, call in a favor) or, at worst, a war-party **raid** (use the **Stormcloak Soldier** or **Imperial Soldier** blocks from the Civil War frame, or reskin **Bandits**). Old favor does not always carry over — which way the party's Hold leans is now *personal*.

#### Dark Brotherhood Assassin

8 HP, 1 Armor, 14 STR, 15 DEX, 12 WIL, ebony dagger (d6) or bow (d6, _bulky_)

- A black-robed killer of the last Sanctuary, striking from the dark and gone before the body is cold. Comes alone or in a pair.
- Opens from stealth — its **first** attack each combat is _Enhanced_. Its blade is poisoned: the first time it wounds a creature in a fight, that creature must pass a STR save or take 1d4 STR. Targets the lord, then the household.
- **Critical Damage**: the stroke finds the throat. The victim cannot cry out, and dies within the hour unless someone reaches them.
