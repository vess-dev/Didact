This frame deliberately keeps the Thu'um out of the players' hands. **Dragonrend is never a Shout the party can spend.** It is:

- **A vision borne by an Elder Scroll.** Read at the Time-Wound (Beat 8), the **Elder Scroll (Dragon)** lets the party *witness* the ancient heroes use Dragonrend against Alduin. This is the "ancient heroes' weapon" — the party enables it, the Warden narrates it, and it works **exactly once**, in that place, to ground Alduin for the Time-Wound battle.
- **An ally's power thereafter.** In the finale at Sovngarde, it is **Gormlaith, Hakon, and Felldir** — the three dead heroes recruited in the Hall of Valor — whose joined Voices strip Alduin's mist and pin him for the killing blow. The party fights; the allies supply the legendary power. Mechanically, treat the heroes' Dragonrend as a scene-framing effect the Warden invokes: for the final battle, Alduin's flight and his untouchability (the **Beyond reach** line of his stat block) are *suppressed while the three heroes Shout in concert*, so the party can close and strike.

If a player asks "can my character learn it?" — no. The Dragonborn can *absorb dragon souls* (a story marker that opens doors, summons the Greybeards, and impresses dragons, and at most a Growth trigger — see Running Campaigns in Skyrim), but **no Shout is ever added to a character sheet.** Their power is the steel in their hands and the allies at their back.
