*Norse apocalypse on the roof of the world. The land is beautiful and indifferent — pine and glacier and aurora — and the dead do not stay buried. The stakes climb from "a town burned" to "the end of the world," but the texture stays small and physical: cold hands on a sword-hilt, a draugr's blue eyes opening in the dark, the unbearable noise a dragon makes when it lands. The party are not chosen saviors handed a destiny; they are mortals who happen to be in the path of the World-Eater and decide to stand there anyway, gathering every ally and old weapon they can before the last fight.*

**Touchstones:** the *Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim* main quest itself; *Beowulf* (a hall, a monster, an old king); the Poetic Edda and Ragnarök; *The Lord of the Rings* (a small fellowship against a world-ending power, allies summoned at the eleventh hour); *The Seventh Seal* (mortality and a cold northern country); *The 13th Warrior*.

**Lean into:** the Voice as a force of nature you witness and never wield — the Greybeards' greeting that nearly knocks you off the mountain, Paarthurnax's slow rumbling speech, Alduin's roar resurrecting a dragon out of a barrow-mound. Word Walls glowing in the dark of a tomb, ancient and unreadable to the party. The civil war grinding on in the background, indifferent to the apocalypse. Snow, and the long climb of the 7,000 Steps, and the unbearable hush of High Hrothgar.

> **A note on the Voice.** In this frame the Thu'um is a story power, never a player rules mechanic. The Greybeards, Paarthurnax, Odahviing, and Alduin *use* it; the party does not get a Shout. Where the original tale has the hero learn **Dragonrend**, this frame replaces it with a thing the party *enables*: a vision read from an **Elder Scroll** and the old heroes' weapon it unlocks (see **The Elder Scroll & Dragonrend**, below). The party's job is to fight, to gather allies, and to land the final blow with those allies at their side.
