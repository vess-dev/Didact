The party has fought, schemed, or hauled freight long enough that someone in a Hold's court knows their name. A **Jarl** (or, more often, the Jarl's **Steward**, who keeps the land-ledger) lets it be known that a parcel is for sale: a clearing in the pines, a defensible knuckle of marsh, a windswept shelf of tundra. Buy it, and it is yours to build on — a base of operations that is *theirs*, not an inn room rented by the night.

What grows there is up to the party. On the page it is a **homestead that starts at Population 1 / Defense 1 / Prosperity 1** with one of its eight slots already filled by the bare **main hall**, exactly as Settlements describes. In the fiction it climbs from a one-room cottage to a great house with wings and outbuildings, a smithy ringing, a garden ripe, children underfoot, and a housecarl at the door. The grand manor *is* the filled slots; the family *is* the rising Population; the standing *is* the Jarl's favor.
