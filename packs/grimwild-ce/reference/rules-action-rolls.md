When you attempt to pull off something risky, clearly describe your goal (why you're doing it) and approach (how you're doing it). The GM will pick which stat the action falls under, and you roll that many d6, looking to the highest for your result.

- **Perfect (6):** You succeed, and avoid any trouble.
- **Messy (4-5):** You succeed, but there's also trouble.
- **Grim (1-3):** You fail, and there's trouble.

Trouble prompts an impact move: the GM makes an impact move (p. 100) to introduce consequences. Remember: the characters are competent, you're not rolling to see how you do, you're rolling to see how it goes. The consequences may not even directly relate to the action taken.

Thorns can cut the result (p. 11), more than one 6 is a critical (p. 10), and a grim that's cut is a disaster (p. 11).

**Reader Lens: D&D.** There are no rounds, and no initiative. PCs act by making action rolls, and the world reacts through impact moves from the GM, usually prompted by player rolls. This means PCs can take action in whatever order makes sense, even acting more than once, as long as the spotlight is shared between everyone. In Grimwild, the players play a more active role, while the GM reacts.
