Light damage, a temporary hindrance to a stat (*out of breath, confused*). Take +1t on the next roll with that stat, and clear the mark.

If a stat is already marked, you take harm (p. 27) instead. As long as a stat is unmarked, even if you have its related harm, you can take a mark to it.

Marks are often combined with another mark or other consequences when inflicted directly (*Agility mark + thrown off your horse*) as an alternative to harm. You also take a mark when you push yourself. If a mark is reduced (such as by a messy defense roll), it becomes set dressing.

*Examples:* mark + small narrative consequence (*mark Agility + drop your weapon | mark Wits + take vex*); two marks (*mark Agility + Wits as you're shaken | mark Wits + Presence as you're confused*).

From the examples of play (p. 116): a messy on a Brawn defense roll made with a marked Brawn marks Agility, then clears the Brawn mark because it was rolled. Clearing marks after other marks are applied avoids taking a mark, rolling it, clearing it, taking another, and so on; it's better to change the fiction up and have it move to a different stat.
