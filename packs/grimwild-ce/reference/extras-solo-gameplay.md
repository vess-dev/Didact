Core rules look largely the same, however there are guardrails added to replace the GM. The following rules are added on top of base Grimwild.
- **When a scene gets stale:** *spend suspense—foreshadow a threat to come—montage to a low stakes scene—build up to a high stakes scene.*
- **When you make an impact move,** choose the most fitting consequence, or use the crucibles for ideas or randomized trouble (roll d8, pick between two): roll `solo-consequences --with stakes=normal`, with its low & normal stakes and high & dire stakes columns. Remember that harm is reserved for particularly hard hits.
- **On a messy,** ask the following questions. If you answer 'yes' to any, make an impact move. If 'no', take suspense.
  - Are you in a scene with a challenge?
  - Are you in the last task pool of a quest?
  - Do you have 3 or more suspense?
- **On a disaster, or with dire stakes,** you take the normal consequences, plus: *roll a peril pool (p. 194)—harm.*

**Consequences** named on the table:
- **Set dressing:** The trouble is only narrative.
- **Strain bond:** Relationship with a faction or ally is strained.
- **Collateral damage:** The environment is destroyed or bystanders are hurt.
- **Lose something:** Gear or resources are ruined or lost.
- **Lock down:** You lose an opportunity or are forced to make a bad choice.
- **Raise stakes:** The scene gets more tense, moving stakes up a level.
- **Twist:** There's an unexpected change for the worse, such as a betrayal.
- **Pressure pool:** Create or roll a pool.

The table also deals marks, harm, vex, conditions (short, long, urgent) and counters.

*Didact:* with the solo option, the action roll asks the messy questions and adds peril on a disaster or at dire stakes.
