Some heritages have innate capabilities beyond their wises. You can link the second talent at character creation to your heritage, or work with the GM to create one. If something isn't strong enough to warrant a talent, work with the GM to add it in as set dressing—a bit of flavor, but can't be relied upon to impact scenes.

See the next page for talents that fit more thematically with backgrounds and heritages, but are otherwise available to all paths.

### Suggested talents for common heritages

- Human: Jack of All Trades (pg. 57)
- Elf: Keen Senses (pg. 73)
- Dwarf: Dauntless (pg. 69)
- Halfling: Lucky (pg. 81)
- Gnome: Arcane Training (pg. 65)
- Orc: Flesh Wounds (pg. 59)
- Dragonkin: Eldritch Growth (pg. 77)
- Fiend: Eldritch Affinity (pg. 75)
- Celestial: Healing Hands (pg. 67)
- Satyr: Crowd Favorite (pg. 89)
- Elemental: Otherworldly Form (pg. 83)
- Giant: Mighty (pg. 59)
- Turtlefolk: Bulwark (pg. 65)
- Lizardfolk: Poisoner (pg. 75)
- Merfolk: Aquatic (pg. 89)
- Kobold: Trap Making (pg. 55)
- Goblin: Opportunist (pg. 75)
- Birdfolk: Flight (pg. 89)
- Firbolg: Kindred Spirits (pg. 63)
- Forged: Artificial Parts (pg. 55)

While these are suggested, there are many talents that could be reflavored to match any heritage. Use these as a starting point, and think about your character’s place in the society they grew up in. Do they fit in, representing their culture well, or were they the outcast?

*Didact:* the second talent's list at creation (and a level's) can be the background talents; talent-lists holds them.
