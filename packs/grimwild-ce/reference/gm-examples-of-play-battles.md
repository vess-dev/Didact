A short summary of the book's worked examples, with the lessons its sidebars draw.
- **Gnolls at the Gate (p. 115).** Suspense spent on a battering ram rolls the 6d Walls Breached pool; a cleric's warding spell, ruled on the spot, adds 2d back; a wizard's critical rolls the marauders' pool and, as a secondary effect, sets the ram on fire (a new 4d pool, rolled at once). *Greater effect would have dropped 1d first; setup would give the next PC an assist without risk. Setting up and rolling pools is a key GM skill.*
- **Outnumbered by Bandits (p. 115).** One fighter against 10 seasoned bandits is impossible, so the fighter runs to split them up: Wits with no thorns, a messy—three bandits still follow (+1t), and a twisted ankle marks Agility; the player adds a tangle for spark. *When unsure whether to add a thorn, add it; be consistent, since numbers are tough; split consequences rather than hammering a PC early.*
- **Treacherous Channel (pp. 116-118).** A thorn cuts the helmsman's perfect to a messy (the Navigate Channel challenge drops 8d→4d, and its Graze Reef move follows); the lookout's failed defense starts a 6d Taking on Water pool beside the 6d Storm Blows In fail state. Aboard the pirate ship, a defense roll made with a marked Brawn marks Agility, then clears the Brawn mark; the rogue baits the kraken into sweeping the deckhands at dire stakes and is dropped, until the cleric's 1d protecting-others roll saves him; when the 4d Waves threat washes the cleric overboard, the wizard's scorching rescue succeeds on a messy and the GM takes suspense instead of a move. *Fail states stop endless rolling; remind players they can assist; don't fear Lock Down; declare dire stakes when a roll could drop a PC who isn't desperate; suspense is a fallback on a messy.*
- **Bandit Showdown (p. 119).** One perfect takes out 4 mook bandits; the rogue's tangle (bragging out loud) earns spark. The leader is a brute, so a 1d story roll says he doesn't flee; at dire stakes, the rogue's grim defense skips marked and goes straight to bloodied. *A single good roll takes out a couple of mooks or one tough (an elite, with a good pool roll); look to a foe's role.*
- **The Red Dragon (pp. 120-121).** After a Build Up, each link of the Great Red Dragon spends its own suspense: fire on the town, kobolds bloodying the cleric, a roar that vexes the party (even the fighter, whose own messy prompted it, gets to defend, since the GM aims for more than a light consequence), and claw and tail strikes that leave the fighter rattled and bloodied. The bard's Inspiration interrupts the dragon's escape. *Suspense can hit several PCs (lessen group attacks); striking a PC's goal can beat harm; the 'fly away' fail state allows a Lock Down.*
