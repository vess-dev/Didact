Settlement maps are built with Expand the Map, but with a different drawing style. The GM begins by drawing the town's outline, either as a vague border (a line, like a path) or as a wall (a barrier). You add roads and notable buildings following the rules below, and barriers, areas, curiosities, and passes as normal.

- **Road (1 token):** Draw a straight line between any two other lines on the map. These act as the main roads used throughout the settlement. Passes can be added to roads for free when placed, allowing them to cross areas and barriers as needed.
- **Buildings (2-3 tokens):** Draw a notable building within an empty space between roads on the map, generally in small areas you've created. Detail it or use the building crucible (p. 135). Created buildings can be minor or major, the difference being the narrative impact.
  - **Minor building (2 tokens):** When you add a minor building, write it on the map in lower case letters.
  - **Major building (3 tokens):** Adding these buildings tells the GM that you want the location to be more important in the setting or story. When you add a major building, write it on the map in capital letters.
- **Special locations (3 tokens):** These locations can be added by the GM for free to spice up the map, or as the story requires.
  - **Curiosity:** Rumors or interesting locations and happenings that can be explored.
  - **Barrier:** Obstacles such as rivers, changes in elevation, and walls that prevent easy travel. These are only crossable via passes.
  - **Area:** Large spaces that are thematically distinct from the larger settlement. Natural areas could include lakes or indoor caverns, while artificial areas might include city districts.

**Building crucible:** roll 2 on each table, smash them together, and interpret—roll `building-crucible`.

*Example, the Village of Humblefalls: the GM draws the outside walls first; the players add roads and a MYSTERIOUS INN, a FESTIVE LIBRARY, a TIMEWORN TEMPLE, a crumbling bathhouse, an overgrown herbalist, a mystic market and a lonely manor.*
