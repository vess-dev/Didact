What counts as risky shifts with the moment and tone of the game. Most often this is when there is an immediate threat or time is of the essence. Assume the characters are competent and only roll when it pushes the story forward, when meaningful results come about no matter the outcome. Damage can sometimes make things risky that otherwise wouldn't be. If there's no risk, give the PC the spotlight (p. 95) to narrate their success. If failing to do something isn't an interesting consequence, focus on what it costs for them to get what they want.

*The Thief is picking the lock to a warehouse on the dock (no risk), but city patrols are making rounds in the area (now the action is risky).*

*The Healer needs to administer an antidote to their ally (no risk), but they have to act quickly, and get there from across the city (now the action is risky).*
