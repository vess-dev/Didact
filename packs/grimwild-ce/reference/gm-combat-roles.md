Make sure each enemy role brings a unique dynamic to combat. To help, you can use the role templates as a simple basis for traits and moves, or even assign them alongside a monster entry (Chapter 5). Each role's traits contain two strengths and a weakness, while its moves have one offensive, one defensive, and one utility. Additionally, each role has a small table for ideas on how to spend suspense in response to common worst-case scenarios that role encounters.

The ten roles (each in the compendium with its traits, moves and suspense table: `didact compendium find <role>`):
- **Blaster:** Ranged foes who unleash overwhelming power from afar, raining chaos on the battlefield. They crumble when the fight gets too close.
- **Brute:** Brutal raiders who thrive in chaos, striking with sudden overwhelming strength. Each strike shakes the ground, leaving few standing.
- **Lurker:** Stealthy stalkers who strike from concealment, waiting patiently for the perfect moment. They falter when exposed or when in prolonged fights.
- **Marksman:** Sharpshooters who excel at picking off targets from a distance. They are vulnerable when caught in melee, or forced to move quickly.
- **Predator:** Persistent hunters who stalk their prey, waiting for the perfect moment to strike. They falter if their prey is tenacious.
- **Protector:** Unyielding guardians who shield allies and endure attacks meant for others. They struggle when separated from those they protect.
- **Skirmisher:** Agile combatants who excel at hit-and-run tactics, harassing foes and striking from unexpected angles. They falter when pinned down.
- **Swarmer:** Relentless attackers who overwhelm enemies with sheer numbers, striking from all sides to wear them down. They falter when isolated.
- **Tactician:** Clever strategists who manipulate the battlefield, exploit every weakness they find, and command those around them. They struggle when their plan fails.
- **Trickster:** Cunning deceivers who thrive on confusion, turning the tide of battle with guile. They crumble when their tricks are exposed or fail.

From the examples of play (p. 119): when in doubt about what an enemy would do, look to their role and go from there.
