**Rulings that come up.** Spellcasting is visible and loud — light in the hands, words in the air; hiding it is extremely difficult at best, and usually impossible. Anyone who knows magic is at work can try to **counter** it, no training needed: break the caster's line of sight, shatter the focus, drag the charmed man out of the room — creativity beats magicka. Helping with your magic is just an assist (say how, roll 1d, share the risk). A spell's effect lasts a beat or two; a potent one, a scene — after that the world reasserts itself. Blessings ride the usual shapes — a 2–4d timer or a small bonus-dice pool — and stretching one across a whole scene or a whole group takes a potent spell. Summoning follows suit: a spell calls a tool, a light, or a helper worth a *2d* power pool; a potent one, a creature worth *4d*. So does healing: a spell can clear marks, vex, or a minor condition; it takes a potent one to mend harm. And magic respects rank like everything else: what would take a tough or lesser foe out of the fight only costs a greater one its next move (Chapter 4).

**Arcana** (Chapter 8) are the relics: **minor**, **major**, and **mythic**, most balanced by a pool or a price. Single-use finds — a scroll, a potion, a filled soul gem — are **expendable**, and punch one tier above their weight. The GM picks the stat an arcana rolls (Wits when nothing fits, or a story roll when it's pure luck), and mythics answer to no cap but the table's judgment.
