Coin is **vantage and set dressing**, not bookkeeping — a merchant always has walking-around money; a pilgrim may not know what anything costs in a city. Serious wealth moves in **hauls**, in three sizes:

- **Minor haul:** a good sword, a fat purse of septims, a filled soul gem, a horse, a crate of Dwemer scrap, a minor relic.
- **Major haul:** a suit of ebony, a caravan's cargo, a house in town, a flawless diamond the size of a thumb, a major relic.
- **Mythic haul:** a ship and its crew, the deed to a keep, a dragon's hoard, a seat on a council.

Hauls belong to the party unless you decide otherwise, and when a hero needs one of them, it happens to be on hand — unless the GM complicates things.

Every hero also carries a personal *4d Coin* power pool. A drink, a bed, a quiver of arrows are set dressing. Roll the pool when you spend big — a bribe, a horse, a healer's miracle — and drop 1d first for the huge asks; an impact move can mean a hitch — a flaw, a cheat, a debt — even when the deal goes through. At 0d, you're broke. Prices quoted in septims are flavor: a few hundred is a minor haul, a few thousand a major one. Selling off a minor haul in a town adds +2d (max 8d) and earns you a second, different downtime action (see Downtime, below). For anything truly priceless, the price is a story — a favor, a job, a debt.

*Didact:* `didact do spend-coin who=<hero>` (`huge` drops 1d first; `keep` for Haggling and the like); `didact do sell-haul who=<hero>`.
