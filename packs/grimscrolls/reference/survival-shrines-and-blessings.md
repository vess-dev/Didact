Pray at a shrine of the Divines — in a chapel, at a wayshrine, on a hillside with offerings in the snow — and two things happen: every ordinary disease you carry is cured, and you take that god's **blessing**. A blessing is a single boon, held until you use it or until the next session ends; you carry one at a time, and a shrine blesses you once a session.

| Divine | The blessing — once… |
|---|---|
| **Akatosh**, the Dragon God of Time | act first, whatever else is happening |
| **Arkay**, of the cycle of birth and death | ignore every thorn from your own harm on one roll |
| **Dibella**, of beauty and art | take **+1d** on a first impression |
| **Julianos**, of wisdom and logic | regain magic (Chapter 4) — or ask the GM one question about a puzzle and get a straight answer |
| **Kynareth**, of air and the wild | push yourself without marking a stat |
| **Mara**, of love and compassion | a healing potion you give or take doesn't roll its pool |
| **Stendarr**, of mercy and justice | take **+1d** on a defense roll for yourself or another |
| **Talos**, the man who became a god | take **+1d** on an act of open courage — in the Fourth Era, the shrine itself is a crime |
| **Zenithar**, of work and commerce | when your *Coin* rolls, one die that would drop stays |

The Tribunal Temple's shrines in Morrowind do the same work under other names, and cure the Blight besides. Daedric shrines offer no blessings — only bargains (Chapter 9).

*Didact:* `didact do pray who=<hero> divine=akatosh` (`tribunal` for a Tribunal shrine); `didact do use-blessing who=<hero>`.
