Tamriel's threats have no hit points — just **traits**, **moves**, and a **tier** that sets their presence. **Mooks** are fodder: one good roll clears several, and a pack is a single task pool, usually **4d**. **Toughs** fall one at a time; a knot of them is a pool too, usually **6d**. **Elites** are a challenge unto themselves, usually **6d**. **Bosses** own the scene at **8d** — or as a **linked challenge** whose parts each carry their own pool, traits, and moves, with a **fail state** that ends the fight the bad way. Elites and bosses framed as challenges bring **2 bonus suspense** of their own (Chapter 9); a pool of mooks or toughs is just a pool.
**Enemies never roll.** When a card makes something dangerous, it shows up as thorns on your rolls, the stakes, the GM's suspense, and the size and stubbornness of a pool. Variants, ranks, and regional cousins ride in each entry's **Note** — re-skin, re-tier, and re-arm to taste. (Talents and relics call mooks **lesser** foes, and elites and bosses **greater** ones.)

*Didact:* `didact gen run grimscrolls.foe --prefill '{"creature": "Draugr"}'` puts a threat in play: its card and its pools (a pack's one pool, a boss's linked parts and its fail state's timer).
