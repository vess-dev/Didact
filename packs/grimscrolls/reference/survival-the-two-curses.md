Two diseases don't just linger. They finish.

**Sanguinare Vampiris** — in the south and east they call it Porphyric Hemophilia — comes from a vampire's bite or its draining spell. It's an **urgent condition**: a *4d Sanguinare Vampiris* timer, rolled each night you sleep. Any cure for disease ends it. At 0d you die quietly before dawn, and get up a vampire.

**Sanies Lupinus** comes from a werewolf's teeth; the Companions' Circle and Hircine's witches pass the same blood on purpose, in a cup. It's a *4d Sanies Lupinus* timer, rolled each nightfall, and any cure for disease ends it. At 0d the first change comes that night, and it isn't yours to steer: the GM plays the wolf for a scene.

**Once you've turned,** the curse is a permanent condition with its **price** but none of its gifts: a vampire burns in the sun and catches fire easily; a werewolf fears silver and changes when the GM spends suspense to say so. To take the gifts as well, take the curse's talent (*Sanguinare Vampiris* or *Lycanthropy*, Chapter 4) — at your next level, or at once by trading away the last talent you gained. A hero can't carry both curses: the beast blood burns the other out.

**Cures** are adventures, and the chronicles agree on their shape: someone who knows the rite, a thing that's hard to get, and a price. A filled black soul gem and a circle of stones at dawn. The heads of the Glenmoril witches, burned in a hero's tomb. A bargain with the Prince who made the curse — who will ask what it's worth to you.

*Didact:* `didact do curse who=<hero> curse=sanguinare-vampiris` makes its 4d timer, which the nights roll as time passes (`didact time advance`); at 0d the hero turns (`didact do turn who=<hero> curse=vampire`).
