**The tier gate.** Read every card against its badge:

- A **minor** item is a narrow edge: set-dressing vantage, a thorn it ignores, **+1d** in a narrow circumstance, a *2d* pool for a spell-sized trick, or a once-per-session trick. Minors are never potent.

- A **major** item is a broad edge — +1d whenever it's the right tool, or permissions nobody else has — plus an engine: a **once-per-session potent** use, a *4d* **power pool** for its signature trick, or both. A weapon that's potent on *every* use is mythic-grade, and sits at major only under a harsh limit.

- A **mythic** item answers to no cap but the table's judgment: ritual-level deeds and any number of potent tricks, its pools going potent by dropping 1d. It counts as its own ritual anchors; where a card lists what it **needs**, those are the thing's own requirements, not a ritual's.

- An **expendable** punches one tier up: a minor ×1 gives one potent effect; a major ×1, one ritual-level effect; a mythic ×1 is a plot device. (A common draught — a single spell-sized effect — is *trivial*, and belongs with the potions in Chapter 6.)
