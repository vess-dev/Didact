Before running any vault in this chapter, run through this short checklist at the table. Each vault entry in this book already addresses most of these; when an entry is thin on one, the Overseer supplies the missing piece from the fiction.

1. **Something to steal.** The Reward is the reason for the trip. The vault should *also* contain one smaller prize a clever party might walk out with — a holotape, a clean Stimpak, a keycard.
2. **Something to be killed.** The Inhabitants give the party enemies to overcome. Keep their numbers honest — a vault is not a combat track, it's a haunted house.
3. **Something that can kill the party.** Telegraph a real danger the party should not fight head-on. A Glowing One in a reactor pool. A radscorpion nest in the maintenance tunnels. The party must be able to hear it coming and choose a different hallway.
4. **Different ways through.** Every vault should have at least **two ways in** — the blast door, and something else (a maintenance hatch, a collapsed ceiling, a sealed side-tunnel the ghouls forgot about). The Layout is a pointcrawl, not a hallway.
5. **Someone to talk to.** A lucid ghoul. A Mr. Handy stuck on a century-old directive. A surviving resident. A child with a password. An Overseer's terminal that answers questions. Every vault has a voice somewhere; find it before running the session.
6. **Something to experiment with.** Every canonical vault is a pre-war experiment — that's the setting's entire thesis. Make the experiment *touchable*. Let the party turn a dial, unplug a wire, listen to the broadcast, drink the water, step into the pod. Fallout vaults are about the temptation of the wrong button.
7. **Something the party probably won't find.** The **Hidden** note on each entry. A player who pokes at a corner the Overseer never described should sometimes be rewarded for the instinct.
