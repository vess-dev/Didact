Broad strokes. Four to six sessions. The campaign is a slow argument between a pacifist and a soldier, with the party breaking the tie.

1. **The Ambush.** The Happy Trails caravan is hit on the approach to Zion. The boss dies. The party survives because a White Leg scout chose to leave them for the canyon. The scout was wrong.
2. **Daniel at the Ranger Station.** Daniel finds the party at a pre-war survivalist's bolt-hole. He is gentle, exhausted, and clear: the Sorrows must leave Zion before Salt-Upon-Wounds's war party reaches them. He needs guides who can talk to the tribes without frightening them.
3. **Joshua Graham in the Cave.** Graham introduces himself plainly and without apology. He has a different plan: arm the Sorrows, train the Dead Horses, meet the White Legs at the Three Marys. The party must now pick a side — or stall between them for a few sessions, which is fine, because the White Legs are getting closer either way.
4. **Visiting the Tribes.** Dead Horse Camp and Sorrows Camp. Follows-Chalk wants to see the Mojave (will leave with the party at campaign's end). Waking Cloud wants to understand the pre-war world she keeps hearing about in Daniel's stories. Each camp has a simple job the party can do to earn loyalty: hunt a yao guai, track a cazador nest, carry a message.
5. **The Survivalist's Journal.** A pre-war U.S. survivalist held out alone in Zion for decades after the war. His rifle, his caches, and his journal are scattered around the canyon. The journal reads like scripture to the Sorrows. Finding it is not the mission — but what the party does with it shapes what Zion remembers about them.
6. **Scouting the White Legs.** The White Legs Staging Ground is a mountain pass. Scouts range from there in pairs. A clean reconnaissance gives Graham what he needs to plan the ambush; a loud one tips Salt-Upon-Wounds and accelerates his attack.
7. **The Three Marys.** The climax. The canyon kill-box where the party's chosen plan — Graham's ambush or Daniel's evacuation route — plays out. Both work. Both cost. See **Climax** below.
8. **After Zion.** Honest Hearts does not end with a victory — it ends with a departure. Daniel weeps, Graham prays, the tribes scatter or gather. Zion goes back to being Zion. The party leaves with Follows-Chalk or without him. Someone at the ranger station watches them go.
