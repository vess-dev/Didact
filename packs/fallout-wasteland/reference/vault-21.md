*Vaults of Fallout: New Vegas*

**Premise.** Every decision in the vault — including the decision to open the door — was made by gambling. Residents resolved all disputes, assignments, and elections through games of chance, and the experiment worked: Vault 21 kept its people safe and equal. In 2274 Mr. House offered to bring it into New Vegas; those in favor won the right to accept in a blackjack game that lasted for hours. House stripped the vault and filled most of it with concrete. Two vault-born residents, **Sarah Weintraub** and her brother, talked him into sparing half the upper level, and Sarah runs it now as the **Vault 21 Hotel**.

**Layout.**
- **Casino Floor.** The vault's old gambling atrium, spared from the concrete — slot machines, a roulette wheel, a blackjack table, and a craps pit. All still partly operational.
- **The Dealer's Booth.** A pre-war dealer robot (broken) still sits in the booth with a deck of cards in its manipulator.
- **Residential Block.** Small cabins, each decorated with a playing card suit.
- **Overseer's Office.** The Overseer left a holotape explaining that the experiment worked — the vault stayed peaceful for generations.
- **The Vault.** A locked storage room with a pre-war card deck that has a star on the back of every card.

**Inhabitants.** Sarah Weintraub, her hotel staff, and the handful of former dwellers who stayed on. They still resolve disputes with dice. They are friendly, shrewd, and happy to gamble with the party.

**Reward.** **The Pre-war Card Deck** — a _petty_ relic that, once per session, shifts the next Reaction roll two steps better when presented with a flourish. A **Pip-Boy 3000** that House's strippers missed, in a drawer of the Overseer's desk.

**Hidden.** The roulette wheel has a slight mechanical bias toward **red 17** — an engineering flaw the Overseer discovered and decided not to fix because it gave her a secret edge on the vault's own experiment. A PC who figures this out may claim an edge at any roulette table for the rest of the campaign.

**Hook.** A caravaneer has lost his caravan's profits to the residents in a poker game. He wants the party to win them back for him — or to cheat.
