Broad strokes. Five to seven sessions. The pacing should feel like a slow tightening rather than an escalating action.

1. **The Fountain Plaza.** The party wakes in the Villa, collars on, fog around them. Elijah's voice is already in their ears. A single Ghost Person is visible down the street. The first lesson of Dead Money is that a Ghost Person who *looks* dead is not dead — the **Return** rule applies from the first encounter.
2. **Recovering Dean Domino.** The lounge singer ghoul is in the residential district, already working on his own plan to steal the gold. He joins the party only because Elijah's collar threat extends to him too. Dean is oily, funny, and will absolutely turn on the party in the climax unless the Overseer gives him a reason not to.
3. **Recovering Dog/God.** In the police station, the party meets the nightkin with two minds. Dog wants food. God wants control. The party can choose which one to stabilize — the choice matters at the Gala. Holograms patrol the station; each one dies only to its *emitter*.
4. **Recovering Christine.** The mute Brotherhood survivor in the clinic has been trying to kill Elijah for a long time. She cannot speak but she can fight. A PC who plays a holotape at her earns a hard ally; a PC who condescends to her earns an armed rival.
5. **The Villa Clinic and the Auto-Doc.** A working pre-war auto-doc can re-tune a single party member's collar to give them a narrow window of immunity to one speaker zone. This is the party's one real edge; using it costs a Stimpak and a roll on the Scars table at HP Lost 1d6.
6. **The Sierra Madre Gala.** The casino's front doors open only when all three companions have been met. The Gala was the pre-war event the casino was built for — and it never happened. The party walks in on a hologram-staged opening night: Vera Keyes's voice, the orchestra playing, the red velvet still laid out.
7. **The Vault.** Below the casino. Gold bars, pre-war security systems, and the original tomb. Elijah's voice comes through the PA — he wants the party to unlock it for him. The fight in the vault is not with Elijah directly; it is with whatever the party has brought down here *and* whatever they cannot bring themselves to walk away from.
8. **The Walk Out.** Dead Money ends with a departure. Either the party seals Elijah inside and leaves with their lives (the "right" ending), walks out with a pocketful of gold (the "wrong" one), or lets the weight of what they're carrying kill them a mile down the road. The Overseer should describe the sunrise.
