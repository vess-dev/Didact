The DLC frames slot naturally into the main campaign they share a region with. In rough play order:

1. **Fallout 3** → Operation: Anchorage, The Pitt, Broken Steel, Point Lookout, Mothership Zeta.
2. **Fallout: New Vegas** → Dead Money, Honest Hearts, Old World Blues, Lonesome Road.
3. **Fallout 4** → Automatron, Far Harbor, Vault-Tec Workshop, Nuka-World.
4. **Fallout 76** → Wastelanders, Steel Dawn, Skyline Valley, Atlantic City.

Broken Steel in particular picks up two weeks after the Fallout 3 main campaign's climax. Lonesome Road picks up the Courier's forgotten past from before the Fallout: New Vegas main campaign even began. Some DLCs (Far Harbor, Nuka-World, Atlantic City) can stand alone; others (Broken Steel, Lonesome Road, Wastelanders) read best after the main.

The Fallout 76 frames run on their own timeline, more than a century and a half before the others: the main campaign opens on Reclamation Day in 2102, Wastelanders follows a year later, Steel Dawn in 2104, and Skyline Valley and Atlantic City in 2105. They make a natural prequel — the Appalachian Brotherhood, the Enclave's forgotten bunker, and the first Vault Dwellers to walk back into the world.
