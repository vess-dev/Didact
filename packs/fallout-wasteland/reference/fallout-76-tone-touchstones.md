*A ghost story in a green place. Almost everyone the party meets is dead: they speak through holotapes, terminal entries, and the robots they left running. The Overseer's job is to make the dead feel present — to let a party learn to love Maria Chavez or Abbie Singh from their voices alone, then find where each of them fell. The land is beautiful. That is the cruelty of it.*

**Touchstones:** *Fallout 76* itself, John Denver's "Take Me Home, Country Roads" (sung by a vault that never got to go home), *The Last of Us* (a plague that leaves its victims still half themselves), Appalachian folklore — Mothman, the Flatwoods Monster, the Grafton Monster, the Wendigo — *Annihilation* (the Cranberry Bog's alien bloom), *Dr. Strangelove* (the Whitespring's bunker bureaucracy). Coal-mining ballads. Ski-lodge brochures. Scout merit badges.

**Lean into:** the Vault Boy cardboard cutouts pointing the way out of the vault; autumn leaves on a rusted school bus; a Protectron still directing traffic in Watoga for a city that fled; the Top of the World, a giant red thumbtack of a ski lift above the clouds; the Scorched, who still carry rifles and still mutter fragments of their old lives; the glow of ultracite crystals splitting through their skin; the Whitespring's robot staff greeting guests who will never check in. The first human voice the party hears alive should arrive very late.
