*Vaults of Fallout 3*

**Premise.** Psychoactive agents were pumped into the vault's air ten days after sealing. The residents suffered mass hallucinations for the rest of their lives.

**Layout.**
- **Entry Hall.** The air is still thick. A thin chemical haze clings to the ceiling. Any PC without a gas mask must make a WIL save each hour or begin to hallucinate.
- **Residential Block.** The residents are long dead, but the vault is filled with their lingering hallucinations — a PC breathing the air hears voices, footsteps, and half-remembered conversations that belonged to the dead. What shambles the halls now are feral ghouls — later scavengers, ghoulified by the vault's leaking reactor, which has soaked the lower levels in Rads for a century. They share the same hallucinations.
- **The Painted Hall.** A corridor hung end to end with stitched bedsheets, on which someone has painted an entire pre-war skyline from memory, in blood. The skyline is of a city that does not exist.
- **Overseer's Office.** The Overseer is still alive, ghoulified, still hallucinating. She thinks the party are her children.
- **Reactor Room.** The chemical dispenser is still running. Shutting it off clears the vault — and wakes up the Overseer.

**Inhabitants.** HALLUCINATING GHOULS (treat as FERAL GHOUL PACK with occasional WIL saves on PCs), GHOUL OVERSEER (treat as FERAL GHOUL REAVER with a story).

**Reward.** A **Chemical Formula** recovered from the dispenser — a recipe for a controlled psychoactive that, in chem form, lets the user automatically pass WIL saves against fear, charm, or compulsion for one scene; _addictive_ (each dose forces an addiction save). The **Skyline Painting** itself, cut down from the Painted Hall (_bulky_, 500 caps to a collector — see **Vault 106 Skyline Painting** in Relics).

**Hidden.** The painted hall's skyline is not imaginary — a PC who spends a turn studying it recognizes the faint outline of a real pre-war city the residents could not possibly have seen from inside the vault. The skyline is accurate to a satellite-photo level of detail. Someone in this vault was receiving visions from somewhere.

**Hook.** A Followers scholar has been studying the vault's chemical and wants the formula — for her own research, she insists.
