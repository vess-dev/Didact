*A grief story in a ruin. Colonial New England, red-brick and sea-spray, the Green Monster still standing over Diamond City. Every stranger in the Commonwealth might be a synth and might not. The Overseer's job is to make both uncertainty and loyalty hurt equally, and to let a pre-war PC's grief matter when the campaign wants to shrug it off.*

**Touchstones:** *Fallout 4* itself, *Blade Runner* (what makes a person a person), *The Prestige* (who you were vs. who you became), *Children of Men* (a single lost child as the whole campaign's engine), John le Carré (the spy-tradecraft feel of Railroad work), *A.I. Artificial Intelligence* (a child who should be a boy, isn't). Old Cape Cod diner signs. The way Boston accents don't exist postwar but the place names still do.

**Lean into:** the rusted-out T-45 in the Concord museum; the crackle of Diamond City Radio; a settler pointing at a raider camp that is "just up the road" — which turns out to be an hour of careful movement. Institute Coursers move in black leather and speak softly. Brotherhood knights descend on cables from a blimp. The Railroad passes messages in chalk on a post-office wall.
