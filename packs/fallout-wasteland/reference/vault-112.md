*Vaults of Fallout 3*

**Premise.** A virtual-reality simulation experiment. The residents were placed in VR stasis loungers and their minds fed into a shared dream world run by the vault's founder, a pre-war scientist. The sim was supposed to last a decade. It ran for two centuries.

**Layout.**
- **Entry Hall.** Still powered. A robotic assistant greets any visitor and politely requests they proceed to the pod chamber.
- **Pod Chamber.** Twelve VR stasis loungers in a circle, ten of them occupied by the long-lived residents, still dreaming. One lounger is empty. One lounger is open and its occupant is a withered corpse.
- **The Dream.** A PC who enters an open pod is pulled into **Tranquility Lane** — a pre-war suburban simulation where everyone is smiling, everyone is cheerful, everyone is watched. Inside the sim, a child with too much authority commands the dead. To exit: either succeed at a difficult WIL-based quest inside the sim, or have an outside ally interrupt the simulation through the external terminal.
- **Control Room.** The external terminal. Anyone reading the logs learns the founder's name and the original ten residents' names. Shutting off the sim kills all ten, peacefully. Waking them one at a time is possible but slow.
- **Reactor Room.** The reactor has enough power for another fifty years.

**Inhabitants.** The DREAMING RESIDENTS (non-combat, moral weight only). The CHILD OVERSEER inside the simulation (treat as a WIL-based encounter, not a combat one).

**Reward.** A **Functional Pip-Boy 3000** (from the corpse in the open pod). A **Holotape of Tranquility Lane's Original Recording** — the founder's voice, gentle and inviting, explaining the experiment.

**Hidden.** The control room has a second terminal behind a slid-aside mural — an **admin console** that lets a user rewrite the simulation's physics rather than just shut it down. With it, a PC may enter Tranquility Lane as a *god-moded* visitor for one session only. The cost: the interface pushes too hard — take 1d4 WIL damage.

**Hook.** A caravaneer in a nearby settlement is the grandchild of the man in the open pod — a wanderer who went looking for the vault's rumored paradise forty years ago and never came out. She has his last letter, written the night before he climbed in. She wants answers.
