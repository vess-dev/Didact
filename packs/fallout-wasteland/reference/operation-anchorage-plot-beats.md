Broad strokes. Use these as a spine for four to six sessions; reorder, skip, or reshape as the table pushes. Each beat is a turning point, not a scripted scene.

1. **The Pod Room.** Olin briefs the party on the cache, McGraw glowers from the doorway, and the rig scans each PC for "anomalies." Any PC who fails the scan gets a single nosebleed moment as the pod tries to reject them — let the player decide whether to push through, and take 1 Fatigue for the trouble.
2. **Drop-In at Listening Post Bravo.** The party wakes on a snowbank in sim loadouts they never packed. A Chinese perimeter patrol is walking toward them. General Chase cheerfully briefs them over the radio as if this were a training exercise. The first Glitch Clock tick should land in this scene — a magazine that refills itself, a dead body that pops back up.
3. **Light the Artillery Tower.** Before the party can push inland, they must activate a signal tower. The mission objective says *light the flare*; the tower is guarded by Crimson Dragoons who shouldn't be there according to the briefing. This is the scene where the sim's scripted clean war first stops making sense.
4. **The Chimera Depot.** A yard of mothballed Chinese armored rigs the mission wants them to sabotage. Two options: rig a Chimera's reactor to take out the next wall, or *steal* one and ride it through. Stealing a Chimera is an off-script victory Chase does not know how to respond to — drop a Glitch tick and watch him improvise badly.
5. **The Pulse Field and the POW Camp.** The sim routes them through an EMP minefield toward a Chinese POW camp. The POWs look *real* — they speak full English, beg for a word home, name their families. Chase orders the party to move past them. What the party does here sets the tone for Jingwei.
6. **The Ice Camp.** The last obstacle before Jingwei's bunker. The Dragoons here are dug in with sniper nests. A loud assault wakes a Chimera; a quiet one rewards the party with three stealth suits they'll want to keep (they cannot). Max the Chase Clock here if the party has been running off-script.
7. **The Duel in the Bunker.** Jingwei waits. The sim insists on a one-on-one duel; breaking the duel summons Sim-MPs. Resolutions in the **Climax** below.
8. **Ejection.** However the duel resolves, the party ejects and the cache cracks open — or doesn't. Olin is waiting with a coffee and a worried look. McGraw wants to know what they saw in there. If the cache opens, **Defender Sibley** mutinies — he refuses to hand pre-war weapons to outsiders and opens fire on McGraw (use **Brotherhood Knight** for Sibley and anyone who sides with him). The party's last fight in this campaign is not in the sim; it's in the bunker, real blood, real bullets, a Brotherhood civil war in a closet.
