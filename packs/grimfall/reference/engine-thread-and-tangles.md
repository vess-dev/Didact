**Set dressing is free.** Minor, common-sense details never cost anything — you have a lighter, the counter has a tip jar, your knife is where knives go. Anything *certain* in your vantage can just be established. Thread is for the details that change the situation.

**Thread** is authorship. You get **2 per session**. Spend 1 to add a story detail beyond set dressing — gear you'd plausibly have, a contact in this town, a rumor that pans out. It must fit your vantage (at least doubtfully) or tie to a story arc; the GM can call for a story roll on the bold ones, add nuance, or veto what breaks the story. A detail can't override a roll or contradict what's already established, and details that touch another PC need their okay.

**Tangles** are complications you volunteer — your character's flaws steering the story. *You light a cigarette inside the chem lab. You can't resist the sealed vault door. You tell the NCR captain exactly what you think of her.* A tangle lands with the weight of an impact move, and you take **spark** for it.

**Quarrels.** When two survivors are at genuine cross-purposes and both players agree to settle it, name the stakes — dramatic, not deadly (bruises, not bloodied) — then both roll 2d; highest wins. No perks apply; others can assist or pile in at 2d with their own agenda, and a tie means compromise or another round. The winner narrates how it goes their way; the loser narrates how they take it. The result is final — and everyone involved takes spark.

**Bonds.** Toward each other PC, hold one bond — an intensity plus a nature (*lowkey doubts, tense rivalry, deep affection*). Change it when something lands; give a short vignette, and the other player takes spark.

**Story arcs.** One group arc, one or two character arcs — short, evocative directions (*prove myself — escape my past — make the water flow*). Resolving one with a real moment earns spark. Arcs flag where you want the drama.
