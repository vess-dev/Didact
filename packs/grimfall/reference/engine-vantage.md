Your frame of reference: who you are, what's on your sheet, and the scene around you. It decides what's plausible — and you're assumed to carry the tools of your trade, so nobody tracks lockpicks.

- **Certain** — stated on your sheet or obvious from the fiction. No roll; it just works. *A sawbones suturing in a calm clinic.*
- **Reasonable** — fits you and the moment. Roll it, 0–1 thorns. *A prospector jimmying a pre-war lock.*
- **Doubtful** — a stretch. Roll with 2+ thorns or higher stakes. *A vault kid staring down a raider boss.*
- **Impossible** — beyond you, no roll. Propose something smaller. *Outrunning a Vertibird on foot.*

**Vigilance.** Nobody rolls to notice. When a hidden danger is in play, the GM spotlights the survivor it matters to and, by their vantage, gives a **reveal** (they see it plainly), a **hint** (one shot at intuition), or a **strike** (it lands — the GM spends suspense, and you get a defense roll). More in Chapter 9.

**Potency** stretches vantage: a perk that grants a **potent feat** lets you attempt what's normally beyond human limits — group-effort results, single-handed wonders, one-in-a-million shots — and moves an action one or more steps toward certain. At its simplest, a potent action is a normal action with a critical bonus built in.
