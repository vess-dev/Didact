Enough structure to run a night on an index card:

- **Fiction pillars.** Before a session, sketch up to seven elements that might come up — a face, a place, a relic, an event — each with three sensory details, its drive (what it wants or does), and one way to embody it at the table: a voice, a gesture, a sound. Hang the night's improv on them, and keep the unused ones for later. Above them, set two or three load-bearing truths for the region and never contradict them: *the water is owned — the Institute replaces people — the bombs out east still work.*
- **Weather & drift.** Track drifting conditions (weather, rad-storms, the mood of an occupying army) as a wandering step: each downtime or dawn, roll a d6 — 1–2, a step toward calm; 3–4, hold; 5–6, a step toward the green — along calm ↔ wind ↔ dust ↔ rad-storm ↔ the sky goes green. It wanders back the way weather does, and everyone plans around it.
- **Story kits.** Prep an adventure as parts, not plot: a **hook** heard three ways, a **pressure pool** or two to keep it moving, four or five **useful pieces** (a site map, two faces with wants, a challenge, a relic), a **fail state**, and the question the night is actually about. Let the party pick the order; the kit doesn't care. *(For the pattern worked, see The Door Under Junction, below.)*
- **Quests.** Frame goals at the weight they deserve: an errand is a montage, a job is a session with a task pool, and a *quest* — cure the plague, take the dam — is a chain of linked task pools (4d + 4d for a moderate one, 4d + 4d + 6d for a daunting one, 4d + 4d + 6d + 6d for an epic one, 4d + 4d + 6d + 6d + 8d for a legendary one) racing linked **peril** pools — one fewer than the quest has, sized like its first links — that the whole table can see. Reward tiers follow Chapter 6's hauls.
