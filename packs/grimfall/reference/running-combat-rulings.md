- **Enemies don't roll.** Their danger reaches the table as thorns on the players' rolls, the stakes, your suspense, and the size of their pools. A deathclaw is scary because its pool is big and its moves land hard — not because it has a stat.
- **Defense stats follow the fiction:** dodge the swing with Agility, absorb the tackle with Brawn, see the ambush coming with Wits, keep your nerve under the commissar's glare with Presence. Players can pitch; the GM picks.
- **Shooting, or working delicate tech, with threats up close:** focus under threat costs — thorns, or impossible until the situation changes. Charging in with a ranged weapon is reckless: +1t, or a separate action to close the gap.
- **Hitting several foes at once** takes the vantage for it — a sweeping weapon, a burst on full-auto, real skill — and carries thorns or higher stakes. Tier matters: one clean roll clears mooks, not toughs.
- **Called shots are just goals.** Name the part instead of the target — the gun hand, the knee, the fusion core, the optics — and say what you want from it: *cripple, disarm, blind, breach.* Price it like anything else, usually **+1t** for the smaller target. On a success, that's what the hit does: a move comes off the enemy's card, a thorn comes off your friends' rolls, a vulnerability opens.
- **Armor is vantage,** not math: metal plate turns knives, and buys thorns against you when you swim. Grit, Plating, and the heavy suits' Armor pools in Chapter 8 are the crunchy exceptions; the optional armor rule for everyone lives in Chapter 10.
- **Vulnerabilities are setups waiting:** exploiting one drops 1d before a pool rolls, lowers thorns, or is the only way in at all (the hologram's emitter, the behemoth's head).
- **Enemy tech doesn't follow player rules.** A courser's relay, a Think Tank forcefield — they're GM moves, bounded by fiction, not by anyone's schematic list.
