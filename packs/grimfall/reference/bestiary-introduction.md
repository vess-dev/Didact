Wasteland threats have no hit points — just **traits**, **moves**, and a **tier** that sets their presence. **Mooks** are fodder: one good roll clears several, and a pack is a single task pool, usually **4d**. **Toughs** fall one at a time; a knot of them is a pool too, usually **6d**. **Elites** are a challenge unto themselves, usually **6d**. **Bosses** own the scene at **8d** — or as a **linked challenge** whose parts each carry their own pool, traits, and moves, with a **fail state** that ends the fight the bad way. Elites and bosses framed as challenges bring **2 bonus suspense** of their own (Chapter 9); a pool of mooks or toughs is just a pool.

**Enemies never roll.** When a card makes something dangerous, it shows up as thorns on your rolls, the stakes, the GM's suspense, and the size and stubbornness of a pool. Variant strains and ranks ride in each entry's **Note** — re-skin, re-tier, and re-arm to taste. (Perks and arcana call mooks **lesser** foes, and elites and bosses **greater** ones.)

Each card's **role** says how the threat fights, and what makes it falter:

| Role | Fights by | Falters when |
|---|---|---|
| **Blaster** | raining power from range | the fight gets close |
| **Brute** | sudden, overwhelming force | it's baited or taunted |
| **Lurker** | striking from concealment | it's exposed, or the fight drags on |
| **Marksman** | picking targets off at a distance | caught in melee or forced to move |
| **Predator** | running its prey down | the prey won't break |
| **Protector** | shielding allies and soaking blows | cut off from those it guards |
| **Skirmisher** | hit-and-run from odd angles | pinned down |
| **Swarmer** | numbers, from every side | its numbers thin |
| **Tactician** | preparation and command | the plan fails |
| **Trickster** | confusion and deceit | its tricks are exposed |
