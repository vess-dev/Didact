*the house always wins — but you're not the house*

**ALSO:** Gambler, Drifter, Fortune's Fool, Wildcard

You don't aim, plan, or train like the others. You just... come out ahead. The round that finds the one gap in the armor. The door that's already unlocked. The stranger who shows up shooting at exactly the right moment. Call it karma, call it a glitch in fate. You call it a pretty good day.

> **Core Perk — LUCK OF THE DRAW**
> *Growth: At levels 3 and 6, gain +1 Luck die per session.*
>
> Fate owes you, and it pays in advance. Before each session, roll **3d** and write down the results: that's your **Luck**. During the session, you can **replace any d6 just rolled** — yours, an ally's, a story die, one the GM rolls for a pool — with one of your Luck results, then cross it off. A 6 saves a roll, or makes a critical. A 5 holds the last stimpak in the pool. A 2 puts one more raider on the floor. *(Your Luck is whatever you rolled — fate giveth.)*
>
> *Charmed life:* Take **+1d** on story rolls that test your own luck — what's in the locker, whether the floor holds, who comes over the ridge, whether you wake up after being dropped.
> *The wheel turns:* When you roll a disaster, you take spark as usual — and roll a fresh Luck die too, if you're short of your full count.

> **[Optional] WILD LUCK CRUCIBLE.** *When your luck spikes strange (a critical or disaster on a Luck-flavored roll), roll a die and read it loose:* a bystander intervenes — gear misbehaves (yours or theirs) — the environment shifts (a collapse, a flare, a gust) — an old debt comes due — the wrong/right person shows up — something you forgot you had matters now.
> **LUCKY CHARM.** *Name the thing fate seems to hang on:* a two-headed coin — a rabbit's foot — a deck of cards — a dog tag that isn't yours — a bottlecap from the day it all started — nothing at all, and that scares people.
