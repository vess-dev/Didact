Travel is where the wasteland does its quiet work. Cross dead country with a **montage roll**; weather, hunger, and terrain are thorns or marks, and the Everlast's **Lead Belly** and **Pack Rat** shrug off most of it.

**Making the map.** When the GM calls to **expand the map** — at the start, at the edge of the known, or whenever the story opens new ground — everyone at the table, GM included, takes **3 exploration tokens** and spends them going around: **settlements**, **sites**, **dangers**, **curiosities**, **areas**, and **barriers**, joined by **paths** — major roads, minor trails, rough scrambles — with a **waypoint** for each day of hard travel. Everything gets a two-word label (*Glowing Orchard, Drowned Underpass, Jackal Country*). Survivors also earn 1–3 tokens for a real discovery, and can spend thread as a token when a wise or story arc explains how they knew. Settlement maps work the same at street scale; vaults and ruins become 5-point site maps (Chapter 9). The GM draws the first road in; nobody knows the whole wasteland, and the map grows the way rumor does.
