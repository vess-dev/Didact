Caps are **vantage and set dressing**, not bookkeeping — a Charmer always has walking-around money; a vault dweller might not know what anything's worth. Serious wealth moves in **hauls**, in three sizes:

- **Minor haul:** a working pistol and ammo, a sack of caps, a crate of clean cans, a brahmin, a minor arcana.
- **Major haul:** a suit of combat armor, a caravan's cargo, a safehouse, laser weapons that hold a charge, a major arcana.
- **Mythic haul:** a functioning reactor, a water chip, a Vertibird, the deed to a town.

Hauls belong to the party unless you decide otherwise, and when a survivor needs one of them, it happens to be on hand — unless the GM complicates things.

Every survivor also carries a personal *4d Caps* power pool. Walking-around money is set dressing: a drink, a bed, a box of shells. Roll the pool when you spend big (bribes, gear, a doctor's miracle), and drop 1d first for the huge asks; an impact move can mean a hitch — a flaw, a cheat, a debt — even when the deal goes through. At 0d, you're broke. Prices quoted in caps are flavor: a few hundred caps is a minor haul, a few thousand a major one. Selling off a minor haul in a settlement adds +2d (a major one +4d; max 8d) and earns you a second, different downtime action (see Downtime, below). For anything truly priceless, the price is a story — a favor, a job, a debt.

**Scavenging.** Picking over the old world is set dressing until it matters. When it does, make a story roll for the place — 1d if it's been picked clean, 2d for an ordinary ruin, 3d for somewhere sealed since the bombs. **Perfect:** a minor haul, or a stash that refills one pool (Stimpaks, a Chem Stash, ordinary ammunition). **Messy:** a find with a catch — it's trapped, it's claimed, it needs a part. **Grim:** nothing, and now something knows you're here. A thorough search takes time; if anything is closing in, roll its pool.
