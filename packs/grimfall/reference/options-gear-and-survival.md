- **Weapon styles.** Give each signature weapon two descriptors (*heavy, sweeping · quick, concealable · scoped, temperamental*). They're vantage, not stats — describe using them and the thorns follow.
- **Armor.** For crunchier plate: light armor gives a *1d Armor* pool each session, heavy armor *2d* (the Armory's heavy suits already have theirs). After a physical consequence lands, roll it to lessen the consequence, as a messy defense would. Light armor carries the *Lightly Armored* condition (it hinders movement); heavy carries *Heavily Armored* (loud, slow, and no swimming). Grit and Plating still *avoid* harm outright; Armor only lessens it.
- **Supplies.** For survival-forward games, each survivor has a *4d Supplies* resource pool — ammunition, rations, the little things. Roll it after heavy use, and as time passes away from settlements; at 0d, hunger and thirst start writing the thorns.
- **Encumbrance.** Normally common sense. If you want teeth: each survivor carries **2 + Brawn** slots of gear beyond armor and main weapons. Most gear takes a slot, small items half, and large or bulky ones two or more. *Strong Back* and *Pack Rat* laugh at this rule.
- **Caps** *(on by default)*. Every survivor carries a personal *4d Caps* power pool (Chapter 6). Turn it off and money is pure vantage: walking-around cash is set dressing, and serious wealth moves only in hauls.
- **Stimpaks** *(on by default)*. A *3d Stimpaks* pool per survivor clears one harm with a breather, on yourself or an ally (Chapter 6). *Grimmer:* *2d*, only yourself, or a single *3d* pool the whole party shares — pick one. *Softer:* *4d*, refilled by any real rest, and a jab into a dropped ally adds +1d to their story roll.
