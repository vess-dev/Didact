Diminishing pools track anything that runs out: progress, threats, resources, time. Roll the pool; dice landing 1–3 drop out. At **0d** the thing happens or runs dry. Pools usually start at 2d (short), 4d (medium), or 8d (long) — 6d sits between, for stubborn problems — and the pools a perk gives you run 2d–4d. Pools are written size-first, in italics: *3d Stimpaks*.


- **Task pools** — effort to overcome: *6d Crack the Vault Door*, *4d Raider Pack*.
- **Pressure pools** — trouble closing in: *6d The Horde Notices You*.
- **Timer pools** — events unfolding: *8d Sunrise*, *4d The Reactor Melts Down*.
- **Resource pools** — stock running out: *4d Clean Water*, *3d Stimpaks*. Use it, then roll it: the use always works, and the roll only shows how much is left. At 0d it's gone. The GM can also roll one as an impact move when losing some makes sense.
- **Power pools** — rolled in place of a stat: take the highest die as your result, then drop the 1–3s. Spark or assist dice rolled alongside stay separate — they never drop and never join the pool. If a use isn't risky, it simply succeeds — but you still roll the pool and drop the 1–3s. *4d Alien Blaster Cells.*
- **Bonus-dice pools** — a buff you draw on: roll the pool *alongside* your own dice and count its best die with yours, then drop its 1–3s. *3d Covering Fire.*
- **Linked pools** — chained phases: *6d Slashing Claws* + *8d The Deathclaw*. Beat the first to reach the second.
- **Campaign pools** — long-fuse events rolled between sessions and in downtime, faction pools among them: *8d The Enclave Returns*.

**Push or pivot.** If a task pool roll drops nothing, you can **push yourself** (mark a stat) to drop 1d anyway, or **pivot** to take a secondary effect instead. When any *other* pool refuses to budge, the GM can spend suspense to drop 1d.
